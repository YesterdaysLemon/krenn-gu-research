# Transverse five-root boundary points are internal blocker flags

## Status

This is an exact local classification over `C`.  It sharpens the
coordinate-boundary alternative in
[`FIVE_ROOT_ZERO_COUPLING_INTERSECTION_LEMMA.md`](FIVE_ROOT_ZERO_COUPLING_INTERSECTION_LEMMA.md):
at every transverse common zero, the zero coordinates of a root vector are
exactly its internal algebraic blocker flags relative to the other four
roots.

It does **not** show that a transverse point exists, that a boundary point
has only one zero coordinate, or that the five-root intersection meets the
coordinate torus.  If the common-zero scheme is not finite and reduced, the
nontransverse branch remains open.

## Setup

Let `V=C^3`, and for `0 <= i < j <= 4` let

```text
B_ij: V x V -> C
```

be bilinear.  Put `X=(P(V))^5`, and let `Z` be the common zero scheme of
the ten forms.  Fix a geometric point

```text
x=([x_0],...,[x_4]) in Z.
```

For each vertex `i`, collect the four covectors obtained from the internal
edges:

```text
alpha_(j,i)(z) = B_ji(x_j,z)       if j<i,
alpha_(j,i)(z) = B_ij(z,x_j)       if i<j,

A_i^int = span{alpha_(j,i):j!=i} subset V*.           (1)
```

Every `alpha_(j,i)` annihilates `x_i`, because `x` is a common zero.
Hence

```text
A_i^int subset x_i^perp.                              (2)
```

Viewed relative to the four-root set

```text
R_i={0,1,2,3,4} minus {i},
```

the space in (1) is exactly the root-covector span at the outside vertex
`i` used in the multi-star blocker definition.  The algebraic flag itself
makes sense without a support assumption; applying the multi-star lower
bound additionally requires the four roots to be supported in the colour
under consideration.

## Theorem

If the ten divisors meet transversely at `x`, then for every `i`

```text
A_i^int=x_i^perp.                                      (3)
```

Consequently, for every colour `c`,

```text
i has the colour-c internal blocker flag for R_i
  <=> e_c^* belongs to A_i^int
  <=> x_i[c]=0.                                        (4)
```

Thus the internal blocker-flag type of `i` relative to the other four roots
is exactly the zero-coordinate set of `x_i`.

## Proof

The tangent space of the `i`th projective factor is

```text
T_[x_i] P(V) = Hom(<x_i>,V/<x_i>) ~= V/<x_i>.          (5)
```

Because every form vanishes at `x`, its differential is well defined on
the projective quotients.  On an edge `ij` it is

```text
d B_ij(delta_i,delta_j)
 = B_ij(delta_i,x_j)+B_ij(x_i,delta_j).                (6)
```

Suppose `dim A_i^int<2`.  The quotient in (5) has dimension two, so there
is a nonzero tangent vector `delta_i mod <x_i>` annihilated by all four
covectors in (1).  Set every other tangent component to zero.  Formula
(6) then vanishes on every edge: the four incident rows vanish by the
choice of `delta_i`, and all other rows see no perturbation.  This gives a
nonzero vector in the kernel of the full `10 x 10` Jacobian, contradicting
transversality.

Therefore `dim A_i^int=2`.  Inclusion (2) is an equality because
`x_i^perp` also has dimension two, proving (3).  Finally,

```text
e_c^* in x_i^perp <=> e_c^*(x_i)=x_i[c]=0,
```

which proves (4).

## Scheme-theoretic boundary dichotomy

If `Z` is finite and reduced, then every point is transverse.  Indeed, the
ten equations generate an ideal of height ten in the regular local ring
of the smooth tenfold `X`; at a reduced point their differentials span the
cotangent space.  (In this branch no form can be identically zero, since
an ideal generated by only nine nonvacuous equations in a tenfold cannot
have zero-dimensional support.)  The intersection class from the
five-root lemma has degree 24, so in this case `Z` consists of exactly 24
distinct points.

Therefore, if the entire five-root intersection lies on the coordinate
boundary, exactly one of the following holds:

1. `Z` is positive-dimensional or nonreduced/nontransverse; or
2. `Z` consists of 24 transverse boundary points, and at every point each
   zero coordinate is exactly an internal four-root blocker flag as in
   (4).

This is a classification of the unresolved boundary, not its exclusion.

## Order-ten one-zero consequence

Assume now that the bilinear forms are internal blocks of a hypothetical
ten-vertex three-colour GHZ witness.  Suppose a transverse five-root point
has exactly one zero coordinate, say

```text
x_i[c]=0,
```

and all other fourteen root coordinates are nonzero.  Then the other four
roots `R_i` are fully supported and pairwise zero-coupled, while `i` is
exactly a colour-`c` blocker for them and blocks neither other colour.

The total blocker union for `R_i` among its six outside vertices cannot
have size four, by the exact subrank-two theorem for `P_4`.  If it has size
five, the unique-nonblocker extraction theorem in
[`ONE_NONBLOCKER_SURPLUS_PERMANENT_EXTRACTION.md`](ONE_NONBLOCKER_SURPLUS_PERMANENT_EXTRACTION.md)
produces a restriction

```text
P_5 -> Delta_3.                                        (7)
```

Hence the exact order-ten alternative at such a point is

```text
P_5 -> Delta_3,
or every one of the six outside vertices belongs to the
four-root total blocker union.                          (8)
```

Conditionally on excluding `P_5 -> Delta_3`, (8) forces the six-blocker
case.  It does not contradict that case.
