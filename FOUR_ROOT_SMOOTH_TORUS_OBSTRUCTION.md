# Smooth four-root intersections meet the coordinate torus

## Status

This is an exact characteristic-zero intersection theorem.  Let six
bilinear forms on four copies of `P^2` cut out a smooth surface of the
expected dimension two.  Then their common zero locus contains a point at
which all twelve homogeneous coordinates are nonzero.

Consequently, in a hypothetical eight-vertex three-colour Krenn--Gu
witness, the six internal blocks on **every** four-vertex set must have a
singular or excess-dimensional common-zero scheme.  This is a simultaneous
discriminant reduction, not an exclusion of that locus and not a proof of
the global conjecture.

## Theorem

Put

```text
X=(P^2)^4
```

and write `h_i` for the hyperplane class on factor `i`.  For each edge
`0<=i<j<=3`, let

```text
B_ij : C^3 x C^3 -> C
```

be a bilinear form, viewed as a section of `O_X(h_i+h_j)`.  Let `Z` be the
scheme-theoretic common zero of the six sections.  If `Z` is smooth of dimension two,
then

```text
Z intersects (C^*)^2 x (C^*)^2 x (C^*)^2 x (C^*)^2.       (1)
```

Here each `(C^*)^2` is the dense coordinate torus in its projective plane.

## The complete intersection is connected

Smoothness of dimension two makes `Z` a pure codimension-six subscheme of
the smooth eightfold `X`.  Locally, its ideal is generated by the six given
equations and has height six in a regular, hence Cohen--Macaulay, local ring.
The six equations therefore form a regular sequence.  Their Koszul complex
resolves `O_Z`.
Place its exterior-degree-`p` term in cohomological degree `-p`; thus the
hypercohomology spectral sequence is

```text
E_1^(-p,q)=H^q(X,wedge^p E^*)  =>  H^(q-p)(Z,O_Z),
E=direct_sum_(i<j) O_X(h_i+h_j).                       (2)
```

For an edge subset `S subset E(K_4)`, its Koszul summand is

```text
O_X(-d_0(S),-d_1(S),-d_2(S),-d_3(S)),                (3)
```

where `d_i(S)` is the degree of vertex `i` in `S`.

The only nonzero cohomology groups among the line bundles that can occur on
one `P^2` factor are

```text
H^0(P^2,O)=C,
H^2(P^2,O(-3))=C.                                    (4)
```

Indeed, a degree in (3) is one of `0,1,2,3`, and `O(-1),O(-2)` are
acyclic.  By Kunneth, a Koszul summand can therefore have nonzero
cohomology only when every graph degree is zero or three.  A nonempty edge
subset of `K_4` with that property is the whole `K_4`: once one vertex has
degree three, its three neighbours cannot have degree one or two, so they
also have degree three.

Thus the hypercohomology page of the Koszul complex has only

```text
S=empty:       H^0(O_X)=C,       Koszul degree 0,
S=E(K_4):      H^8(O_X(-3,-3,-3,-3))=C,
                                    Koszul degree 6.  (5)
```

There are no other entries from which a differential could alter total
degree zero.  The second displayed entry contributes in total cohomological
degree `8-6=2`, not degree zero.  Hence

```text
H^0(Z,O_Z)=C.                                         (6)
```

Thus `Z` has no nontrivial idempotent and is connected.  Since `Z` is smooth
over `C`, all of its local rings are regular domains.  Distinct irreducible
components therefore cannot meet; equivalently, the irreducible components
of a smooth scheme are open and closed.  Connectedness now forces a single
component, so `Z` is irreducible.

## Its Chow class cannot lie on one coordinate boundary

The coordinate boundary of `X` is the union of the twelve irreducible
divisors

```text
H_(i,c)={x_i[c]=0}.                                   (7)
```

If (1) failed, irreducibility would put `Z` inside one fixed `H_(i,c)`.
For the inclusion `j:H_(i,c)->X`, restriction `A^*(X)->A^*(H_(i,c))` is
surjective, because `H_(i,c)` is a linear `P^1` in the `i`th `P^2` factor.
The projection formula then gives

```text
j_* j^*(alpha)=h_i alpha.
```

Consequently the pushforward to `X` of every cycle supported on that divisor
lies in the ideal `(h_i)`.

On the other hand, regular intersection gives

```text
[Z]=product_(0<=i<j<=3)(h_i+h_j)                     (8)
```

in

```text
A^*(X)=Z[h_0,h_1,h_2,h_3]/(h_0^3,h_1^3,h_2^3,h_3^3).
```

Fix `i=0`.  In (8), choose the endpoint away from zero on the three edges
`01,02,03`.  On the remaining triangle, the two cyclic endpoint
orientations give

```text
2 h_1^2 h_2^2 h_3^2.                                 (9)
```

The residue classes of the monomials with every exponent at most two form a
`Z`-basis of the Chow ring.  Thus (9) is nonzero and is the complete part of
(8) outside the ideal `(h_0)`.  In particular `[Z]` does not lie in `(h_0)`.
The same argument applies to every factor, contradicting support on any
divisor in (7).  This proves
(1).

## Eight-vertex consequence

Assume an eight-vertex matching tensor equals `Delta_3`, and fix any four
vertices `R`.  If their six internal bilinear forms had a smooth
two-dimensional common-zero scheme, the theorem would give fully supported
vectors `x_i`, `i in R`, satisfying

```text
B_ij(x_i,x_j)=0             for all i!=j in R.        (10)
```

For each target colour, the multi-star blocker theorem then requires at
least four outside blockers.  There are exactly four outside vertices, so
all four are common blockers for all three colours.  Write that outside set
as `U`.  In the notation of
[`MULTI_STAR_BLOCKER_FACTORISATION_LEMMA.md`](MULTI_STAR_BLOCKER_FACTORISATION_LEMMA.md),
the tight factorisation has no residual modes, so `H_empty=R_d=1` and its
equation (10) reads

```text
F_U=sum_(d=0)^2 X_d D_d,
X_d=product_(i in R) x_i[d],
D_d=product_(u in U) z_u[d].                          (11)
```

Here every `X_d` is nonzero by full support.  Moreover `F_U` is the
four-by-four permanent obtained from `P_4` by the four local linear maps

```text
z_u |-> (B_iu(x_i,z_u))_(i in R).
```

Rescaling the three coordinates in one outside mode normalizes the three
nonzero coefficients `X_d`.  Hence (11) produces a local restriction

```text
P_4 -> Delta_3.                                      (12)
```

But the order-four permanent tensor has exact subrank two over `C`, by
[`FOURTH_ORDER_PERMANENT_SUBRANK_OBSTRUCTION.md`](FOURTH_ORDER_PERMANENT_SUBRANK_OBSTRUCTION.md).
Therefore (12) is impossible.

It follows that, in any hypothetical eight-vertex witness, every one of the
`binomial(8,4)=70` four-vertex systems is in the algebraic locus where the
six equations either fail to have codimension six or their expected
surface is singular.  The theorem does not yet prove that these 70
overlapping degeneracies are mutually incompatible.
