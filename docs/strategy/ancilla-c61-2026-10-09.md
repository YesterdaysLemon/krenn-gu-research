### 2.1 What an ancilla is

**Krenn–Gu–Soltész 2019** (`krenn-gu-soltesz-2019-perfect-matchings`;
Definition 2.4, Question 2, Figure 3, and the physics glossary in Section 3).

- A colouring is **k-monochromatic** if the first `k` vertices share one
  colour and every other vertex is coloured red.
- The red vertices are the trigger (heralding) photons.
- Question 2 asks for bi-coloured weighted graphs in which the `d`
  k-monochromatic colourings have unit weight and every other colouring
  cancels.
- Nothing restricts edges among trigger vertices.
- A trigger vertex is therefore a one-colour vertex in the sense of C_{6,1},
  once its non-red edges are removed (they contribute only to colourings
  that must cancel or are post-selected away).

**Gu–Erhard–Zeilinger–Krenn 2019, "Graphs II"** (PNAS; Figure 7 A–F, the
paragraph "Restriction for GHZ state generation", footnote 10, and SI
Figure 17).  The trigger vertices are relabelled `T` (one fixed mode).  A
six-photon, two-trigger setup aimed at GHZ(4,3) yields a fourth "Maverick"
term.

**Gu–Chen–Zeilinger–Krenn 2019, "Graphs III"** (Phys. Rev. A; the Figure 7
caption and Appendix C).  It treats SRV states with one trigger `t` that
keeps a fixed mode.  Its no-go statements explicitly exclude additional
ancillary photons.

### 2.2 The two-ancilla GHZ(6,3) construction (PyTheus, Fig. 3)  [EXACT check]

**Ruiz-Gonzalez et al. 2023** (`ruiz-gonzalez-2023-pytheus`; Section 2.1.1
"Ancillary Particles" and Figure 3).  The caption describes a graph for
GHZ(6,3) with two ancillary particles, vertices 6 and 7.  Both ancillas are
in the same mode.

The edges, as read from the rendered figure (colour 0 = blue, 1 = red,
2 = green; a diamond means a negative weight; all magnitudes 1 except the
ancilla edge `κ`):

```text
B-edges:  21 gg(-)  20 bb(-)  10 rr  14 bb  34 gg(-)  35 bb(-)  45 rr
ancilla:  36 r.  37 r.  26 r.  27 r.  06 g.  07 g(-)  57 g.  56 g(-)     (colour at the B end)
          67  κ
```

The exact tensor (script `pytheus_exact.py`) is

```text
T = κ|0^6⟩ + 2|1^6⟩ + 2|2^6⟩      (13 supported perfect matchings);
with the 67 edge deleted:  T = 2|1^6⟩ + 2|2^6⟩.
```

So for every `κ != 0` this is an exact C'_{6,1} element (normalize by the
torus), and it is not a C_{6,1} element.

- Colour 0 is produced only by the matching `67 | 20 | 14 | 35`.
- Colours 1 and 2 are produced only by matchings that send both ancillas
  into `B`.

**This is an apparent exact witness of an ancilla-assisted class.**  It is
reported here as an escalation item.  It is not a Krenn–Gu witness: the
state has two vertices fixed to one mode.  It is not a C_{6,1} witness either.
Its consequence for the route is in Section 3.2.

### 2.3 The four-ancilla GHZ(6,3) construction (Erhard graph)  [EXACT check]

Krenn–Gu–Soltész, Figure 3, describes the "Erhard graph".  It has 10
vertices; vertices 7–10 are triggers whose only half-edges are red.  The
figure attributes the graph to an experimental technique of Manuel Erhard.

The 20 edges and weights, as read from the figure and its weight table:

```text
weight 1:     23 bb, 27 gr, 45 bb, 46 gg, 4-10 rr, 56 rr, 89 rr
weight -1:    38 rr, 58 gr, 67 br
weight -1/2:  1-10 br
weight i:     28 gr, 3-10 rr, 57 gr, 68 br, 79 rr, 9-10 rr
weight -i:    37 rr
weight i/2:   12 rr, 13 gg
```

Here `xy` gives the colours at the lower and the higher endpoint.  The exact
tensor over `Q(i)` (script `erhard_exact.py`) is

```text
T = |0^6 0000⟩ + |1^6 0000⟩ + |2^6 0000⟩      (20 perfect matchings),
```

exactly as the figure's table states.

- The trigger graph is the star `{79, 89, 9-10}`; vertex 9 is adjacent
  only to triggers.
- Iterated Lemma-5 contraction produces four ancillas in two
  **non-adjacent pairs** (`C_{6,2}`).  The star has no perfect matching in
  its complement, so this graph is not of that shape.
- Expanding at vertex 9 writes its tensor as
  `Σ M_ij Φ(r_i, r_j)` over the trigger rows `r_7, r_8, r_10`.  These rows
  are linearly independent, and `M` is the symmetric matrix with
  off-diagonal entries `(i, 1, i)` and determinant `-2`.
  - Since `M` has rank 3, this expansion does not collapse to a single
    `Φ(ρ, σ)`, which has rank at most 2.
  - It does not show that no C_{6,1} configuration with the same tensor
    exists.

**Escalation item, as above.**  It is an exact ancilla-assisted GHZ(6,3), not a
Krenn–Gu witness, and not a C_{6,1} or C_{6,2} witness.

### 2.4 Impossibility statements

- **Graphs II** says such six-vertex graphs cannot produce high-dimensional
  GHZ states, citing the 2017 PRL.
  - The surrounding text and the KGS glossary attribute the GHZ
    restriction to Bogdanov's lemma under positive (real, non-interfering)
    weights.  KGS calls it "Bogdanov's Lemma"; its original source is not

  - This is not an impossibility for complex weights.  Its own example uses

- **KGS** describes the Erhard graph as the only example then known for

## 3. The exact equations  [EXACT]

### 3.1 Form

For a word `γ` on `B`, write `ρ_γ = (r_z[γ_z])_z` and
`σ_γ = (s_z[γ_z])_z`.  Let `Θ_γ[z, z'] = T_{B - {z, z'}}(γ|)` for
`z != z'`, with `Θ_γ[z, z] = 0`.  Then

```text
T(γ) = κ T_B(γ) + Φ(γ),        Φ(γ) = ρ_γ^T Θ_γ σ_γ = Σ_{z != z'} r_z[γ_z] s_z'[γ_z'] T_{B-z-z'}(γ|).      (1)
```

Proof: Laplace expansion at `a`, then at `b`.  `Φ` is symmetric bilinear in
the row pair `(r, s)`, and its coefficients are the order-4 sub-coefficients
of `B`.

- **C_{6,1}**: `Φ = GHZ6` up to the torus, with `κ = 0`.
- **C'_{6,1}**: `κ T_B + Φ = GHZ6`.
- **Embedding.**  Make `a` and `b` three-colour vertices whose rows at
  colours 1 and 2 are zero, and set `W_ab = 0`.  The result is an ordinary
  eight-vertex configuration with tensor `GHZ6 ⊗ e_0 ⊗ e_0`.
  - Every clause family proved for the zero pattern of every complex
    configuration applies verbatim to this embedding.
  - Local `GL_3` changes at `a` and `b` show that C_{6,1} is equivalent to
    the existence of an eight-vertex configuration with `W_ab = 0` and tensor
    `GHZ6 ⊗ p ⊗ q`, for any fixed nonzero `p` and `q`.

### 3.2 Generalized Lemma 5 and why only κ = 0 matters

For any `ℓ, m in C^3` and any configuration `W` on `V = B ∪ {u, v}`,
contract `u` with `ℓ` and `v` with `m`.  This turns them into one-colour
vertices with rows `ℓ^T W_uz` and `m^T W_vz` and edge `κ = ℓ^T W_uv m`.
Then

```text
Σ_{x,y} ℓ_x m_y T_V(x, y, γ) = κ T_B(γ) + Φ(γ).
```

This is (1) of the compressed-Hessian note, contracted.  It was replayed
exactly on 160 words of four random integer configurations
(`lemma5_replay.py`).

So every order-8 witness gives C'_{6,1} elements for every full-support
`(ℓ, m)`, with no residual.  PyTheus Fig. 3 shows C'_{6,1} is nonempty
[EXACT], so **the edge-allowed contraction excludes nothing.**  Only the
slice `κ = ℓ^T W_uv m = 0` can carry an exclusion.

That slice fails only when every block is a single nonzero entry.  That is
the `N8R1` class (Section 6).

### 3.3 An order-8 witness gives more than one C_{6,1} element

By the equivalence in the compressed-Hessian note (Section 8), a witness
gives a C_{6,1} element for **every** `(ℓ, m)` on the quadric
`ℓ^T W_uv m = 0`.  All of them share the same `B`, and their rows depend
linearly on `ℓ` and `m`.  So C_{6,1} = ∅ is sufficient but not necessary for
the order-8 exclusion.

### 3.4 A small exact no-go for the PyTheus pattern without its edge

PyTheus Fig. 3 attaches the ancillas, for colour `c`, to a pair `P_c` of `B`
and closes the rest by two colour-`c` edges.  Its pairs are
`P_1 = {2, 3}` and `P_2 = {0, 5}`, plus the edge `67` for colour 0.

Try the symmetric completion with no ancilla edge, `P_0 = {1, 4}`:

- B-edges `10 rr, 45 rr, 21 gg, 34 gg, 20 bb, 35 bb`;
- every ancilla attached to both vertices of each `P_c`, in colour `c`;
- all weights symbolic.

The exact symbolic tensor (`design_c61.py`, 18 matchings) is supported on
the nine words `(c c c c' c' c')`.  Its coefficient matrix is

```text
M[c, c'] = P_c Q_c' (α_c β'_c' + α'_c β_c'),
```

with `P, Q, α, α', β, β'` monomials in the weights.  This has rank at most
two, so it is never a nonzero diagonal.  **This support family is excluded
for all complex weights.**  In this family the third colour needs exactly
the ancilla edge.

This is a statement about one support family, not about C_{6,1}.

**(AK) Ancilla killer.  For each ancilla `a` and each colour `c`, some
`z in B` has `r_z` equal to a nonzero multiple of `e_c`.**

*Proof.*

1. For each `z` with `r_z != 0`, let `H_z = ker(r_z^T)`, a plane in `C^3`.
   For the other `z`, let `H_z = C^3`.
2. Since `κ = 0`, every perfect matching pairs `a` with some `z in B`.  So
   `T(γ) = Σ_z r_z[γ_z] T_{V-a-z}(γ|)`, and `T` vanishes on
   `H_1 x ... x H_6`.
3. Hence `GHZ6` vanishes there.  That is `G(y) = Σ_c Π_z y_z[c] = 0`, with
   six factors, each of dimension at least 2.
4. The hyperplane-annihilation theorem then gives: for each `c`, some
   `H_u` lies in `{y[c] = 0}`.  `H_u = C^3` is impossible, and a plane
   `ker(r_u^T)` equals `{y[c] = 0}` iff `r_u` is a nonzero multiple of
   `e_c`.  ∎

No genericity is needed: the ancilla has no colour index to contract.  The
three partners are distinct, so each ancilla has at least three neighbours.

(AK) uses `κ = 0`.  In C'_{6,1} the `ab` channel `κ T_B` survives the
restriction, and PyTheus Fig. 3 violates (AK): ancilla 6 has no
single-entry partner in colour 0.  So (AK) is a genuine `κ = 0` family.  It
separates C_{6,1} from C'_{6,1} at the support level, but in the runs below
it does not exclude C_{6,1}.

**(CK) Conditional coloured killer.  If `z in B` has `r_z = s_z = 0`, then
for each `c` some `u in B` has `W_zu` supported exactly on column `c`.**

*Proof.*  This is the killer theorem's proof verbatim.

1. Contract `z` with a full-support `x`.
2. Restrict each `u in B - z` to `ker(x^T W_zu)`.
3. The `z`–ancilla channels are already zero.
4. The target is `Σ_c x_c Π_{u in B - z} y_u[c]`, which has five factors.
5. A Zariski-open set of `x` gives one fixed partner per colour.  ∎

**Why the unconditional killer fails.**

- If `r_z != 0`, the channel `x^T r_z · T_{V-z-a}` survives the
  restriction.
- To kill it, `x` must lie in `r_z^⊥ ∩ s_z^⊥`.  That is a line, or a plane
  when `r_z` and `s_z` are proportional.
- The killer argument needs `x` to range over an open set of full-support
  vectors.  In the plane case it does, and gives only the weaker conclusion
  that, for each `c`, some `u` has `W_zu[:, j] ∈ span(r_z)` for every
  `j != c`.  This is not encoded.
