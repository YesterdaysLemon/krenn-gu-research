## Hostile checks

### Is the common kernel really common?

Yes.  The three coordinates of `beta` are contractions of the three fixed
root--root blocks.  They do not depend on the nonroot or its colour.  Direct
contraction of every singleton formula gives `L_u(beta)=0` for each
`u=x,y,r`.  The primary replay proves the polynomial identity with
algebraically independent entries; the audit checks it from separately coded
exact data.

### Is a quotient being confused with a subspace restriction?

No.  The maps `L_u` factor through `C^3/<beta>`.  Dualizing identifies the
quotient tensor with restriction of the trilinear form to `beta^perp`.  The
proof uses the latter only to calculate coordinates.  Both scripts also
compare a direct three-map contraction against the quotient route in all 27
output coordinates.

### Does support alone really determine the binary orbit?

Yes over `C`.  On the chart `beta=(1,A,B)`, the quotient has exactly the six
coefficients displayed in the theorem and hyperdeterminant
`-48 A^2 B^2`.  Support one gives zero.  Support two gives the concise W
tensor, whose rank is three.  Support three gives nonzero hyperdeterminant and
therefore the open binary GHZ orbit of rank two.  Coordinate permutations
cover the other charts.

### Is hyperdeterminant zero being used as a rank theorem without concision?

No.  In the support-two case all three flattenings have rank two.  A
hypothetical two-term decomposition would therefore have independent factor
pairs in all modes and be locally equivalent to `Delta_2`, contradicting
zero hyperdeterminant.  The displayed three terms give the upper bound.

### Does the result accidentally exclude the genuine binary boundary?

No.  It says the opposite: when exactly two target colours survive and
`beta!=0`, the only possibility is the full-support `beta` orbit, for which
the common quotient is genuinely `Delta_2`.  The theorem records this as an
open physical compatibility problem.

### Is the rank-one cross-matrix conclusion stronger than tensor uniqueness permits?

No.  A concise binary rank-two tensor has an identifiable two-term product
decomposition.  Pulling the two target diagonal terms back through three
invertible quotient maps fixes the same two input lines at every nonroot,
up to one global exchange.  The colour-`c` source covectors are consequently
scalar multiples of one fixed vector, making the root-by-nonroot matrix an
outer product.  Its permanent is the nonzero pure target coefficient, so no
factor or matrix entry can vanish.  Both replays check the diagonalizing
lines over the exact quadratic extension generated by a primitive cube root.

### What is not covered?

- `beta=0`, where the singleton equations no longer force a quotient;
- rank-one contracted cross maps in the one-colour and zero-colour branches;
- existence or nonexistence of a full target-consistent sensor on any branch;
- all `m>=4` Euler--hafnian recurrence layers;
- the all-balanced rank-drop branch.

## Independence of the audit

The primary verifier uses SymPy and symbolic polynomial expansion.  The
audit imports neither SymPy nor repository code.  It uses `Fraction`, a
separate direct permutation evaluator for `P_3`, its own row reduction and
Cayley invariant, deterministic physical block data for the singleton
factorization, a separate direct-versus-quotient contraction test, and a
two-coordinate implementation of `Q[omega]/(omega^2+omega+1)` for the
diagonal-line calculation.

The audit is independent at the implementation and representation levels.
The written support exhaustion and binary matrix-pencil argument remain the
mathematical proof; neither script is presented as a global proof.
