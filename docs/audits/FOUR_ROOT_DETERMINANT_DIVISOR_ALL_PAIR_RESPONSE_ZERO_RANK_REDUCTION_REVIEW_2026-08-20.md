## Independent rederivation of the determinant-divisor reduction

### Quotient cross and maximum-root steps

Let \(L=L(H)\) and \(M=R(H)\).  Quotienting the six response identities in
both residual slots gives

~~~text
bar A_u boxtimes bar C_v+bar A_v boxtimes bar C_u=0.
~~~

If both quotient families are nonzero, flattening one summand against the
realigned other summand forces every active quotient block to have rank one,
the active supports to agree, their residual factor lines to agree, and their
local factor line at each labelled port to agree.  The scalar equations are
\(s_ut_v+s_vt_u=0\).  Three active ports would imply
\(r_u=-r_v=-r_w\) and \(r_v=-r_w\), hence \(2r_v=0\); characteristic zero
therefore gives support size at most two.  The one-slot quotients then lift the
two-port normal form and kill every direct \(B\)-block.  Ratios are used only
after the corresponding scalars have been declared nonzero; the pre-normalized
relations remain polynomial.

At rank two, both-quotient escape has one or two active ports.  Maximum-root
maximality turns the escaping residual factors into coordinate covectors:
otherwise a torus kernel, together with arbitrary inactive port vectors, would
extend the root to five vertices.  Quotienting the complete target by those
coordinate lines retains rank two on the \(H\)-side.  If the two residual
colours agree, the target has flattening rank two against a rank-one product;
if they differ, only the third target colour remains and forces the retained
\(H\) to have rank one.  Both are contradictions.

For one-sided escape, the singleton branch first kills every inactive block by
a rank comparison.  A torus zero of rank-two \(H\) then makes the other five
vertices a forbidden root.  In the two-port branch, realignment gives one
common contained residual factor and one escaping factor.  The maximum-root
argument makes the residual and local factors coordinate.  Pure target
coefficients force the two local colours, and the all-third-colour slice of the
complete target, together with \(\Pi_Q\ne0\), would make \(H\) rank one.  The
opposite shore is symmetric.  This proves pointwise that every rank-two
survivor is double-contained.  Rank zero is separately impossible because
GLS4 supplies \(H_Q\ne0\), and GLS10 excludes rank three on this six-response
chart (the seventh response is automatic on the full-rank chart).

Every five-root construction was checked on the named physical vertex set.
Each proof specifies the torus zero or kernel vectors killing the only
potentially nonzero edges; it does not infer maximality from a support mask or
from a formal companion deck.

### Rank-two conformal core and blocker consequences

On the double-contained rank-two branch the response equations are equivalent
to

~~~text
Xi_uv=A_u boxtimes C_v+A_v boxtimes C_u
      =H boxtimes D_uv,
B_uv=-D_uv.
~~~

The quotient class of \(\Xi_{uv}\) in
\((L\otimes M)/KH\) vanishes.  Defining
\(W_u=\operatorname{im}\Phi_u\subset L\oplus M\), the induced bilinear map

~~~text
beta((a,c),(a',c'))=[a tensor c'+a' tensor c]
~~~

makes distinct \(W_u\)'s mutually orthogonal.  A direct normal form for the
kernel of \(\beta(w,-)\) shows that it cannot contain two nonzero mutually
orthogonal vectors.  Pure-shore vectors in a \(W_u\) are excluded by a
response rank comparison followed by a forbidden five-root.  Thus both
projections of each nonzero \(W_u\) are injective, \(\dim W_u\le2\), and at
most two ports are active.  Zero or one active port again gives a five-root,
so exactly two are active.

At each active port, a torus point in \(\ker\Phi_u\) would combine with a
rank-two torus zero of \(H\) and the two inactive ports to make a five-root.
Annihilator duality therefore puts a fixed coordinate covector in the full
combined coefficient-row span.  The separate moving-blocker argument evaluates
the response at a regular torus zero of \(H\), shows that at most two port
kernels meet the torus, and hence forces both active ports to block every such
zero.  The theorem correctly distinguishes the fixed full-row-span blocker
from the possibly moving blocker after contraction.

Quotienting both residual slots of the complete target gives the exact
four-port identity using only the six \(B_{uv}\) blocks.  Both quotient spaces
remain labelled one-dimensional spaces.  This projection kills \(\Pi_Q\), so
the theorem does not infer target attachment or companion survival from the
clean quotient.

### Exhaustive rank-one trichotomy

Write \(H=x\otimes y\).  The two quotient families relative to \(Kx\) and
\(Ky\) have exactly three mutually exclusive patterns:

1. both are contained, giving the Wick form
   \(B_{uv}=-(a_u\otimes c_v+c_u\otimes a_v)\);
2. exactly one escapes, with common escaping support of size one or two and
   the stated singleton/two-port normal forms; or
3. both escape, with common support of size one or two and every direct
   \(B\)-block zero.

The cross-support lemma proves the size bound only in the escaping branches;
the double-contained branch is not assigned a support-at-most-two conclusion.
The maximum-root arguments in Branches I and II force only the explicitly
listed coordinate factors and disjunctions.  In Branch III the complete target
turns singleton support into a three-colour monomial triangle and two-port
support into the exact two-site insertion identity.  The proof does not assume
that independently declared companions integrate to one incidence family.

### Six pair responses versus the seventh response

For a scalar evaluation write \(h,a_u,c_u,b_{uv}\) for the six edge families
and \(t\) for the six-vertex hafnian.  Multiplying the fifteen-matching
expansion for \(t\) by \(h\) and substituting

~~~text
h b_uv+a_u c_v+a_v c_u=0
~~~

gives the denominator-free identity

~~~text
h t=-2 sum_(|S|=2) product_(u in S)a_u product_(v notin S)c_v.
~~~

Each of the six monomials occurs twice positively and four times negatively.
No value of \(h\) is inverted.  The exact rank-one control has all six pair
responses zero and the displayed quartic nonzero, so literal all-seven zero
requires that additional equation.

## Independent rederivation of the two follow-up exclusions

### Six labelled active-pair quotients

In the rank-two survivor, let \(s,t\) be the two active ports and \(m,n\) the
inactive ports.  At each active vertex \(v\in\{q_0,q_1,s,t\}\), let \(R_v\)
be its incident coefficient-support space.  The inherited injective
projections give

~~~text
dim R_q0=dim R_q1=2,
dim R_s=d_s in {1,2},
dim R_t=d_t in {1,2}.
~~~

Every active edge is supported on its endpoint spaces and every edge meeting
\(m\) or \(n\) vanishes.  Quotient any active pair
\(D=\{v,w\}\) by \(R_v,R_w\).  All physical edges incident to \(D\) die;
the unique complementary active edge \(P\) is the only possible survivor.
For

~~~text
Gamma_v={c:e_(v,c)^* belongs to R_v},
N_D=|{0,1,2}-(Gamma_v union Gamma_w)|,
~~~

the complete target becomes one simple tensor on the left and exactly \(N_D\)
independent colour summands on the right.  The two inactive labelled tags
\(e_{m,c}^*\otimes e_{n,c}^*\) keep those summands independent even if the two
quotient factors are proportional.  Hence \(N_D\ge2\) is impossible;
\(N_D=1\) makes the complementary physical edge a nonzero coordinate
rank-one tensor; and a rank-two complementary edge forces \(N_D=0\).  No
claim is made about the companion quotient when \(N_D=0\).

The four profiles \((d_s,d_t)=(1,1),(2,1),(1,2),(2,2)\) are exhaustive.  The
rank-two edge \(H\) excludes \((1,1)\) because two one-dimensional support
spaces cannot cover three colours.  In \((2,1)\), the rank-two \(A_s,C_s\)
edges force both residual support sets to be the same two colours, while the
opposite \(A_t\) quotient forces the missing colour onto a residual shore
where it is not contained.  The \((1,2)\) case is symmetric.

For \((2,2)\), the five rank-two edges force the missing-colour sets of
\(s,t,q_0\) to be three distinct singletons and the missing-colour set of
\(q_1\) to equal that of \(q_0\).  Both combined images are full planes.  In
bases with \(H=I_2\), write them as graphs of invertible matrices
\(\mathsf T,\mathsf S\).  Coefficientwise conformal orthogonality has the
unique solution

~~~text
mathsf T=qJ,                 mathsf S=-qJ,
J=[[0,1],[-1,0]],           q!=0.
~~~

The nonzero \(q\) is derived from invertibility.  Therefore

~~~text
[B_st]=q X_s^T J X_t
~~~

has matrix rank two because \(X_s,X_t\) are surjective.  The remaining
\(D=Q\) quotient has exactly one target colour and forces the same edge to be
coordinate rank one.  This contradiction excludes the last profile and hence
the entire rank-two core.

### Common-incidence column splice and characteristic-zero descent

On the rank-one singleton triangle, the three pure companions are not
independent physical tensors.  All are permanents of the same four root-to-
outside incidence maps \(L_v:V_v\to K^4\), and their complements share the
same three tail maps.  If the three active colours are \(i,j,k\), splice the
three active columns into one synthetic map

~~~text
D[:,i]=alpha L_q1[:,i],
D[:,j]=beta  L_q0[:,j],
D[:,k]=gamma L_t[:,k].
~~~

Permanent multilinearity and the three multiplied pure-companion identities
then reconstruct all 81 coefficients as

~~~text
P_4(D,L_v1,L_v2,L_v3)
  =sum_c mu_c e_c tensor e_c tensor e_c tensor e_c.
~~~

The factors \(\alpha,\beta,\gamma\) are multiplied into columns and never
divided out.  The only division is postcomposition by the explicit invertible
output map \(\operatorname{diag}(\mu_0^{-1},\mu_1^{-1},\mu_2^{-1})\), using
the declared target nonzeros.  This would give a restriction
\(P_4\to\Delta_3\), contradicting the committed exact theorem
\(\operatorname{subrank}_{\mathbb C}(P_4)=2\).

For an arbitrary characteristic-zero field, all entries involved generate a
finitely generated subfield of \(K\) over \(\mathbb Q\).  Such a field embeds
injectively into \(\mathbb C\); the embedding preserves the finite polynomial
equalities and every declared nonzero.  A hypothetical characteristic-zero
splice would therefore yield the forbidden complex restriction.  This is a
valid descent, not an unsupported extension of the complex subrank theorem.
