## 1. Exact claim under review

The theorem starts in the complete nonzero `r=1` matrix-unit Laurent torus
for one fixed endpoint-label support.  For any selected target equations, it
forms the lattice generated by exponent differences inside each residual's
support and claims:

1. exact faithful descent of the combined target ideal to that lattice group
   algebra;
2. inclusion of every normalized mixed fibre, across arbitrary word
   multidegrees, in the endpoint-character kernel;
3. a direct-sum lattice criterion proving genuine noninteraction even when
   physical variables overlap;
4. a global unit-or-known-sign holonomy dichotomy once all fibres of a
   selected active cycle are binomial; and
5. extension of the existing torsion-sheet rank-zero/rank-one decision to
   residual equations from arbitrary multidegrees and to pure target anchors.

It does not claim that a cross-degree lattice overlap is forced, that overlap
makes an ideal a unit, or that an arbitrary active cycle is binomial.

## 2. Adversarial proof checks

### 2.1 Is each normalization legal?

Yes.  A mixed residual is normalized only after a matching monomial has been
chosen from its nonempty fibre.  Every physical amplitude is inverted in the
stated complete nonzero torus, so that monomial is a unit.  Empty mixed fibres
are zero equations.  An empty pure fibre gives the scalar `-1` and is already
a unit certificate.

The pure equations are not divided by a matching monomial or incorrectly
made homogeneous.  Their canonical reference is the constant target term,
whose exponent is zero.

### 2.2 Is the support-difference lattice reference independent?

Yes.  It is generated by all pairwise differences inside each polynomial
support.  Choosing another reference translates one normalized support by a
lattice exponent and multiplies that generator by a Laurent unit.  Neither
the lattice nor the ideal changes.

### 2.3 Is the ambient Laurent ring free when the lattice is nonsaturated?

Yes.  Choose one representative from every coset of `Z^E/L_S`.  The ambient
group algebra is the direct sum of rank-one copies of `C[L_S]` indexed by
those cosets.  Torsion or infinite order in the quotient changes the basis
index set, not freeness.  No Smith invariant is discarded.

### 2.4 Does faithful descent preserve actual torus solvability?

Yes over `C`.  Extension of a proper ideal remains proper by faithful
flatness, and a proper ideal in the Laurent coordinate ring has a complex
torus point.  Equivalently, every solution character on the subgroup extends
to the physical exponent lattice because `C*` is divisible.  The theorem is
deliberately stated over `C`; no unsupported extension claim is made over an
arbitrary characteristic-zero field.

### 2.5 Why do different multidegrees enter one kernel?

The absolute exponent of a matching maps to its full endpoint word under
`Gamma`, not merely to its three colour counts.  Two matchings in the same
exact word fibre therefore have difference in `ker Gamma`.  This remains true
separately for every word, so the lattice generated by all such differences
lies in the same kernel even when the words have different multiplicities.

The theorem does not infer that their absolute reference monomials have the
same character or that bridge transport changes multiplicity.  Homogeneous
target zero permits independent unit normalization before the equations are
combined.

### 2.6 Is this more than saying that equations share variables?

Yes.  The theorem identifies the exact intrinsic exponent lattice, proves
faithful ideal descent to its group algebra, and supplies an algebraic
separation criterion.  In the imported U7D fixture, the `(3,3,2)` neighbour
and `(4,4,0)` cycle use four common physical edge coordinates, but their
difference lattices have ranks one and three with combined rank four.  Their
intersection is zero, so the direct-sum theorem proves that the proper
neighbour block cannot change cycle elimination.

This fixture illustrates the criterion; it is not used as an arbitrary-order
case cover.

### 2.7 Does zero lattice intersection suffice for the direct sum?

Yes because `L` is defined as `L_0+L_1`.  If the intersection is zero, every
element has a unique decomposition and `L=L_0 direct_sum L_1`.  No saturation
inside the physical edge lattice is required.

### 2.8 Can a nonzero lattice overlap be called a coupling theorem?

No.  This was the main wording hazard found during review.  Nonzero overlap
only removes the direct-sum no-coupling certificate.  The equations may still
leave a proper quotient.  The reviewed theorem now distinguishes:

```text
common physical variables: not sufficient;
non-direct lattice overlap: permits interaction, not sufficient;
unit combined ideal: actual fixed-support exclusion.
```

Accordingly, the theorem calls the common lattice an exact algebraic
**interface**, not a universal unit-forcing result.

### 2.9 Why are pure targets different?

Their residuals include the constant `-1`.  Differences from exponent zero
are absolute pure-matching exponents, which need not lie in the endpoint-
character kernel.  Adding them enlarges the global lattice by anchor
directions.  The theorem retains this distinction explicitly and does not
silently classify pure anchors as mixed fibre ratios.

### 2.10 Can cross-multiplicity equations impose a stronger proper polynomial in H?

No once the selected active cycle is fully binomial and parity consistent.
The cycle core already sends `H` to the scalar `(-1)^m`.  In any nonzero
quotient, the kernel of `C[H]` is exactly the maximal ideal of that scalar.
Additional equations can make the quotient zero, producing the unit ideal,
but there is no third proper elimination ideal.

This conclusion is not a solution of U7.  It proves that the load-bearing
positive task is a unit certificate, not a different compatible polynomial
in `H`.

### 2.11 Does the holonomy dichotomy cover aggregate cycle fibres?

No.  It assumes every complete cycle fibre belongs to the binomial core.  If
one cycle fibre is aggregate, the relation `H=(-1)^m` has not been derived,
and only the global support-difference descent remains available.

### 2.12 Do the U7F low-rank criteria really extend across multidegrees?

Yes.  Their group-algebra proof requires a free input lattice containing all
residual supports and a parity-consistent binomial sublattice.  The new global
descent supplies exactly that input.  Sign-character extension, Smith
torsion, finite Fourier decomposition, and the univariate Laurent PID do not
refer to word multidegree.  Every torsion sheet is still required.

### 2.13 Is the result merely a generic Laurent-polynomial tautology?

The free support-difference descent is a general group-algebra mechanism, but
the checkpoint proves and records the missing mathematical bridge to the
matrix-unit proof forest: the exact matching supports, endpoint-character
kernel, pure-anchor distinction, active holonomy exponent, separability
criterion, and imported low-rank quotient all fit one fixed-support target
ideal.  That bridge removes the prior same-multidegree restriction.  It does
not claim to settle the remaining combinatorial unit-forcing problem.
