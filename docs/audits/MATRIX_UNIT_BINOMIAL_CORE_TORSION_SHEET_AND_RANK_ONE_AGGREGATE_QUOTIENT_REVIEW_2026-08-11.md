## 1. Exact claim under review

The theorem starts after the proved `U7E` normalization of a complete mixed
same-multidegree matrix-unit block to `C[L]`.  It selects a
parity-consistent binomial core containing all fibres of an active cycle whose
complete cycle fibres are binomial.  It claims:

1. exact untwisting of the signs and quotient to `C[L/L_B]`;
2. finite Fourier decomposition over the torsion subgroup of `L/L_B`;
3. sheetwise equivalence of the residual unit/properness question;
4. complete criteria in quotient free ranks zero and one; and
5. the elimination alternative `(1)` or `(H-(-1)^m)` when the selected cycle
   is included in the core.

It does not claim that an arbitrary cycle is binomial, that the quotient has
low free rank, or that the low-rank residual is necessarily a unit.

## 2. Adversarial proof checks

### 2.1 Can the sign character always be extended?

Yes over `C`.  Put `L_B <= L` in Smith form.  If `d_i e_i` generates one
finite-index direction and the sign character assigns it `s_i`, choose a
`d_i`th root of `s_i` for `e_i`; choose arbitrary nonzero values on the free
complement.  This is exactly the divisibility of `C*`.

The theorem deliberately states this checkpoint over `C`, not over an
arbitrary characteristic-zero field whose multiplicative group may lack the
needed roots.

### 2.2 Does untwisting change the solution problem?

No.  Multiplying each basis monomial `X^l` by a nonzero character value is a
group-algebra automorphism.  Because the extension takes every selected
`r_i` to `-1`, it sends `1+X^r_i` to `1-X^r_i` exactly.  No variable is
specialized and no equation is discarded.

### 2.3 Is the quotient really `C[L/L_B]` when `L_B` is nonsaturated?

Yes.  The natural group-algebra map has kernel generated by `1-X^l` for
`l in L_B`.  A generating set for `L_B` generates this ideal by the identity

```text
1-X^(a+b)=(1-X^a)+X^a(1-X^b)
```

and Laurent inversion.  Nonsaturation is retained as torsion in `L/L_B`; it
is not silently removed.

### 2.4 Does the answer depend on the chosen extension character?

No.  Two extensions differ by a character trivial on `L_B`, hence by a
character of `L/L_B`.  The resulting presentations are related by a
group-algebra automorphism.  Sheet labels and displayed coefficients can
change, but unit/properness and elimination cannot.

### 2.5 Is the torsion Fourier map an exact product or only point sampling?

It is an exact algebra isomorphism.  Over `C`, the group algebra of a finite
abelian group is the product of one copy of `C` for every character.  Tensoring
with the free Laurent ring gives a finite product of Laurent rings.  The
primitive idempotents of the finite group algebra isolate the factors, so an
ideal in the product is the product of its sheet ideals.

This would fail in the stated form in characteristic dividing the torsion
order; that boundary is excluded by working over `C`.

### 2.6 Is unit status really sheetwise?

Yes.  In a finite product `R_1 x ... x R_s`, an ideal contains the global
unit exactly when its projection to every `R_i` contains `1`.  It is proper
when at least one component ideal is proper.  The proof does not infer a
global result from one convenient character sheet.

### 2.7 Is the free-rank-zero criterion exhaustive?

Yes.  Every sheet ring is `C`; its residual ideal is zero precisely when all
residual scalars vanish, otherwise it is the whole field.  Enumerating all
`|T|` characters is finite and exhaustive.  Sampling fewer characters would
not prove either outcome, and the theorem does not authorize that shortcut.

### 2.8 Does a univariate gcd generate a Laurent ideal?

Yes.  `C[t^(+/-1)]` is the localization of the PID `C[t]` and is a PID.
Powers of `t` are units, so each polynomial must first be normalized by
removing its Laurent monomial factor.  A nonzero constant gcd is the unit
case; a zero gcd means every residual is zero; a nonconstant gcd is proper.

The primary and independent audits both include negative-exponent
regressions to prevent treating a power of `t` as a nonunit factor.

### 2.9 Does a proper rank-one sheet have an actual complex torus point?

Yes.  A normalized nonconstant gcd has a nonzero complex root because any
factor `t` was removed.  Every residual polynomial is divisible by the gcd,
so all vanish at that root.  If the gcd is zero, every nonzero `t` works.
Together with the torsion character, inverse untwisting, and extension from
`L` to the physical edge lattice, this gives an actual complex torus point
for the selected complete block.

### 2.10 Can aggregate equations impose a stronger nonunit polynomial in H?

No once all binomial cycle fibres are in the selected core.  That core already
contains `H-(-1)^m`.  If the aggregate quotient is nonzero, the elimination
ideal is proper.  Since `H-(-1)^m` is maximal in `C[H]`, the proper elimination
ideal containing it is exactly that ideal.  If every sheet is killed, the
whole ideal is `(1)`.  These are the only two cases.

### 2.11 Does the theorem cover aggregate cycle fibres?

No.  This was the main scope hazard found during review.  The theorem needs
all `m` cycle equations in a binomial core in order to force the sign before
the aggregate quotient.  If even one complete cycle fibre has three or more
terms, that fibre is not a core binomial and the holonomy argument does not
apply.  The reviewed theorem now states this exclusion in its status,
assumptions, and frontier consequence.

### 2.12 Is this merely the earlier two-coset closure lemma?

No.  The earlier partial-circuit lemma derives a new relation when one
residual amplitude has one or two nonzero coset terms.  It does not identify
the full binomial-core quotient, retain nonsaturated torsion, decompose every
aggregate equation across all torsion characters, or give complete
free-rank-zero and free-rank-one ideal criteria.  The two results are
compatible but have different scopes.

## 3. Evidence independence

The primary verifier uses exact SymPy arithmetic and checks:

- Smith quotient types `C_2`, `Z`, and `C_2 direct_sum Z`;
- character untwisting of positive-term aggregate fibres;
- exact Gaussian Fourier sheets and multiplication;
- proper and unit rank-one gcd systems;
- torsion-by-rank-one sheet gcds; and
- the holonomy unit/known-sign alternative.

The independent audit imports neither repository modules nor a symbolic
algebra package.  It separately implements:

- Gaussian rational arithmetic;
- the `C_2` group ring and Fourier transform;
- exact rational polynomial division and Euclidean gcd;
- Laurent monomial normalization;
- explicit quotient-coordinate maps; and
- complementary character-sheet unit covers.

The implementations share only the displayed curated algebraic inputs.  The
finite checks audit the mechanisms; the universal quotient and decomposition
claims rest on the written proofs.

## 4. Remaining boundaries

The theorem leaves all of the following open:

- an active cycle with an aggregate complete fibre;
- forcing any useful binomial core from arbitrary response data;
- proving quotient free rank at most one;
- proving that every low-rank sheet is killed;
- uniformly deciding free rank at least two;
- coupling different word multidegrees;
- pure-cofactor branching and alternating cycles;
- the deeper-blocker branch;
- arbitrary exclusion of the complete nonzero `r=1` matrix-unit branch; and
- the global conjecture.

No finite decorated support, random experiment, or bounded search is promoted
to an arbitrary-order case cover.

## 5. Verdict

The theorem is accepted as an exact characteristic-zero (`C`) arbitrary-order
reduction.  It closes the residual aggregate ideal problem for every selected
binomial-cycle core whose quotient free rank is zero or one, but it does not
prove that an arbitrary live `U7` instance enters or dies in those classes.

The canonical frontier should record:

```text
rank 0 quotient  -> finite exact character criterion;
rank 1 quotient  -> finite exact sheet-gcd criterion;
rank >=2         -> exact multivariate sheet ideals, open;
aggregate cycle  -> outside this theorem, open;
proper quotient  -> exactly H=(-1)^m;
unit quotient    -> fixed-support exclusion.
```

The global Krenn--Gu conjecture remains **UNRESOLVED**.
