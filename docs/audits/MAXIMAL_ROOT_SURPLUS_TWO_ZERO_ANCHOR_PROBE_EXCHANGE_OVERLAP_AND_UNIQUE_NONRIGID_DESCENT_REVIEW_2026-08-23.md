## Probe-exchange proof audit

Fix an old probe `a_sigma`, its vector `z`, a nonzero `k in ker J_n`, and a
colour with `z_c k_c!=0`.  If no other auxiliary label has a nonzero pure
`c`-shore from `a_sigma`, the coordinate-covector alternative supplies at
each other auxiliary label a contraction vector that kills the evaluated
probe shore but keeps coordinate `c` nonzero.

Every complete physical matching is then killed by the partner of
`a_sigma`:

```text
other old probe:       zero anchor;
n:                     joint-kernel equation;
any t!=n:              chosen edge-kernel equation.
```

The independent matching census gives exactly

```text
105=15+15+75,
```

with 15 matchings for each of the seven possible partners.  The surviving
target coefficient is the nonzero product of the selected `c`-coordinates.
This argument uses the complete matching tensor, not a formal deck or an
off-target response fixture.

The hypotheses `z_c!=0` and `k_c!=0` are load-bearing.  The theorem makes no
pointwise assertion in a colour already absent from either vector.

## Uniformity and exceptional-fibre audit

For fixed probe and colour, the pure-nonzero locus for each candidate label
is contained in a linear preimage `P_(sigma,t,c)`.  Empty loci are discarded
before irreducibility is invoked.  This is essential: the closure of an empty
pure-nonzero locus cannot certify a nonzero fixed block.

The remaining finitely many linear spaces cover the nonempty principal open
`D(z_c)`, so one is the whole probe space and comes from a genuinely nonempty
pure-nonzero locus.  Finite hyperplane avoidance activates the three fixed
blocks at one fully supported probe vector.  Repeating the argument inside
successive kernels of the selected scalar forms yields at most three strata
and retains all zero-block and coordinate-divisor fibres.  No generic fixed
block is used pointwise without this flag.

The irreducibility argument is over an infinite characteristic-zero field.
The finite-field verifier checks only the covector alternative, not this
geometric step; the written proof carries uniformization.

## Five-label overlap and joint-rank audit

On the `r=3` unique-nonrigid branch, the fully supported `k` activates all
three colours.  Each old probe therefore has an injective colour-to-label map
into the five rigid labels.  Two three-element images in a five-element set
must meet.

At an overlap, each entire probe block has a nonzero one-coordinate row
space.  Equal coordinates make the joint row space one axis and the kernel
its coordinate plane.  Unequal coordinates make the joint row space the two
coordinate axes and the kernel the remaining coordinate line.  The primary
and independent tables enumerate all `60^2=3600` ordered star pairs and all
`6480` overlap occurrences:

```text
2160 same-coordinate rank-one overlaps;
4320 cross-coordinate rank-two overlaps.
```

The overlap label is rigid because its kernel is contained in a coordinate
hyperplane, but it is deficient because the kernel is nonzero.  This does not
contradict the uniqueness of the **nonrigid** label.

## Six-vertex reconstruction and finite-theorem boundary

Contract the fully supported kernel at `n` and a boundary kernel vector at an
overlap label `t`.  Every surviving original matching either uses `n--t` or
sends `n,t` to the two vertices represented by the unique port--port edge in
the effective six-vertex matching.  The three branches of that edge are

```text
hW_uv+a_u tensor b_v+b_u tensor a_v.
```

The argument is polynomial and includes `h=0`.  Matchings sending either
contracted label to an old probe vanish by the joint-kernel equations; the
root edge vanishes by the zero anchor.  This is the same physical bijection
proved in `GLS58`, but `GLS59` repeats it because one contracted label is now
nonrigid.

In a same-coordinate overlap, one may choose a kernel vector with both other
coordinates nonzero, giving exactly two target colours.  In a
cross-coordinate overlap, the kernel is one coordinate axis, giving exactly
one target colour.  The accepted complete six-vertex theorem assumes at
least three nonzero target colours.  It cannot be reapplied to exclude either
descent.

Both verifiers independently reconstruct two full eight-vertex controls and
check all `729` coefficients after double contraction.  The same-coordinate
control has exactly two constant-colour words; the cross-coordinate control
has exactly one.  Each control also has:

- one rank-zero nonrigid label;
- five coordinate-rigid labels;
- a three-colour pure star from each old probe;
- a three-colour pure star from the nonrigid label;
- the declared rank-one or rank-two overlap.

These are local exact interface controls.  Their uncontracted eight-vertex
tensors are not the complete GHZ target, so they do not prove witness-locus
existence or refute any witness theorem.

## Natural receiver audit

`GLS56` proves that if the three pair responses among the nonrigid label's
pure-star neighbours are all target-diagonal, the three evaluated old-probe
shores at those neighbours are zero.  At `r=3`, only two other auxiliary
labels remain.  The pointwise probe-exchange theorem requires three distinct
nonzero pure neighbours for a fully supported old-probe vector.  Therefore
simultaneous target diagonality is impossible.

This closes the proposed old-probe `GLD3` receiver as a route, but it does not
exclude the source branch: an actual point can and must have an off-diagonal
star-pair response.  Off-diagonal activity is not the target-diagonal activity
accepted by `GLD3`.

## Hostile case-cover findings

1. The arbitrary-root theorem is conditional on a selected nonzero joint-
   kernel vector.  It is not an arbitrary-root proof that such a vector is
   fully supported or that the forced neighbours are rigid.
2. Zero anchor is essential.  With `W_(a_0,a_1)!=0`, matchings pairing the
   selected probe to the other probe remain.
3. The fixed-block statement has a complete exceptional-section flag, but
   the root-order-three overlap uses the whole-domain fixed blocks and the
   already-proved fully supported kernel at the unique nonrigid label.
4. A pure probe-incidence block is not a promoted pair response or a quotient
   survivor.
5. The second deficient label supplies a legal physical contraction, not a
   contradiction.  Binary and mono targets lie outside the accepted finite
   theorem's hypothesis.
6. One overlap does not encode all complete mixed equations.  Different
   overlap labels and different plane-kernel choices may be coupled, but that
   is a successor obligation.
7. The natural `GLD3` route is excluded because target diagonality fails.  No
   alternative receiver is constructed.
8. All-rigid `GLS57`/`GLS58`, nonzero anchor, response divisors, complete
   nuisance survival, synchronization, activity, target anchors, and
   arbitrary-root downstream coverage remain separate.
