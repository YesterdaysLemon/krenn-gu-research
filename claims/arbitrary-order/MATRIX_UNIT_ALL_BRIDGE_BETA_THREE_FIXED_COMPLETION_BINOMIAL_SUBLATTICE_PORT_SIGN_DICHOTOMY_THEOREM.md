# Matrix-unit all-bridge beta-three fixed-completion binomial-sublattice port-sign dichotomy

## Status and exact scope

This is an exact conditional characteristic-zero interface theorem between
the A6 fixed-completion rank-three block and a U7F parity-consistent binomial
core in the same complete mixed multidegree.

In addition to the A6 hypotheses, assume that the three A6 matching
differences lie in the lattice generated by the chosen binomial core:

~~~text
L_A6=<u_1,u_2,u_3>_Z subset L_bin.                  (1)
~~~

The parity-consistent binomial core has one fixed sign character
`rho_bin:L_bin->{+1,-1}`.  It is this single character, restricted to
`L_A6`, that controls the A6 block.  The restriction is not chosen separately
on different torsion-character sheets.

Modulo the binomial-core ideal, the normalized A6 branch polynomial becomes
the scalar

~~~text
s=1+rho_bin(u_1)+rho_bin(u_2)+rho_bin(u_3).          (2)
~~~

There is an exact dichotomy.

1. If `s!=0`, adjoining the A6 branch equation makes the whole normalized
   block ideal the unit ideal.  This excludes that fixed-completion branch,
   globally across all quotient sheets.
2. If `s=0`, the four block signs consist of exactly two plus signs and two
   minus signs.  The A6 branch polynomial is then already in the binomial-
   core ideal.

In the A6 `Q/C^2` case, its two complementary nonzero doubleton port sums
sharpen the balanced alternative.  The signs must be constant within each
doubleton and opposite across the two doubletons.  Of the three balanced
partitions, only the partition aligned with the A5 doubletons survives on
the A5 nonzero-port locus.  A misaligned balanced partition is excluded only
after localization, equivalently saturation, by those two port sums.  The
unsaturated binomial-core ideal need not be the unit ideal.

In the `Q/Q` case every A5 port in the block is a singleton, so the ports add
no restriction beyond the two-plus/two-minus condition.

This theorem does not force the lattice containment (1), a compatible fixed
completion, or a suitable binomial core.  It does not say that the A6 fibre
is binomial, promote the A6 branch equation to a universal target generator,
construct a physical witness, or exclude the simultaneous all-bridge branch.
The global Krenn--Gu conjecture remains **UNRESOLVED**.

## 1. A6 block inside one complete mixed multidegree

Work over `C` in the complete nonzero `r=1` matrix-unit torus.  Assume the
full A6 fixed-completion setup.  Thus a globally least beta-three bipartite
pure core with one of the A5 `Q/Q` or `Q/C^2` route kernels has exactly four
perfect matchings, and one fixed nonzero completion `K` embeds them into one
complete mixed word fibre.

Choose one completed matching as reference and write the four normalized
exponents as

~~~text
0,u_1,u_2,u_3.                                      (3)
~~~

A6 proves that `u_1,u_2,u_3` are linearly independent and that

~~~text
L_A6=Z u_1 direct_sum Z u_2 direct_sum Z u_3        (4)
~~~

has rank three.  The normalized formal block polynomial is

~~~text
p_A6=1+X^u_1+X^u_2+X^u_3.                           (5)
~~~

At the A6 fixed-completion branch, the pointwise pure cancellation supplies
the additional equation

~~~text
p_A6=0.                                             (6)
~~~

Equation (6) is load-bearing and branch-specific.  Although all four terms
lie in a complete mixed fibre, `p_A6` may be only a proper zero subrelation
of that complete fibre equation.  It is not asserted to be a generator of
the target ideal away from the A6 completion branch.

Let `mu` be the mixed multidegree of that fibre.  Following U7E, let

~~~text
A_mu=C[L_mu]                                        (7)
~~~

be the group algebra of the lattice generated by all within-fibre matching
differences in the complete `mu` block.  The A6 differences lie in `L_mu`,
so `p_A6 in A_mu`.

## 2. Parity-consistent binomial core and containment

Choose a set `B` of complete binomial fibres of multidegree `mu`.  Orient
their normalized equations as

~~~text
1+X^r_j=0,             j in B,                       (8)
~~~

and put

~~~text
L_bin=<r_j:j in B>_Z subset L_mu,
J_bin=(1+X^r_j:j in B) subset A_mu.                  (9)
~~~

Assume the core is parity-consistent: every integer relation

~~~text
sum_j c_j r_j=0                                      (10)
~~~

has even coefficient sum.  U7E and U7F then give the well-defined sign
character

~~~text
rho_bin(sum_j c_j r_j)=(-1)^(sum_j c_j)             (11)
~~~

and an exact quotient by the binomial core.  In the original, non-untwisted
presentation, every `l in L_bin` satisfies

~~~text
X^l=rho_bin(l)       modulo J_bin.                   (12)
~~~

The new interface assumption is precisely (1).  No saturation of `L_bin`
inside `L_mu` is assumed.  Because (1) concerns actual lattice membership,
not merely rational span, equation (12) applies to each `u_i`.

Write

~~~text
epsilon_0=1,
epsilon_i=rho_bin(u_i) in {+1,-1},  i=1,2,3.         (13)
~~~

These four signs are fixed by the chosen binomial core.  If U7F subsequently
decomposes `L_mu/L_bin` into torsion-character sheets, the `u_i` already
have zero quotient class.  Hence every sheet sees the same signs (13) and
the same scalar (2).  The eight possible triples of abstract signs describe
possible restrictions arising from different binomial cores; they are not
eight independently selectable sheet values for one fixed core.

## 3. Scalar dichotomy

### Theorem 1 (global branch unit or balanced restriction)

Under (1), the image of `p_A6` in `A_mu/J_bin` is the scalar `s` from (2).
Exactly one of the following occurs.

#### Imbalanced branch

If `s!=0`, then

~~~text
(J_bin,p_A6)=A_mu.                                   (14)
~~~

Consequently the corresponding ideal in the full physical Laurent ring is
also the unit ideal.  There is no physical-torus point satisfying both the
chosen binomial core and the A6 fixed-completion branch equation.

#### Balanced branch

If `s=0`, then

~~~text
p_A6 in J_bin,                                      (15)

#{i in {0,1,2,3}:epsilon_i=+1}=2,
#{i in {0,1,2,3}:epsilon_i=-1}=2.                   (16)
~~~

In this case adjoining `p_A6` changes neither the binomial-core ideal nor
its quotient:

~~~text
(J_bin,p_A6)=J_bin.                                  (17)
~~~

The ideal `J_bin` is proper by parity consistency, but additional complete
target equations may still enlarge it to the unit ideal.

### Proof

By (1), (12) gives

~~~text
p_A6 = 1+epsilon_1+epsilon_2+epsilon_3 = s
        modulo J_bin.                                (18)
~~~

If `s!=0`, it is an invertible scalar over `C`; hence adjoining `p_A6`
adjoins `1` and proves (14).  U7E's faithful Laurent extension carries this
unit conclusion to the full physical Laurent ring.  Since the scalar (18)
is obtained before the U7F sheet decomposition and is the same on every
sheet, this is a global unit conclusion for the stated branch, not merely
the death of one sheet.

If `s=0`, (18) proves (15) and (17).  A sum of four numbers in `{+1,-1}` is
zero exactly when two are `+1` and two are `-1`, proving (16).  U7F's
character extension and exact quotient show that `J_bin` is proper before
any other residual equations are imposed.  QED.

This theorem distinguishes two meanings of exclusion.  Equation (14) is an
unsaturated unit certificate for the combined binomial-core plus A6 branch
ideal.  It does not make the binomial core alone a unit, and it does not
apply away from the fixed-completion branch where (6) is known.

## 4. The `Q/C^2` nonzero-port refinement

Assume now that the A6 core has the `Q/C^2` route kernel.  Use A5/A6 notation
for the four core matchings.  The even route has two complementary doubleton
blocks

~~~text
D_x={K union M_y1,K union M_y2},
D_y={K union M_x1,K union M_x2}.                     (19)
~~~

Choose the A6 reference matching in `D_x`.  Relabel within the doubletons so
that

~~~text
D_x has normalized exponents {0,u_1},
D_y has normalized exponents {u_2,u_3}.              (20)
~~~

The normalized edge-inclusive block-port polynomials are, up to their common
nonzero reference monomial,

~~~text
q_x=1+X^u_1,
q_y=X^u_2+X^u_3.                                    (21)
~~~

A5 and A6 prove pointwise on the fixed-completion branch that

~~~text
q_x!=0,
q_y!=0,
q_x=-q_y                                             (22)
~~~

after the same normalization.  The nonvanishing in (22) is the important
extra datum; the exact negation is already implied by `p_A6=q_x+q_y=0`.

### Theorem 2 (unique aligned balanced partition)

Suppose the scalar branch of Theorem 1 is balanced.  Then the A5 nonzero-port
locus is compatible with the binomial core if and only if

~~~text
epsilon_0=epsilon_1=-epsilon_2=-epsilon_3.           (23)
~~~

Equivalently, the signs are constant within each A5 doubleton and opposite
across the two doubletons.  With the reference convention
`epsilon_0=1`, the unique surviving restriction is

~~~text
(epsilon_0,epsilon_1,epsilon_2,epsilon_3)
   =(+1,+1,-1,-1).                                   (24)
~~~

For either of the two other balanced partitions, the normalized port
polynomials satisfy

~~~text
q_x=q_y=0 modulo J_bin.                              (25)
~~~

Thus the misaligned cases have no solution on the nonzero-port locus.  In
ideal language,

~~~text
(J_bin : (q_x q_y)^infinity)=A_mu                   (26)
~~~

for a misaligned restriction.  This is a localized or saturated exclusion;
it does not assert `J_bin=A_mu` in the unsaturated ring.

### Proof

Modulo `J_bin`, equations (12), (13), and (21) give

~~~text
q_x -> epsilon_0+epsilon_1,
q_y -> epsilon_2+epsilon_3.                          (27)
~~~

For two signs, their sum is nonzero exactly when they are equal.  Hence the
two nonvanishing requirements in (22) force equality within both
doubletons.  Balancedness then forces the common signs of the two
doubletons to be opposite.  The fixed reference sign gives (24).

Every other balanced partition places one plus and one minus sign in each
doubleton, proving (25).  Therefore `q_x,q_y in J_bin`, so their product lies
in `J_bin`; localizing at `q_x q_y`, or equivalently saturating by it, gives
the unit ideal (26).  This rules out precisely the locus on which both A5
port contributions are required to be nonzero.  QED.

The port polynomials in this section are restricted to the injected A6
block.  No assertion is made about all matchings in the complete mixed
fibre using the same endpoint edges.  Nor are `q_x` and `q_y` complete target
equations; their nonvanishing comes from the weighted A5 cofactor ports.

## 5. The `Q/Q` boundary

In the A6 `Q/Q` kernel, each of the four route ports inside the injected
block is a singleton.  After normalization its image modulo `J_bin` is one
of the signs `epsilon_i`, hence is always nonzero.  Therefore the port data
impose no further restriction beyond Theorem 1:

~~~text
Q/Q compatible sign condition: exactly two plus and two minus. (28)
~~~

This is an exact boundary, not a failure of the sign theorem.  Any exclusion
of a balanced `Q/Q` restriction must use another complete target equation,
another multidegree, completion coverage, or a different structural input.

## 6. Sharp algebraic controls

The following controls live in free group algebras.  They prove sharpness of
the algebraic interface; they are not complete physical matrix-unit tables
or simultaneous all-bridge witnesses.

### 6.1 Imbalanced unit control

Let

~~~text
L_A6=Z u_1 direct_sum Z u_2 direct_sum Z u_3
~~~

and choose three independent binomial generators

~~~text
r_1=u_1,
r_2=u_2,
r_3=u_3.                                             (29)
~~~

There is no integer relation among the `r_i`, so the binomial core is
parity-consistent.  Its fixed sign character has

~~~text
rho_bin(u_1)=rho_bin(u_2)=rho_bin(u_3)=-1,
s=1-1-1-1=-2.                                       (30)
~~~

Thus `J_bin` is proper by itself, while `(J_bin,p_A6)=(1)`.  This realizes
the imbalanced exclusion without any saturation or torsion-sheet argument.

An all-positive restriction is also algebraically realizable, but not from
the generators `r_i=u_i`.  For example, after adjoining an independent
direction `v`, the four independent generators

~~~text
v, v+u_1, v+u_2, v+u_3                              (31)
~~~

all receive sign `-1`, while every `u_i` receives sign `+1`, giving `s=4`.
This optional control prevents confusing abstract sign restrictions with
the signs forced by a particular generating set.

### 6.2 Aligned `Q/C^2` survivor control

Use the doubleton ordering (20) and choose

~~~text
r_1=u_2,
r_2=u_3,
r_3=u_1+u_2.                                        (32)
~~~

These three vectors are a unimodular basis of `L_A6`: relative to
`u_1,u_2,u_3` their determinant has absolute value one.  Hence there is no
integer kernel relation and the core is parity-consistent.  Assigning sign
`-1` to every generator gives

~~~text
rho_bin(u_1)=+1,
rho_bin(u_2)=rho_bin(u_3)=-1.                        (33)
~~~

Consequently

~~~text
s=1+1-1-1=0,
q_x=2,
q_y=-2.                                             (34)
~~~

The A6 branch equation is redundant, both port sums remain nonzero, and the
localized binomial-core ideal stays proper.  This control proves that the
aligned balanced case cannot be excluded by the A6 equation and port
nonvanishing alone.  Other complete residual equations could still exclude
a physical realization.

## 9. Exact boundary

This theorem proves exactly the following conditional interface:

~~~text
A6 fixed-completion branch
+ parity-consistent same-multidegree binomial core
+ integral containment L_A6 subset L_bin
-> imbalanced scalar unit, or balanced sign restriction;

balanced Q/C^2
+ A5 nonzero doubleton ports
-> the unique aligned sign partition on the localized port locus.
~~~

It does **not** prove any of the following:

- existence or universality of the A6 fixed completion;
- existence of a parity-consistent binomial core meeting (1);
- rational-span containment in place of integral lattice containment;
- saturation of `L_bin` in `L_mu`;
- that the A6 complete fibre is binomial or all-binomial;
- that the A6 branch polynomial is a universal complete-target generator;
- that a balanced misaligned `Q/C^2` core is a unit before port localization;
- that the aligned control extends to a complete physical matrix-unit table;
- that the remaining complete target equations preserve a balanced branch;
- exclusion of the balanced `Q/Q` case;
- exclusion of the simultaneous all-bridge or general `r=1` branch; or
- a proof or refutation of the Krenn--Gu conjecture.

The imbalanced outcome is a genuine unit for the stated combined branch
ideal.  The misaligned `Q/C^2` outcome is instead a no-solution theorem on
the A5 nonzero-port locus.  Keeping those conclusions distinct is
load-bearing.  The global status remains **UNRESOLVED**.
