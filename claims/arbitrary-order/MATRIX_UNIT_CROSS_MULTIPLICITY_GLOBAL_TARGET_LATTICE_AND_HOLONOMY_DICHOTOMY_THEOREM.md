# Matrix-unit cross-multiplicity global target lattice and holonomy dichotomy

## Status

This is an exact arbitrary-order theorem over `C` for one fixed complete
nonzero `r=1` matrix-unit label support.  It removes the same-multidegree
restriction from the lattice interface proved in `U7E` and from the residual
quotient interface proved in `U7F`.

For any selected collection of mixed target-zero equations, possibly drawn
from different endpoint-colour multidegrees, divide each nonzero coefficient
polynomial by one of its invertible matching monomials.  Every normalized
polynomial lies in one common group algebra

```text
C[L_mix],
```

where `L_mix` is generated by all within-fibre matching differences in the
selected collection.  The ambient physical Laurent ring is free and
faithfully flat over this algebra.  Unit-ideal status, properness, and every
holonomy elimination whose exponent lies in `L_mix` therefore descend
exactly.  No saturation or direct-summand hypothesis on `L_mix` is needed.

The endpoint-character map puts `L_mix` inside one universal character
kernel even when the selected words have different colour multiplicities.
This is the exact cross-multiplicity algebraic interface.  It is stronger
than the observation that equations share physical edge variables, but it
does **not** force the resulting ideal to be a unit.

The three pure target equations may be included as well.  Their constant
target terms add absolute matching-exponent directions to a larger global
support-difference lattice `L_tar`; they are exact anchor equations rather
than homogeneous mixed-fibre relations.

For a parity-consistent binomial active cycle, arbitrary additional target
equations from any multidegrees then have only two holonomy outcomes:

```text
unit ideal,                         or
exactly H=(-1)^m in every proper quotient.
```

Thus a cross-multiplicity continuation cannot produce a different stronger
**proper** polynomial condition on `H`.  It must prove that the combined
target ideal is the unit ideal.  After quotienting by a selected binomial
core, the existing torsion-sheet criteria in free ranks zero and one apply
to all selected target equations, not only to one same-degree block.

This theorem does not force a favourable lattice overlap, low quotient rank,
or unit certificate.  It does not cover an active cycle with an aggregate
cycle fibre.  The `r=1` branch and the global Krenn--Gu conjecture remain
**UNKNOWN/UNRESOLVED**.

## 1. Fixed-support target residuals

Let `Omega` have positive even cardinality and let `E` be the physical edges
of a fixed complete matrix-unit label support.  Every edge `e={u,v}` has one
ordered endpoint-colour pair

```text
(a_e(u),a_e(v)) in {0,1,2}^2
```

and one nonzero physical amplitude `lambda_e`.  Work in the physical Laurent
ring

```text
R=C[Z^E]=C[lambda_e^(+/-1):e in E].                 (1)
```

For a perfect matching `M`, write `1_M in Z^E` for its incidence exponent
and `chi_M` for its endpoint-label word.  For a word `chi`, put

```text
F_chi={M:chi_M=chi}.                                (2)
```

The target residual is

```text
P_chi = sum_(M in F_chi) lambda^(1_M)               if chi is mixed,
P_(c^Omega) = sum_(M in F_(c^Omega)) lambda^(1_M)-1 if chi=c^Omega. (3)
```

Thus every mixed equation is `P_chi=0`, while the three pure equations are
`P_(c^Omega)=0`.  An empty mixed fibre gives the zero polynomial and may be
ignored.  An empty pure fibre gives the scalar `-1` and already makes the
selected target ideal the unit ideal.

More generally, everything below applies to any finite selected family
`S` of nonzero Laurent residuals of the form (3).

For each `chi in S`, choose an exponent

```text
b_chi in supp(P_chi).                               (4)
```

For a mixed residual, `b_chi` may be the exponent of any reference matching.
For a pure residual, the canonical choice is the constant exponent zero.
Define

```text
Q_chi = lambda^(-b_chi) P_chi.                      (5)
```

Let the global support-difference lattice be

```text
L_S = < a-b : a,b in supp(P_chi), chi in S >_Z
      <= Z^E.                                       (6)
```

It is independent of all reference choices.  Equation (5) belongs to
`C[L_S]`, because every exponent in it has the form `a-b_chi`.

## 2. Exact global target-lattice descent

### Theorem 1 (global support-difference descent)

Put

```text
A_S=C[L_S],
J_S=(Q_chi:chi in S) <= A_S,
I_S=(P_chi:chi in S) <= R.                          (7)
```

Then

```text
I_S=J_S R,
I_S intersect A_S=J_S,                              (8)
```

and the following are equivalent:

1. `J_S=(1)` in `A_S`;
2. `I_S=(1)` in `R`;
3. the selected target equations have no solution with every physical
   amplitude nonzero.

If the ideals are proper, every common character solution on `L_S` extends
to at least one physical-torus solution on `Z^E`.

### Proof

Every factor `lambda^(b_chi)` in

```text
P_chi=lambda^(b_chi) Q_chi                         (9)
```

is a unit in `R`, proving the first identity in (8).

Choose one representative of every coset in `Z^E/L_S`.  Every exponent has
a unique expression as a lattice exponent plus its chosen representative,
so

```text
R = direct_sum_(t in Z^E/L_S) A_S lambda^t          (10)
```

as an `A_S`-module.  This is a free module and contains the identity coset
as a basis summand.  Hence `A_S -> R` is faithfully flat, even when `L_S` is
nonsaturated in `Z^E`.  Extension-contraction gives the second identity in
(8), and faithful flatness gives equivalence of the two unit statements.

The Laurent ring `R` is the coordinate ring of the complex physical torus.
By the Nullstellensatz, a proper ideal in `R` has a common point in that
torus.  Equivalently, a character `L_S -> C*` annihilating the normalized
residuals extends to `Z^E`: put the subgroup inclusion in Smith form and use
the divisibility of `C*` to choose all required roots.  This proves the final
claims.  QED.

The theorem is about the exact ideal, not a relaxation obtained by replacing
matching monomials with independent variables.

### Corollary 2 (reference and ambient-coordinate independence)

Changing any `b_chi` multiplies `Q_chi` by a unit of `A_S`.  Changing the
edge basis by any integral Laurent automorphism sends `L_S` and `J_S`
isomorphically to their transformed versions.  Unit status, solution
existence, and in-lattice elimination are therefore intrinsic.

## 3. Endpoint-character kernel across multidegrees

Define the endpoint-character homomorphism

```text
Gamma:Z^E -> Z^(Omega x {0,1,2})                    (11)
```

by sending an edge exponent to the two endpoint-colour basis vectors carried
by that matrix unit:

```text
Gamma(e)=epsilon_(u,a_e(u))+epsilon_(v,a_e(v)).      (12)
```

For every perfect matching,

```text
Gamma(1_M)=sum_(v in Omega) epsilon_(v,chi_M(v)).    (13)
```

### Theorem 3 (cross-multiplicity kernel theorem)

Let `S_mix` be any collection of mixed words, with no common-multidegree
assumption.  Then

```text
L_mix:=L_(S_mix) <= ker Gamma.                       (14)
```

Consequently every selected mixed equation, after its own invertible
reference monomial is removed, lies in the one exact algebra

```text
C[L_mix] <= C[ker Gamma].                            (15)
```

Equations from different endpoint-colour multidegrees can therefore interact
through common Laurent characters in `L_mix`; no transport operation between
their absolute words is required.

### Proof

If `M,N in F_chi`, then (13) gives

```text
Gamma(1_M-1_N)=0.                                   (16)
```

The generators in (6) for a mixed target-zero residual are exactly such
within-fibre differences.  This proves (14), regardless of the multidegree
of `chi`.  Equations (5) and (15) follow.  QED.

This does not identify reference monomials from different words.  Their
absolute endpoint characters remain different.  Homogeneous target zero is
what permits each mixed residual to be divided by its own invertible
reference before the normalized equations are combined.

### Pure target anchors

For a pure residual, the support also contains the constant exponent zero.
With the canonical reference `b_(c^Omega)=0`, its support differences include
the absolute exponents of its pure matching monomials.  These need not lie in
`ker Gamma`.  Thus the full target lattice has the exact form

```text
L_tar=< L_mix, pure matching exponents for c=0,1,2 >_Z, (17)
```

with all exponents from every multi-term pure fibre included.  The pure
equations are anchor relations in `C[L_tar]`; they are not silently treated
as mixed homogeneous equations.

## 4. What constitutes cross-multiplicity coupling

Physical variable overlap alone is not an exact coupling criterion.  The
support-difference lattice gives a simple rigorous no-coupling certificate.

Partition a selected normalized system into two families with lattices
`L_0,L_1` and ideals `J_0<=C[L_0]`, `J_1<=C[L_1]`.  Let

```text
L=L_0+L_1.                                          (18)
```

### Theorem 4 (direct-sum separation)

If

```text
L=L_0 direct_sum L_1,                               (19)
```

then

```text
C[L] isomorphic to C[L_0] tensor_C C[L_1],          (20)
```

and the combined quotient is

```text
C[L]/(J_0+J_1)
  isomorphic to (C[L_0]/J_0) tensor_C (C[L_1]/J_1). (21)
```

If `J_1` is proper, then

```text
(J_0+J_1) intersect C[L_0]=J_0.                     (22)
```

If `J_1` is a unit, the combined system is a unit.  The symmetric statements
hold with the two families exchanged.

### Proof

The direct sum in (19) gives the group-algebra tensor product (20), and
quotienting gives (21).  If `J_1` is proper, the second tensor factor in
(21) is a nonzero complex vector space.  Tensoring a complex vector space
with a nonzero complex vector space is faithful; explicitly, choose a linear
functional on the second quotient taking its identity to one.  It supplies
a left inverse to the natural map from the first quotient into (21).  This
proves (22).  QED.

Hence two blocks can share physical edge coordinates while remaining exactly
separated if their difference lattices form a direct sum.  A nonzero lattice
overlap permits interaction but does not by itself prove that the combined
ideal is a unit.

## 5. Arbitrary target equations versus binomial-cycle holonomy

Suppose a selected active cycle has length `m` and every complete cycle fibre
is binomial.  Normalize and orient those equations as

```text
1+X^(r_i)=0,                 i=1,...,m.              (23)
```

Assume the signed core is parity-consistent.  Thus its sign character is
well defined, and it forces

```text
z=r_1+...+r_m,
H=X^z=(-1)^m.                                      (24)
```

Let `S` now contain the cycle equations and any additional target residuals:
mixed words of arbitrary multidegrees, aggregate fibres, and any subset of
the three pure target anchors.  Construct `L_S,J_S,I_S` by Theorem 1 and
adjoin the exact definition `H-X^z`.

### Theorem 5 (global target holonomy dichotomy)

The elimination ideal in `C[H]` is exactly

```text
(1)                 if J_S=(1),
(H-(-1)^m)          if J_S is proper.                (25)
```

There is no third nonunit possibility, regardless of how many endpoint-
colour multidegrees occur in `S`.

### Proof

The binomial cycle equations already put `H-(-1)^m` in the enlarged ideal.
If `J_S` is a unit, Theorem 1 gives the first line.

If `J_S` is proper, the quotient is a nonzero `C`-algebra and (24) makes the
image of `H` the scalar `(-1)^m`.  The kernel of

```text
C[H] -> A_S/J_S
```

is therefore the maximal ideal of that scalar, namely
`(H-(-1)^m)`.  QED.

This is a broad method boundary.  Additional cross-multiplicity equations
can exclude the fixed support by making the ideal a unit.  They cannot leave
a proper system while imposing a polynomial on `H` incompatible with the
already forced sign.

If an active cycle fibre is aggregate, equations (23)--(24) have not been
proved.  The theorem then supplies the global lattice descent but not the
holonomy dichotomy.

## 6. Global low-rank residual decision

Let `L_B=<r_1,...,r_k>` be any parity-consistent selected binomial core in
the global lattice `L_S`, containing all binomial fibres of the active cycle.
The sign character on `L_B` extends to `L_S` over `C`.  Untwisting therefore
gives the exact quotient

```text
C[L_S]/J_B isomorphic to C[L_S/L_B].                (26)
```

Every remaining selected target equation, including equations from different
multidegrees and pure target anchors, maps to its full residual polynomial in
this quotient.

### Corollary 6 (cross-multiplicity torsion-sheet criterion)

Write

```text
L_S/L_B isomorphic to Z^q direct_sum T              (27)
```

with `T` finite.  Then:

1. finite Fourier decomposition over every character of `T` is exact;
2. for `q=0`, the combined ideal is a unit exactly when every finite
   character sheet contains a nonzero residual scalar;
3. for `q=1`, the combined ideal is a unit exactly when the normalized
   univariate Laurent gcd is a nonzero constant on every torsion sheet; and
4. if any sheet survives, the selected active-cycle elimination is exactly
   `(H-(-1)^m)`.

### Proof

The global descent in Theorem 1 supplies the only hypothesis of the
binomial-core quotient that previously came from one same-multidegree block.
The character extension, nonsaturated Smith torsion, finite Fourier product,
and Laurent-PID arguments of the imported
[`binomial-core torsion-sheet theorem`](MATRIX_UNIT_BINOMIAL_CORE_TORSION_SHEET_AND_RANK_ONE_AGGREGATE_QUOTIENT_THEOREM.md)
now apply verbatim to the residuals in `C[L_S/L_B]`.  Theorem 5 gives the
last statement.  QED.

For `q>=2`, the reduction remains exact but the residual sheet ideals are
arbitrary multivariate Laurent ideals.  No uniform unit criterion is proved
here.

## 7. Consequence for the live U7 edge

The proved cross-multiplicity decision tree is now:

```text
selected target residuals from any multidegrees
    -> one exact support-difference lattice algebra;

direct-sum difference lattices
    -> no holonomy transfer unless one block is already a unit;

parity-consistent binomial cycle plus arbitrary residuals
    -> unit ideal or exactly H=(-1)^m;

global quotient free rank 0
    -> finite exhaustive torsion-character decision;

global quotient free rank 1
    -> finite exhaustive torsion-sheet Laurent-gcd decision;

global quotient free rank at least 2
    -> exact multivariate sheet ideal, uniformly open.
```

This closes the idea that a different multidegree might yield a stronger
compatible nonunit polynomial in `H`.  The load-bearing positive obligation
is sharper: prove that the combined global target ideal is the unit ideal.
That still requires at least one new combinatorial or algebraic mechanism:

1. force a singleton, odd signed dependency, or another exact unit
   certificate from arbitrary active response data;
2. force a useful non-direct lattice overlap across multidegrees and show it
   kills every quotient sheet;
3. force quotient free rank at most one and kill every low-rank sheet;
4. control the rank-at-least-two multivariate ideals;
5. handle an aggregate active-cycle fibre; or
6. close the pure-cofactor or deeper-blocker exits.

No item in this list is claimed here.
