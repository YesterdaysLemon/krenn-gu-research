This document records a parent-theorem attempt on the all-order support
conjecture (AP') of the all-diagonal branch.  Its exact mathematical content
is:

1. **Lemma 1 (normalization):** every AP' model can be shrunk, without losing
   any axiom, to the family of sets reachable from `V` by supported Laplace
   descent together with the uniquely matchable sets.  Proved.
2. **Lemma 2 (chain normalization):** every AP' model contains a colour-0
   Laplace chain from `V` to the empty set, and vertices may be relabelled
   along it.  Proved; it is the soundness statement for the search below.
3. **Theorem 3 (branching lemma):** in every AP' model on `n >= 6` vertices
   some colour's reachable family is **not** generated by a single perfect
   matching: the edges used by supported Laplace steps between reachable sets
   do not form a matching.  Proved at every even order; it imports the
   nonmonochromatic-perfect-matching theorem for three-edge-coloured cubic
   graphs that WB1 already imports.
4. **Lemma 4 (the (F') ladder):** singleton noncancellation (F') is a valid
   necessary support condition that implies forcing (F), so the ten-vertex
   RZP model sits strictly between AP' and actual witnesses.  Proved; nothing
   new mathematically, recorded because it fixes which parent is the right
   one.
5. **Computation:** the results of bounded SAT searches for AP' and AP'+(F')
   at `n = 10` and `n = 12` using Lemmas 1 and 2 (Section 6); see the
   correction above for which of them stand.

None of this proves AP' at any new order by hand, and none of it is a
witness or an exclusion.  The all-diagonal branch and the global Krenn–Gu
conjecture remain **UNRESOLVED**.

## Parent-theorem checkpoint

**Parent proposition.**  For every even `n >= 6`, (AP') has no model on `n`
vertices.  (AP') is the support abstraction of
[WB2](ALL_DIAGONAL_SUPPORT_LEVEL_WEIGHTED_BOGDANOV_FINITE_EXCLUSION_THEOREM.md):
three graphs `G_c` on `V` and three families `S_c` of even subsets with
`(a)`, `(L)`, `(S)`, `(F)`, `(H2)` as stated there.  Quantifiers: all three
graphs and all three families; no weights, no field.

**Upstream supply.**  The WB2 bridge: every all-diagonal witness yields an
AP' model.  WB2 proves the parent at `n = 6, 8` by computation.

**Downstream consumer.**  The all-diagonal parent of the
[fibre-exact targets brief](../../docs/strategy/fibre-exact-targets-2026-09-01.md):
a proof of (AP') at every order shows that every Krenn–Gu witness has a
bichromatic entry.

**Sibling mechanisms synthesized.**  WB1's top-down structure (Laplace
accessibility from `V`, active-edge exclusivity, and the final
nonmonochromatic perfect matching of the cubic union of the three colour
matchings) and WB2/WB3's bottom-up support-only structure (forcing of
uniquely matchable sets, reachability without weights).  Theorem 3 is exactly
the statement that WB1's final step survives in the support abstraction
*whenever the Laplace strategy of every colour is uniform*, and that the only
open content of (AP') is therefore in non-uniform strategies.

**Controls tested.**  The two WB2 relaxations that are SAT at `n = 8`, the
WB3 ten-vertex "third colour needed" control and twelve-vertex "fixed
orientation insufficient" control, and the RZP10 result; see Section 6.

**Outcome.**  See Section 7 for the honest outcome and the proof-distance
delta.

## 1. Definitions

Fix an AP' model `(G_c, S_c)_{c=0,1,2}` on `V`, `|V| = n` even.

- For `A in S_c` and `v in A`, a **supported Laplace child** of `A` at `v` is
  a set `A - {v,u}` with `vu in G_c` and `A - {v,u} in S_c`.  Axiom (L) says
  every `A in S_c` has such a child at every `v in A`.
- The **reachable family** `R_c` is the smallest family containing `V` and
  closed under taking supported Laplace children.  Every member of `R_c` is in
  `S_c`.
- The **prefix family** is `P_c = {V - A : A in R_c}`.  A member of `P_c` is
  the vertex set of a matching `{e_1, ..., e_k}` of `G_c` that can be removed
  from `V` one edge at a time staying inside `S_c`.
- `U_c` is the family of even sets `A` such that `G_c[A]` has exactly one
  perfect matching, including the empty set and the edges of `G_c`.
- The **step edges** of colour `c` are
  `M_c = { e in G_c : some A in R_c has e subset A and A - e in R_c }`.
- `E_c = {e in G_c : V - e in S_c}` is the top-active graph of WB3.

Two consequences of (H2) used repeatedly:

- (two-part) for `c != d` there is no even `A` with `empty != A != V`,
  `A in S_c`, and `V - A in S_d`;
- (exclusivity) `E_c` and `G_d` are edge-disjoint for `c != d`: an edge
  `e in E_c ∩ G_d` would give `V - e in S_c` and `e in S_d`.

## 2. Lemma 1 (normalization)

**Lemma 1.**  If `(G_c, S_c)` is an AP' model, so is `(G_c, R_c ∪ U_c)`.
Consequently (AP') has a model on `V` if and only if it has a model in which
every member of every `S_c` is reachable from `V` or uniquely matchable.

*Proof.*  Write `S'_c = R_c ∪ U_c subset S_c`.

(a): `empty in U_c`; `V in R_c`.  A two-element member of `S'_c` is an edge
of `G_c` (members of `R_c` are in `S_c`, whose two-element members are edges;
two-element members of `U_c` are edges by definition), and every edge is in
`U_c`.

(L): let `A in S'_c` and `v in A`.  If `A in R_c`, a supported child
`A - {v,u}` exists in `S_c` by (L) for the original model, and it is
reachable, hence in `R_c`.  If `A in U_c` with unique perfect matching `N`,
then `A - {v, N(v)} in U_c`, because a second perfect matching of
`A - {v,N(v)}` together with `vN(v)` would be a second perfect matching of
`A`, and `vN(v) in G_c`.

(S): `R_c subset S_c` consists of matchable sets, and `U_c` does by
definition.

(F): uniquely matchable sets are in `U_c subset S'_c`.

(H1): `V in R_c`.  (H2): `S'_c subset S_c`, so every ordered partition with
classes in the `S'_c` has classes in the `S_c`.  ∎

The lemma does **not** preserve the singleton-noncancellation condition (F')
of Section 4: shrinking `S_c` can create a set outside `S'_c` with exactly one
`S'_c`-child at some vertex.  The search below therefore uses Lemma 1 only
for pure (AP').

## 3. Lemma 2 (chain normalization) and symmetry

**Lemma 2.**  Every AP' model has a chain
`V = A_0 ⊃ A_1 ⊃ ... ⊃ A_{n/2} = empty` with `A_i = A_{i-1} - e_i`,
`e_i in G_0`, and every `A_i in S_0`.  The axioms are invariant under
permutations of `V` and permutations of the colours, so an AP' model exists on
`V` if and only if one exists with `e_i = {2i-2, 2i-1}` for every `i`, that
is, with `{2i-2, 2i-1} in G_0` and `{2i, ..., n-1} in S_0` for all `i`.

*Proof.*  Apply (L) to `V` at vertex `0`, then to the child at its least
vertex, and so on; the chain reaches the empty set because every member of
`S_0` is even and (L) applies to every nonempty member.  Relabel `V` so that
the `i`-th removed edge is `{2i-2, 2i-1}`.  ∎

The symmetries that preserve the normalized chain include the `n/2`
transpositions `(2i-2  2i-1)` and the colour transposition `(1 2)`.  For each
of these generators the search adds the standard lex-leader constraint on the
vector of edge-support variables `(g[c][e])`, ordered by colour then edge.
This is sound because the lexicographically least assignment in each orbit of
the group generated by these symmetries satisfies every generator's lex-leader
constraint; imposing a subset of a group's lex-leader constraints never
removes every member of an orbit.

## 4. Lemma 4 (the (F') ladder)

For an all-diagonal witness and a vertex `v in A`, the Laplace expansion
`haf(Z^c[A]) = sum_u Z^c_vu haf(Z^c[A - vu])` has exactly one nonzero term
only if `haf(Z^c[A]) != 0`.  In support terms:

> **(F')** if `A notin S_c` then for every `v in A` the number of supported
> Laplace children of `A` at `v` is not one.

**Lemma 4.**  (F') is a valid necessary condition for the support model of
every all-diagonal witness, and (a), (S), (F') together imply (F).

*Proof.*  Validity is the single-term remark.  For the implication, induct on
`|A|`.  Let `G_c[A]` have exactly one perfect matching `N` and let `v in A`.
A supported child `A - {v,u}` requires `A - {v,u}` matchable, which with
`vu in G_c` gives a perfect matching of `A` through `vu`; so `u = N(v)`.  By
induction `A - {v,N(v)} in S_c`, and `vN(v) in G_c`, so there is exactly one
supported child at `v`.  By (F'), `A in S_c`.  ∎

Hence the models form a ladder

```text
all-diagonal witnesses  ->  AP' + (F')  (the RZP model of the ten-vertex theorem)  ->  AP'.
```

The [ten-vertex RZP theorem](../finite/n10/TEN_VERTEX_ALL_DIAGONAL_RECURSIVE_HAFNIAN_EXCLUSION_THEOREM.md)
excludes the middle model at `n = 10` with checked DRAT certificates.  A
proof of the parent for AP'+(F') at every order would carry the same
downstream consequence (every witness has a bichromatic entry) as a proof for
AP'; the parent proposition for AP' is the stronger and possibly false one.

## 5. Theorem 3 (branching lemma)

**Theorem 3.**  Let `(G_c, S_c)` be an AP' model on `n >= 6` vertices.  Then
for some colour `c` the step edges `M_c` do not form a matching: there are
reachable sets `A, A' in R_c` with supported children `A - e`, `A' - e'` and
`|e ∩ e'| = 1`.

*Proof.*  Suppose every `M_c` is a matching.

*Step 1: `M_c` is a perfect matching and `R_c = {V - ∪J : J subset M_c}`.*
(L) at `V` gives for every `v` a supported child `V - {v,u}`, so `vu in M_c`;
as `M_c` is a matching it is perfect.  Every supported step between reachable
sets removes an edge of `M_c`, so by induction along the generating closure
every `A in R_c` has the form `V - ∪J` with `J subset M_c`.  Conversely, let
`J subsetneq M_c` and assume `V - ∪J in R_c`; for `e in M_c - J` and `v in e`,
(L) gives a supported child `V - ∪J - {v,u}` with `vu in M_c`, and `vu = e`
because `M_c` is a matching.  So every `V - ∪J` is reachable.

*Step 2: the three matchings are pairwise disjoint.*  Each `e in M_c` has
`V - e in R_c subset S_c`, so `e in E_c`.  By exclusivity `E_c` is
edge-disjoint from `G_d supset M_d` for `d != c`.

*Step 3: a nonmonochromatic perfect matching.*  `K = M_0 ∪ M_1 ∪ M_2` is a
simple cubic graph on `n >= 6` vertices, properly three-edge-coloured with
perfect-matching colour classes.  By the imported theorem of Chandran,
Gajjala and Illickan (MFCS 2024, Theorem 1.7; the same import as WB1,
Step 10), `K` has a perfect matching `F` that is not one of the `M_c`.

*Step 4: the rainbow partition.*  Put `A_c = V(F ∩ M_c)`.  The `A_c` are even,
partition `V`, and at least two are nonempty because `F` is not
monochromatic.  Since `M_c` is a perfect matching of `V`,
`V - A_c = V(M_c - F)` is a union of `M_c`-edges, so `A_c in R_c subset S_c`
by Step 1.  This contradicts (H2).  ∎

**What the theorem says.**  Call a colour *uniform* when its reachable family
is generated by one perfect matching, as in the Bogdanov configuration of the
WB2 sharpness table.  Theorem 3 says every AP' model has a non-uniform colour:
some vertex `v` has two different supported Laplace partners `u != u'` in two
reachable contexts `A ∋ v` and `A' ∋ v`.  Equivalently, the parent proposition
for AP' is equivalent to the parent proposition for AP' restricted to models
with at least one non-uniform colour.  In WB1 the degree bound forces
uniformity after Steps 3–8; in WB3 non-uniformity is confined to the
orientation of even support cycles.  At unrestricted degree it is the whole
problem.

**Sharpness of the hypothesis.**  The theorem needs all three colours: the
two-colour Bogdanov configuration (two disjoint perfect matchings whose union
is a Hamiltonian cycle) has both colours uniform and no two-part violation.
It needs `n >= 6`: on four vertices `K_4` with its three perfect matchings is
uniform in every colour and is an AP' model.

## 7. Outcome and proof-distance delta

**Honest outcome.**  Theorem 3 is a proved implication: the parent
proposition for AP' at all orders is equivalent to the same proposition
restricted to models with a non-uniform colour, and WB1's final mechanism
(the nonmonochromatic perfect matching of the cubic union) is exactly what the
uniform case uses.  The finite case `n = 10` of the parent is closed by one
encoding with a checked certificate (the audit encoding; the primary
certificates were withdrawn after review); AP'+(F') at `n = 10` is excluded
only through RZP10 until a corrected certificate exists.

**Proof-distance delta.**  One parent obligation was replaced by strictly
smaller typed obligations:

- AP' at every even `n >= 6`  ⟶  AP' for models with a non-uniform colour at
  every even `n >= 12` (Theorem 3 for the uniform case; computation for
  `n <= 10`).

No exhaustive child cover closed; no two branches acquired a common theorem;
no route was eliminated by a countermodel (none was found: every bounded
search that finished was UNSAT).

**The next load-bearing lemma.**  Theorem 3 uses that the step edges of a
colour form a matching.  The weakest hypothesis under which its mechanism is
still available is that only the *top* step edges do:

> **Conjecture (top-matching lemma).**  No AP' model has all three top-active
> graphs `E_c = {e : V - e in S_c}` perfect matchings.

Under this hypothesis the three top-active matchings are pairwise disjoint and
their union `K` is a three-edge-coloured cubic graph with a nonmonochromatic
perfect matching `F`; what is missing is a reason for the classes
`V(F ∩ E_c)` to be in `S_c` when the deeper Laplace steps of some colour leave
its top matching.  The search above confirms the lemma at `n = 10` and the
`n = 12` run is recorded in Section 6.  A proof would reduce the parent to
models in which some vertex carries two top-active edges of the same colour,
which is where WB3's degree-four analysis stops being available and where
Theorem 3's branching must occur at the top level.

**What did not work.**  An attempt to prove AP' by induction on `n` through
deleting the first chain edge fails because the deleted pair is top-active in
colour 0 and therefore absent from the other two support graphs, so the
restricted structures of colours 1 and 2 lose axiom (H1).  Minimal
unsatisfiable rainbow-clause subsets of size 207 at `n = 8` make a short
case analysis unlikely at the level of partitions alone.

## 8. Second pass (2026-10-08, later): the branching WLOG and the top-matching lemma

### 8.1 Lemma 2' (branching on the chain)

**Lemma 2'.**  Every AP' model on `n >= 6` vertices has, after a permutation
of the colours and a relabelling of `V`, the normalized colour-0 chain of
Lemma 2 **and** a chain vertex `v in {2i-2, 2i-1}` that has, in some
reachable set `A in R_0` containing `v`, a supported Laplace partner
`u != v`'s chain partner.

*Proof.*  Theorem 3 gives a colour `c`, a vertex `v`, and reachable sets
`A ∋ v` and `A' ∋ v` with supported partners `u != u'`.  Permute the colours so
that `c = 0`.  Because `A` is reachable there is a chain of supported steps
from `V` to `A`; continue it through `A - {v,u}` to the empty set by (L).
Relabel `V` so that this chain is the normalized one; then `{v,u}` is a chain
edge `{2i-2, 2i-1}` and `A = {2i-2, ..., n-1}`, and `A'` witnesses the second
partner.  The statement is symmetric under the endpoint swap of every chain
edge and under the colour swap `(1 2)`, so the lex-leader clauses of Section 3
remain sound.  ∎

The search option `--branching` (which needs `--normalize`, whose reachability
variables were also made exact in this pass) adds this hypothesis.  Its
`n = 10` run (73,776 variables, 260,443 clauses, 493 s) was produced with the
unsound symmetry block and is withdrawn; no corrected `n = 10` or `n = 12`
result with this hypothesis exists yet.

### 8.2 Which consequences are cheap at `n = 10` (withdrawn)

**All timings in this subsection were produced with the unsound symmetry
block and are withdrawn.**  The table is kept for the record only.

For each candidate statement `L` the instance AP' + top-matching + `not L`
was solved; the time to refute `not L`, against 169 s for the top-matching
instance alone, measures how far `L` is from the axioms.  This is not a
proof of `L` beyond `n = 10`.

| candidate `L` (under the top-matching hypothesis) | refutation of `not L` |
|---|---|
| `G_0 != M_0`, i.e. colour 0 has a non-top-active edge | 0.3 s |
| `G_1 != M_1` | 6.9 s |
| the top matchings of colours 0 and 1 form a Hamiltonian cycle | 245 s |
| the top matchings of colours 1 and 2 form a Hamiltonian cycle | 308 s |
| some `G_c` contains an `M_c`-alternating 4-cycle | 175 s |

| some colour branches at level 2 | 51 s |

Reading: in a top-matching model every colour must have an extra edge; that
is a short consequence.  Pairwise Hamiltonicity of the top matchings, the
presence of a short alternating cycle, and top-level branching are each
essentially as hard as the whole `n = 10` problem, so none of them is a
cheap stepping stone; a hand proof of the top-matching lemma should not
expect to establish them first.

### 8.3 Towards "every colour has an extra edge"

Let a top-matching model have `G_0 = M_0`.  Then every `G_0`-matchable set
is a union of `M_0`-edges with a unique perfect matching, so `S_0` is exactly
the family of `M_0`-unions.  By (H2):

- (i) no proper nonempty `M_0`-union lies in `S_1` or in `S_2`;
- (ii) no `M_0`-union `U` has `V - U = A_1 ⊔ A_2` with `A_1 in S_1`,
  `A_2 in S_2`, both nonempty.

Build a colour-1 chain from `V` adversarially: after the first step
`V - ab` (`ab in M_1`), always expand a *widow*, a remaining vertex whose
`M_0`-partner has already been removed.  Write the removed set as
`B = P ⊔ W` with `P` an `M_0`-union and `W` the removed vertices whose
`M_0`-partners remain.  Expanding a widow `w` with partner `z` either removes
a second widow (`|W|` drops by two) or a fresh vertex (`|W|` unchanged), so
`|W| in {0, 2}` along the chain; `|W| = 0` before the end makes `B` an
`M_0`-union with `V - B in R_1`, contradicting (i).  Hence `|W| = 2` at every
intermediate step, and by (ii) with `A_1 = V - B`, `U = P`, `A_2 = W`:

> the half-pair `W` of every intermediate set of such a chain is not an
> edge of `G_2`, and more generally `W ∪ (P - P')` is outside `S_2` for every
> `M_0`-sub-union `P' ⊆ P`.

The same holds with colours 1 and 2 exchanged.  So the model's partner
choices along every widow-first chain of one colour are confined by the
other colour's support.  I did not close this into a contradiction by hand.
(An earlier remark that the solver does so in 0.3 s at `n = 10` is withdrawn
with Section 8.2.)  The
[extra-edge document](ALL_DIAGONAL_SUPPORT_LEVEL_AP_PRIME_TOP_MATCHING_EXTRA_EDGE_LEMMA.md)
proves that the axiom subset behind any such short refutation has models at
large orders.  This is the sharpest sub-lemma of the top-matching
lemma with a started proof.
