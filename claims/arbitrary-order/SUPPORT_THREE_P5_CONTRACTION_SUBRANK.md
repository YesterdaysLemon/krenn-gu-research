# Support-three `P_5` contraction subrank obstruction

## Status

This is an exact ordinary-subrank theorem over `C`.  It closes the
previously open support-three contraction case in the attempt to extend
the order-four permanent obstruction:

```text
every contraction of P_5 by a vector of support at most three
has ordinary subrank at most two.                         (1)
```

For support exactly three the subrank is exactly two.  This is not yet a
proof that `P_5` itself has subrank two, and it is not a proof of the
global Krenn--Gu conjecture.

## The quartic tensor

Let

```text
V = X direct-sum <a,b>,   dim(X)=3,
```

and let `q` be a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form on `X`.  Extend
`a,b` to covectors on `V` that vanish on `X`, and extend `q` by zero off
`X`.  Consider the symmetric four-tensor

```text
T = Sym(a tensor b tensor q).                            (2)
```

Equivalently, up to a nonzero scalar, its quartic polynomial is

```text
a b q.
```

**Theorem.**  The ordinary subrank of `T` is at most two.

Assume for a contradiction that there are injective maps

```text
phi_i : C^3 -> V,   i=0,1,2,3,
```

whose pullback of `T` is the three-term diagonal tensor `Delta_3`.
Injectivity follows already from conciseness of `Delta_3`.

Put

```text
alpha_i = a composed with phi_i,
beta_i  = b composed with phi_i,
K_i     = ker(alpha_i) intersect ker(beta_i).            (3)
```

Every `K_i` is nonzero because it is the kernel of at most two
covectors on `C^3`.

## The `P_3` contraction constraint

Take nonzero `t in K_i`.  Then `phi_i(t)` is a nonzero vector of `X`.
Contracting (2) in mode `i` gives

```text
Sym(a tensor b tensor q(phi_i(t), -)).                   (4)
```

The three displayed linear forms are independent: `q` is
nondegenerate, while `a,b` vanish on `X`.  After discarding its radical,
(4) is therefore the order-three permanent tensor `P_3`.

On the target side the same contraction is

```text
sum_(c=0)^2 t[c] e_c tensor e_c tensor e_c.              (5)
```

If all three coordinates of `t` were nonzero, (5) would be equivalent
to `Delta_3`.  That would give a restriction from `P_3` to `Delta_3`.
Both tensors are concise in dimension three, so the three local maps
would be invertible and preserve tensor rank.  This is impossible:
`P_3` has tensor rank four, whereas `Delta_3` has tensor rank three.

Thus every vector in every `K_i` has at least one zero coordinate.  A
linear subspace over the infinite field `C` cannot be covered by three
proper subspaces unless it lies in one of them.  Consequently

```text
1 <= dim(K_i) <= 2,
K_i is contained in a coordinate hyperplane.            (6)
```

There are only two kernel types:

1. `K_i` is a line generated by a vector whose coordinate support has
   size one or two.  Then `span(alpha_i,beta_i)=K_i^perp` has dimension
   two.
2. `K_i` is a coordinate plane `H_c={t:t[c]=0}`.  Then
   `span(alpha_i,beta_i)=<e_c^*>` has dimension one.

## Complementary-pair identity

For distinct modes `i,j`, define

```text
F_ij(x_i,x_j)
  = alpha_i(x_i) beta_j(x_j)
    + beta_i(x_i) alpha_j(x_j).                         (7)
```

If `{i,j}` and `{k,l}` are complementary pairs, pulling (2) back gives

```text
Delta_3 = sum_(i<j) F_ij Q_kl,                          (8)
```

where `Q_kl` is the pullback of `q` to modes `k,l`.
Restrict modes `k,l` to `K_k,K_l`.  Every term except the complementary
one vanishes, so

```text
sum_c x_i[c] x_j[c] x_k[c] x_l[c]
  = F_ij(x_i,x_j) Q_kl(x_k,x_l).                        (9)
```

As `x_k,x_l` vary, the diagonal matrices

```text
diag(x_k[0]x_l[0], x_k[1]x_l[1], x_k[2]x_l[2])          (10)
```

therefore span a space of dimension at most one.  Whenever (10) is
nonzero, it is proportional to `F_ij`.  In particular, their ranks and
their row and column spaces agree.

## Exhausting the kernel types

Call a two-dimensional `K_i` a *plane* and a one-dimensional `K_i` a
*line*.  A line is recorded by the one- or two-element coordinate
support of its generator.  A plane is `H_c` and is recorded by its
missing coordinate `c`.

### No planes

Suppose all four kernels are lines.  If two line supports intersect in
exactly one coordinate, (10) has rank one, while the complementary
`F_ij` has rank two because both complementary maps in (3) have rank
two.  This is impossible.

If two line supports intersect in two coordinates, they are the same
two-element set `P`.  The corresponding diagonal matrix has rank two.
Its complementary `F_ij` must have row and column spaces equal to the
coordinate plane on `P`.  Hence both complementary kernels are the
singleton line on the third coordinate.  Those two singleton supports
then intersect in exactly one coordinate, which was just excluded.

Thus all four nonempty line supports would have to be pairwise
disjoint, impossible with only three coordinates.

### Exactly one plane

Let the plane be `H_c`.  Pair it in (9) with any of the other three
lines.  The complementary `F_ij` has rank two.  The left side has rank
at most one, so it must be zero.  Therefore every line is the singleton
line `<e_c>`.

Now pair two of those lines.  The left side of (9) is a nonzero
rank-one diagonal matrix supported at `c`.  The complementary modes are
`H_c` and `<e_c>`.  Their covector spaces are respectively

```text
<e_c^*>   and   <e_d^*: d != c>.
```

The rank-one form `F_ij` cannot have both row and column space
`<e_c^*>`.  Contradiction.

### Exactly two planes

The two planes must miss different coordinates; otherwise their
coordinatewise products span a two-dimensional space, contrary to
(10).  For distinct missing coordinates their product is a nonzero
singleton coordinate.  The complementary modes are both lines, so
`F_ij` has rank two, again contradicting the rank-one left side.

### Exactly three planes

The three planes must have three different missing coordinates.  The
remaining line cannot have two-coordinate support: pairing it with the
plane missing the third coordinate would make (10) span a
two-dimensional space.  Thus it is a singleton line `<e_d>`.

Pair the two planes whose missing coordinates are the two colours other
than `d`.  Their coordinatewise product is supported at `d`.  The
complementary modes are `H_d` and `<e_d>`, whose covector spaces are
`<e_d^*>` and the coordinate plane excluding `e_d^*`.  As in the
one-plane case, their `F_ij` cannot be the required diagonal form
supported at `d`.  Contradiction.

### Four planes

Two of four planes must miss the same one of three coordinates.  Their
coordinatewise products span the other two coordinates, violating the
one-dimensional bound (10).

Every case is impossible, proving the theorem.

## Application to contractions of `P_5`

Contract the order-five permanent tensor by

```text
z = (z_0,z_1,z_2,0,0),   z_0 z_1 z_2 != 0.
```

Its quartic polynomial is, up to a nonzero scalar,

```text
x_3 x_4 (z_0 x_1 x_2 + z_1 x_0 x_2 + z_2 x_0 x_1).    (11)
```

The matrix of the quadratic factor has determinant
`2 z_0 z_1 z_2`, so it is nondegenerate.  Equation (11) is exactly the
tensor in (2), and hence has subrank at most two.

It has subrank at least two.  The two support entries

```text
(0,1,3,4),   (1,3,4,0)
```

form an induced two-edge diagonal box; both have the nonzero coefficient
`z_2`.  Coordinate projections and one rescaling give `Delta_2`.
Therefore every support-three contraction has exact subrank two.

If the contraction vector has support one or two, its essential
quartic is a product of four independent linear forms, hence is an
order-four permanent tensor after an invertible change of coordinates.
The order-four permanent subrank theorem again gives subrank two.
This proves (1).

## Boundary

If `P_5` restricted to `Delta_3`, then in every local three-plane and
for every coordinate three-plane of `C^5`, every intersection vector
would pull back to a target vector with a zero coordinate.  Equivalently,
for every pair of the five row covectors of every local map, their span
would have to contain a target coordinate covector.  Turning that
five-point projective incidence condition into a contradiction is the
next boundary; the theorem above does not silently assume it.
