# Matrix-unit complete same-multidegree fibre-lattice reduction and binomial parity dichotomy

## Status

This is an exact **arbitrary-order structural reduction** over every
characteristic-zero field.  It applies to the complete nonzero `r=1`
matrix-unit torus and to the complete target-zero block of any fixed mixed
word multidegree.

It proves four things.

1. Every complete same-multidegree block descends, after one monomial is
   removed from each nonempty matching fibre, to the group algebra of the
   lattice generated by within-fibre matching differences.
2. This descent preserves unit-ideal status and preserves elimination of any
   holonomy monomial whose exponent lies in that lattice.
3. A singleton fibre is a Laurent unit, at every order and for every label
   support.
4. If every nonempty fibre is binomial, the entire block is classified by one
   exact integer-kernel parity test.  An odd signed dependency makes the block
   the unit ideal.  If no such dependency exists, the block is proper and an
   active cycle's holonomy elimination is exactly its already-known linear
   sign equation; the rest of the same-degree block cannot secretly impose a
   stronger polynomial in that holonomy.

The signed-lattice parity criterion itself was already proved in
[`INTEGER_SIGNED_LATTICE_TRANSPORT_THEOREM.md`](INTEGER_SIGNED_LATTICE_TRANSPORT_THEOREM.md).
The new result is the arbitrary-order bridge from complete matrix-unit word
blocks to that lattice theorem, including faithful descent and exact
holonomy elimination.

This theorem does **not** show that an arbitrary active block contains a
singleton, an odd dependency, or only binomial fibres.  A fibre with at least
three compatible matchings remains an explicit aggregate Laurent polynomial.
Aggregate coupling, cross-multiplicity coupling, the pure-cofactor exits, the
deeper-blocker branch, exclusion of the `r=1` matrix-unit branch, and the
global Krenn--Gu conjecture remain **UNKNOWN/UNRESOLVED**.

## 1. Complete word block and normalized fibre polynomials

Let `n` be even and let `E` be the edge set of `K_n`.  Fix one nonzero
matrix unit on every physical edge.  Its endpoint labels are fixed elements
of `{0,1,2}`, while its nonzero physical amplitude is an independent Laurent
variable `lambda_e`.

For a perfect matching `M`, write

```text
lambda^M = product_(e in M) lambda_e,
1_M in Z^E
```

for its Laurent monomial and incidence exponent.  The endpoint labels of the
edges of `M` induce one word `chi(M) in {0,1,2}^n`.

Fix a mixed multidegree

```text
mu=(mu_0,mu_1,mu_2),
mu_0+mu_1+mu_2=n,
```

with at least two nonzero entries, and let `W_mu` be all words with those
three multiplicities.  For `chi in W_mu`, define the complete compatible
matching fibre and target coefficient

```text
F_chi = {M : chi(M)=chi},
C_chi = sum_(M in F_chi) lambda^M.                  (1)
```

An empty fibre contributes the zero polynomial.  Because `mu` is mixed,
target equality with the ternary GHZ tensor requires `C_chi=0` for every
`chi in W_mu`.

Work over a characteristic-zero field `k` in the Laurent ring

```text
R = k[Z^E]
  = k[lambda_e^(+/-1) : e in E].                  (2)
```

For every nonempty fibre choose one reference matching `M_chi`.  Define

```text
r_(chi,M) = 1_M - 1_(M_chi),
f_chi     = sum_(M in F_chi) X^r_(chi,M),          (3)
```

and let `L_mu <= Z^E` be the integer lattice generated by all exponents in
(3).  Set

```text
A_mu = k[L_mu],
J_mu = (f_chi : F_chi nonempty) in A_mu,
I_mu = (C_chi : chi in W_mu) in R.                 (4)
```

The choice of reference changes `f_chi` only by multiplication by a unit of
`A_mu`; therefore `J_mu` is independent of all reference choices.

## 2. Fibre-lattice descent

### Theorem 1 (exact normalized block)

For every even order, fixed complete matrix-unit label support, and mixed
multidegree,

```text
C_chi = lambda^(M_chi) f_chi,
I_mu  = J_mu R.                                    (5)
```

In particular, the complete block contains no exponent information outside
the within-fibre difference lattice once one invertible reference monomial
per fibre is removed.

### Proof

For `M in F_chi`, equation (3) gives

```text
lambda^M
 = lambda^(M_chi) lambda^(1_M-1_(M_chi)).
```

Summing over the complete fibre proves the first identity in (5).  The
reference monomial is a unit in `R`, so `C_chi` and `f_chi` generate the same
principal ideal in `R`.  Taking all words proves the second identity.  Every
exponent of every `f_chi` lies in `L_mu` by definition, hence `f_chi in
A_mu`.  No matching, word, or target equation has been discarded.  QED.

### Theorem 2 (faithful unit and elimination descent)

The inclusion `A_mu -> R` is free and faithfully flat.  Consequently

```text
J_mu R intersect A_mu = J_mu,
I_mu=R  iff  J_mu=A_mu.                             (6)
```

More generally, let `z in L_mu`, introduce an indeterminate `H`, and impose
the exact holonomy definition `H-X^z`.  Then

```text
((J_mu, H-X^z) R[H]) intersect k[H]
 = (J_mu, H-X^z) A_mu[H] intersect k[H].            (7)
```

Thus the smaller fibre-lattice group algebra computes the exact elimination
ideal in `H`; it is not a specialization or relaxation.

### Proof

Choose one representative `t_g in Z^E` for each coset
`g in Z^E/L_mu`.  Every exponent in `Z^E` has a unique expression

```text
l+t_g,  l in L_mu.
```

The Laurent monomials therefore give an `A_mu`-module decomposition

```text
R = direct_sum_(g in Z^E/L_mu) A_mu X^(t_g).        (8)
```

This is a free module, even when `L_mu` is nonsaturated and the quotient has
torsion.  The summand for the zero coset is `A_mu` itself, so extension and
contraction of an ideal of `A_mu` are exact.  This proves (6).

Adjoining `H` preserves the same direct-sum decomposition.  Because `z` lies
in `L_mu`, both `J_mu` and `H-X^z` lie in the zero-coset summand
`A_mu[H]`.  Extension followed by contraction therefore returns their ideal
in `A_mu[H]`.  Contracting once more to `k[H]` proves (7).  QED.

The proof uses only the coset basis of a group algebra.  It does not assume
that `L_mu` is saturated, that a selected minor is unimodular, or that the
matching differences span all of `Z^E`.

## 3. Singleton and all-binomial blocks

### Theorem 3 (complete binomial parity dichotomy)

The following conclusions hold for the complete block.

1. If some fibre is a singleton, then `f_chi=1`, hence

   ```text
   J_mu=A_mu and I_mu=R.                            (9)
   ```

2. Suppose instead that every nonempty fibre has exactly two matchings.
   Index those fibres by `i=1,...,s`, choose one matching as reference in
   each, and write

   ```text
   f_i=1+X^r_i,
   L_mu=<r_1,...,r_s>_Z.                           (10)
   ```

   Let

   ```text
   phi: Z^s -> L_mu,
   phi(c)=sum_i c_i r_i.                            (11)
   ```

   Then exactly one of the following occurs.

   **Odd-dependency branch.**  There is a `c in ker(phi)` with
   `sum_i c_i` odd.  In this case

   ```text
   J_mu=A_mu and I_mu=R.                            (12)
   ```

   **Parity-consistent branch.**  Every `c in ker(phi)` has even coefficient
   sum.  In this case `J_mu` and `I_mu` are proper.  The rule

   ```text
   rho(sum_i c_i r_i)=(-1)^(sum_i c_i)              (13)
   ```

   is a well-defined character `L_mu -> {+1,-1}` and gives a common exact
   zero of all normalized binomials in the group algebra.

3. Let `i_1,...,i_m` be the binomial fibres of an imported active transport
   cycle, oriented so that its Laurent holonomy exponent is

   ```text
   z=r_(i_1)+...+r_(i_m),
   H=X^z.                                           (14)
   ```

   Reversing any ratio replaces `r_i` by `-r_i` and does not change its
   forced sign.  In the odd-dependency branch the holonomy elimination ideal
   is `(1)`.  In the parity-consistent branch it is exactly

   ```text
   (J_mu, H-X^z) A_mu[H] intersect k[H]
     = (H-(-1)^m).                                  (15)
   ```

   By Theorem 2 the same equality holds in the full physical Laurent ring.

### Proof

A singleton has only the reference matching, so its normalized polynomial is
`1`; this proves item 1.

In the quotient by (10), every relation says `X^r_i=-1`.  For any
`c in Z^s`, including negative coefficients because all Laurent monomials
are units,

```text
X^(phi(c))=(-1)^(sum_i c_i).                        (16)
```

If `phi(c)=0` and the coefficient sum is odd, equation (16) gives `1=-1`.
Since the field has characteristic zero, `2` is invertible and the ideal is
the unit ideal.

If all kernel coefficient sums are even, two representations of the same
lattice vector differ by an even-sum kernel vector.  Thus (13) is
well-defined and multiplicative.  The evaluation

```text
ev_rho: k[L_mu] -> k,
ev_rho(X^l)=rho(l)
```

sends every `1+X^r_i` to zero.  Therefore `J_mu` is proper, and Theorem 2
makes `I_mu` proper.  Over `C`, the character also extends to `Z^E`: in a
Smith basis, choose the required roots on the finite-index directions and
arbitrary nonzero values on a complement.  Hence the proper branch has an
actual complex physical-torus point for this block, not merely a formal
quotient.

Finally, (10) and (14) force `H=(-1)^m`.  This proves containment of the
right side of (15).  In the parity-consistent branch the quotient is nonzero
and contains the base field faithfully, while `H` acts as the scalar
`(-1)^m`.  The kernel of `k[H] -> A_mu[H]/(J_mu,H-X^z)` is therefore exactly
the principal ideal `H-(-1)^m`, proving the reverse containment.  QED.
