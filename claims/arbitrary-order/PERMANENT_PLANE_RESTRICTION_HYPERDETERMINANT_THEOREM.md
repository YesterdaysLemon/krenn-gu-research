## Setting

- `perm(x, y, z) = sum_{pi in S_3} x_{pi1} y_{pi2} z_{pi3}` on `F^3`.
- `Y_0, Y_1, Y_2 in F^{3 x 2}`: the two rows of inner vertex `t` at its two
  chosen colours, restricted to the three outer columns, are the columns of
  `Y_t`.  `V_t` is the column span of `Y_t`.
- The **cube tensor**: `A_ijk = perm(Y_0[:, i], Y_1[:, j], Y_2[:, k])`,
  `i, j, k in {0, 1}`.  These are the eight cube words of the six-vertex cut
  (Corollary 5 of the bilinear-grid document).
- The **Plücker vector** `l_t = Y_t[:, 0] x Y_t[:, 1]` (cross product).  Its
  coordinates are the three `2 x 2` minors of `Y_t`, up to sign; `l_t != 0`
  iff `V_t` is a plane, and then `V_t = l_t^perp`.  `H_u = e_u^perp` is a
  coordinate plane, with Plücker vector `e_u`.
- `P_s = l_0[s_0] l_1[s_1] l_2[s_2]` for a permutation `s` of `{1, 2, 3}`;
  the six permutations split into three even and three odd ones.
- A trilinear Plücker monomial `l_0[a] l_1[b] l_2[c]` is **all-equal** if
  `a = b = c`, **two-equal** if exactly two of `a, b, c` are equal, and a
  **permutation monomial** `P_s` otherwise (`3 + 18 + 6 = 27`).
- `S` is the five-dimensional subspace of `(F^3)^{(x)3}` spanned by
  `e_a (x) e_a (x) e_a` (`a = 1, 2, 3`), `eps_+ = sum_{s even} e_s` and
  `eps_- = sum_{s odd} e_s`, where `e_s = e_{s0} (x) e_{s1} (x) e_{s2}`.
- The cube ideal `I = (A_ijk) subset F[Y]` (18 variables).

## Theorem 1 (the hyperdeterminant factors through Plücker coordinates)

Cayley's hyperdeterminant of the cube satisfies `Det(A) = F(l_0, l_1, l_2)`,
where `F` is the 21-term polynomial

```text
F = sum_s P_s^2  -  2 sum_{s,s' differ by a transposition} P_s P_s'  -  6 sum_{s != s' of equal parity} P_s P_s'
  = det(L)^2 - 8 (e2(P_even) + e2(P_odd))
  = 4 det [[Q_12, l_0], [l_0^T, 0]],
```

with `L` the `3 x 3` matrix with rows `l_0, l_1, l_2`, `e2` the second
elementary symmetric polynomial of the three even (resp. odd) `P_s`, sums
over unordered pairs, and `Q_12 = (l_1 l_2^T + l_2 l_1^T)/2 - 2 diag(l_1 * l_2)`
(entrywise product).  Moreover:

- **(a)** `F` is symmetric in `l_0, l_1, l_2` and absolutely irreducible.
- **(b)** Slice covariant: for `v in V_0`,
  `det(Y_1^T B_v Y_2) = q_0(v) = l_1^T adj(B_v) l_2 = (l_1.v)(l_2.v) - 2 sum_u l_1[u] l_2[u] v_u^2`,
  with `B_v` the hollow matrix of `v` and `adj(B_v) = v v^T - 2 diag(v)^2`.
  The determinant of the cube contracted with `x` in direction 0 is
  `q_0(Y_0 x)`, and `F` is `4` times the bordered discriminant of `q_0`
  on `V_0 = l_0^perp` (the third line of the display).
- **(c)** Special values: `F(e_1, e_2, e_3) = 1`; `F(e_a, e_a, e_a) = 0`;
  `F(l, l, l) = -48 (l_1 l_2 l_3)^2`; `F(l, l, m) = -8 (l_1 l_2 l_3) perm(l, m, m)`.
- **(d)** `F` lies in the ideal of `F[l]` generated by the space `J_3` of
  Theorem 2.  So the hyperdeterminant carries no rigidity information beyond
  the degree-three certificates.

*Proof.*  The identity `Det(A(Y)) = F(l(Y))` is a polynomial identity in the
18 entries, checked by full expansion (verifier [1]).  That a factorization
through the `l_t` exists is forced: `Det` is invariant under
`Y_t -> Y_t g_t` for `g_t in SL_2` (it has weight `det(g_t)^2`), and the
`SL_2`-invariants of a `3 x 2` matrix are generated by its `2 x 2` minors.
The three expressions for `F` and the special values are polynomial
identities (verifier [1]).

(a) Symmetry holds because `perm` is symmetric.  Irreducibility: over an
algebraically closed field every factor of the tri-homogeneous `F` is
tri-homogeneous.  If the degrees in `l_0` split `1 + 1`, then for every
fixed `(l_1, l_2)` the quadratic form `F(., l_1, l_2)` is a product of two
linear forms, so its Hessian has rank `<= 2`; at
`(l_1, l_2) = ((2,-3,5), (7,1,-4))` it has rank 3.  If they split `2 + 0`,
a nonconstant factor `H(l_1, l_2)` divides every entry of the `l_0`-Hessian
of `F`; their gcd over `Q` is the constant `2`, and a gcd does not change
under field extension.  By symmetry the same holds for `l_1` and `l_2`.

(b) `adj(B_v) = v v^T - 2 diag(v)^2` by direct computation;
`det(Y_1^T B Y_2) = l_1^T adj(B) l_2` is Cauchy–Binet for `3 x 2` matrices.
The discriminant of a binary quadratic is the bordered determinant of the
ambient form restricted to `l_0^perp`.  All three are replayed by verifier [2].

(d) Every product `P_s P_s'` with `s != s'` contains a two-equal factor
(two positions share a coordinate, and the third position has a
different one), so `F = sum_s P_s^2` modulo two-equal monomials.  For
`s != s'` of equal parity, `P_s^2 = P_s (P_s - P_s') + P_s P_s'`.  Both
`P_s - P_s'` and the two-equal monomials lie in `J_3` (Theorem 2).  ∎

**Answer to the factorization question.**  The hyperdeterminant does factor
through the Plücker coordinates (necessarily), as the explicit tridegree
`(2, 2, 2)` form `F`.  It does not factor further.  It is a quadratic form
in the six permutation monomials only: no all-equal and no two-equal
monomial occurs.  Its zero set is a hypersurface: the cube is degenerate.
Identical vanishing of the cube, which is what rigidity is about, is the
linear condition `l_0 (x) l_1 (x) l_2 in S`, with `dim S = 5`
(Theorem 2, Corollary 3).  It is not a condition on `Det`.

## Theorem 2 (the degree-three Plücker part of the cube ideal)

**(a) Polarization identity** (over `Z`).  For every row triple
`r = (r_0, r_1, r_2) in {1,2,3}^3`,

```text
sum_{i,j,k} (-1)^{i+j+k} Y_0[r_0, 1-i] Y_1[r_1, 1-j] Y_2[r_2, 1-k] A_ijk
      = - perm(l_0 x e_{r_0}, l_1 x e_{r_1}, l_2 x e_{r_2}) =: - Phi_r(l).
```

Each multiplier is a product of three entries, one per inner vertex.
`Phi_r = 0` for `r = (a, a, a)`.  For two-equal `r` it is a sum of two
two-equal monomials.  For `r` a permutation it is a difference of two
permutation monomials of the same parity; for example,
`Phi_(1,2,3) = P_(3,1,2) - P_(2,3,1)`.

**(b) Span.**  Over a field of characteristic not two, the 27 forms `Phi_r`
span a 22-dimensional space `J_3` of trilinear forms in `(l_0, l_1, l_2)`,
and `J_3 = Ann(S)`.  Explicitly, `J_3` consists of the forms
`f = sum c_abc l_0[a] l_1[b] l_2[c]` with

```text
c_aaa = 0 (a = 1, 2, 3),    sum_{s even} c_s = 0,    sum_{s odd} c_s = 0.
```

It therefore contains all 18 two-equal monomials, which is Proposition 4 of
the bilinear-grid document.  It also contains the four-dimensional space of
same-parity differences `P_s - P_s'`, which is new here.  In characteristic
two the span has dimension 16.

**(c) Completeness.**  Over `Q`, `J_3` is the whole intersection of `I`
with the trilinear Plücker forms in multidegree `(1, ..., 1)` (degree one in
each of the six columns).  So every degree-three certificate whose target is
a Plücker polynomial is a combination of the identities in (a).

*Proof.*

- (a) By trilinearity, the left side is `perm(u_0, u_1, u_2)` with
  `u_t = sum_i (-1)^i Y_t[r_t, 1-i] Y_t[:, i]`.  Coordinate `p` of `u_t` is
  `Y_t[r_t,1] Y_t[p,0] - Y_t[r_t,0] Y_t[p,1]`, which is
  `-(l_t x e_{r_t})[p]`.  The descriptions of `Phi_r` follow from
  `(l x e_r)[p] = 0` for `p = r` and `+- l[q]` otherwise, where
  `q = {1,2,3} - {r, p}`.  All 27 identities are replayed (verifier [3]).
- (b) The `27 x 27` integer coefficient matrix of the `Phi_r` has Smith
  invariants 16 ones, 6 twos and 5 zeros (verifier [4]).  Its rank is
  therefore 22 over every field of characteristic not two, and 16 in
  characteristic two.  The five spanning vectors of `S` are annihilated by
  every `Phi_r`, and they have disjoint supports, so `S` is the whole
  kernel.  Hence `J_3 = Ann(S)`.  The coordinate description is `Ann(S)`
  written out.
- (c) Exact integer ranks (verifier [5]).  The 216 products of an `A_ijk`
  with a trilinear monomial in the complementary columns span a space of
  rank 208.  Adjoining the 27 Plücker monomials raises the rank to 213.  So
  the intersection has dimension `208 + 27 - 213 = 22`.  The same holds over
  `F_3`; over `F_2` the intersection has dimension 17, and the
  `Phi_r` span only 16 of it.  A hand reason in characteristic zero:
  `I_(1^6)` is `SL_2^3`-stable, and the Reynolds operator maps each product
  `m A_ijk` to a combination of the alternating contractions in (a).  The
  verifier checks only the exact ranks.  ∎

## Corollary 3 (basis-free plane rigidity with a degree-three proof)

Let `F` have characteristic not two.  If the eight cube values vanish, then
`l_0 (x) l_1 (x) l_2 in S`.  Consequently, either some `l_t = 0` (rank of
`Y_t` at most one), or there is a coordinate `u` with
`l_0, l_1, l_2 in F e_u`.  In particular, for planes:

```text
perm vanishes on V_0 x V_1 x V_2   <=>   V_0 = V_1 = V_2 = H_u for one u
                                    <=>   l_0, l_1, l_2 lie on one coordinate axis.
```

Set-theoretically, `J_3` cuts out the Plücker image of the zero set of `I`:
`{some l_t = 0} ∪ ⋃_u (F e_u)^3`.

*Proof.*  Every `Phi_r` lies in `I` by Theorem 2 (a), so it vanishes, and
`X = l_0 (x) l_1 (x) l_2` lies in `Ann(J_3) = S`.  Write
`X = sum_a alpha_a e_a^{(x)3} + beta eps_+ + gamma eps_-`.  Every two-equal
coordinate of `X` is zero.  Suppose all `l_t != 0`.

- If `beta != 0`, then `l_0[1] l_1[2] l_2[3] = beta` and
  `l_0[2] l_1[3] l_2[1] = beta`, so `l_0[1], l_1[2], l_2[1]` are nonzero.
  Then the two-equal coordinate `l_0[1] l_1[2] l_2[1]` is nonzero, a
  contradiction.  Likewise `gamma = 0`.
- So `X = sum_a alpha_a e_a^{(x)3}`, and `X != 0`.  If `alpha_a` and
  `alpha_b` are nonzero with `a != b`, then `l_0[a] l_1[a] l_2[b] != 0`, a
  two-equal coordinate.  So `X = alpha_a e_a^{(x)3}`, and every `l_t` is a
  multiple of `e_a`.

Conversely, `Y_t = 0` gives `l_t = 0` with arbitrary `l_s`, `s != t`, and a
zero cube.  The `H_u` triple is isotropic because every term of `perm` uses
coordinate `u` of some argument.  Over `F_3` and `F_5` the rank-one tensors
in `S` are also enumerated by brute force (verifier [6]).  ∎

This is Theorem 3 of the bilinear-grid document.  The new point is that
every step is a degree-three certificate.  The rank-one argument then needs
no Nullstellensatz and no case analysis on full vectors.  The review's
counterexample to the support-level paraphrase of its Corollary 5 (ii)
(proportional full rows) is exactly the escape `l_t = 0`.

## Corollary 4 (Nullstellensatz degrees)

- **(i) Lower bound, and why 3.**  Every element of `I` has degree at least
  one in each `Y_t`.  A nonzero `SL_2`-invariant of positive degree in
  `Y_t` has degree at least two in it, one Plücker coordinate.  So every
  nonzero Plücker polynomial in `I` has degree at least 6.  Every
  certificate `sum g_ijk A_ijk` for it has multipliers of degree at least 3,
  and a target of Plücker tridegree `(1, 1, 1)` needs multipliers of degree
  exactly 3 (project onto the target's multidegree).  The degree is three
  because vanishing of the cube is a **linear** condition on
  `l_0 (x) l_1 (x) l_2`, that is, on one Plücker coordinate per inner
  vertex.
- **(ii) One coordinate per vertex.**  For `mu = l_0[a] l_1[b] l_2[c]`:
  - two-equal: `mu in I`, with multiplier degree 3 (Proposition 4);
  - permutation monomial `P_s`: `P_s` is not in `I`, but `P_s - P_s'` is in
    `I` for `s'` of the same parity (degree 3), and `P_s^2` is in `I`
    (multiplier degree 9), by the decomposition in the proof of
    Theorem 1 (d).  So the Nullstellensatz exponent of `P_s` is exactly 2;
  - all-equal: `mu` is not in the radical of `I`, because all planes equal
    to `H_a` give a zero cube with `mu != 0`.

  (verifier [7])
- **(iii) Support level.**  Suppose that on a support the three minors of
  `mu` are single nonzero monomials.  An element of `J_3` whose
  monomials other than `mu` all vanish identically on the support exists
  exactly when `mu` is two-equal, or `mu = P_s` and some same-parity
  `P_s'` (`s' != s`) has an identically zero minor.  This is linear
  algebra in `Ann(S)`: `e_mu` must not lie in the span of `S` restricted
  to `mu` and the vanishing monomials.  Certificates whose other terms
  cancel in a more complicated way are not classified here.  In the
  remaining permutation-monomial case, the value-level statement
  (Corollary 3, exponent 2) still applies.
