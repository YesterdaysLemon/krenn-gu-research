# Maximal-root surplus-two pointwise selector failure and decomposable-retraction boundary

## Status

**Proved exact characteristic-zero reduction and sharp abstract boundary.**
This note separates three algebraic questions that have different
quantifiers and therefore cannot be represented by one undifferentiated
"failure ideal."

1. For a nuisance matrix \(A\) and desired column \(g\) depending on Laurent
   torus variables, failure of the desired-column separator at **every
   geometric torus point** is equivalent to equality of the radicals of
   every determinantal (equivalently, relevant Fitting) ideal of \(A\) and
   \([A\mid g]\). With a response-coordinate ideal \(\mathcal R\), absence
   of a fiber having both nonzero response and desired survival is exactly
   \(\mathcal R I_j([A\mid g])\subset
   \sqrt[\mathrm{geom}]{I_j(A)}\) for every \(j\).
   On a complete fixed-\(Q\) witness equation this response-gated profile is
   equivalently the pure-column profile \([A\mid D]\), and one shared-variable
   incidence ideal encodes common attachment for any finite target family.
2. GLS2 fixed-\(Q\) supply failure is instead a statement over the outside
   **function field**. It is exactly a projected-kernel condition, or a
   finite union of coefficientwise determinantal rank strata. A constant
   kernel vector or a kernel at one sampled contraction is not an equivalent
   encoding.
3. Even an injective companion sensor gives only an unrestricted recovery
   \(R\Gamma=P\). A legal constant target selector has the much narrower
   form \(R=\lambda\otimes\operatorname{id}\). A canonical quotient class
   measures the difference.

Two exact modules over \(\mathbb Q\) show that these distinctions are
load-bearing. In particular, an injective sensor, maximal nuisance-slice
rank, a nonzero three-colour pure diagonal target, and a nonzero desired
response can coexist with complete legal-selector failure. These modules
are **not physical graphs, hypothetical witnesses, or counterexamples to any
Krenn--Gu statement**. They prove that the missing bridge must use a
physical companion/hafnian identity or the complete mixed target equations;
abstract rank and pure-target data alone do not provide it.

The statements below are algebraic over any characteristic-zero field.
The geometric point statements are taken after extension to an algebraic
closure. Every localization and every declared nonzero factor is displayed;
no exceptional factor is divided out silently. This theorem does not
close target attachment, permanent extraction, or the global conjecture.
The global Krenn--Gu conjecture remains **UNRESOLVED**.

## 1. Pointwise absorption on a Laurent torus

Let \(K\) be a characteristic-zero field, let \(\overline K\) be an algebraic
closure, and put

~~~
R=K[t_1^(+/-1),...,t_d^(+/-1)].                       (1)
~~~

Let \(h_1,\ldots,h_s\in R\) be all factors declared nonzero on the chart,
put \(h=h_1\cdots h_s\), and work in \(R_h\). Thus the geometric chart is

~~~
T_h={xi in (overline K^*)^d:h(xi)!=0}.                (2)
~~~

Take

~~~
A in Mat_(m x n)(R_h),       g in R_h^m,
B=[A | g] in Mat_(m x (n+1))(R_h).                    (3)
~~~

In the selector application, the columns of \(A(\xi)\) span all nuisance
coefficient slices and \(g(\xi)\) is the desired coefficient. Finite-dimensional
linear duality says that a legal separating functional at \(\xi\) exists exactly
when \(g(\xi)\notin\operatorname{im}A(\xi)\). Hence **pointwise absorption**
means

~~~
g(xi) in im A(xi) for every xi in T_h.                (4)
~~~

For a matrix \(M\), let \(I_j(M)\) be the ideal generated by its
\(j\times j\) minors, with \(I_0(M)=R_h\) and \(I_j(M)=0\) when \(j\)
exceeds the matrix dimensions. Write \(\sqrt[\mathrm{geom}]{I}\) for the
contraction to \(R_h\) of
\(\sqrt{I(R_h\otimes_K\overline K)}\).

### Theorem 1 (radical--Fitting criterion for universal pointwise absorption)

The following are equivalent.

1. The desired column is absorbed at every geometric point, as in (4).
2. For every \(\xi\in T_h\),

   ~~~
   rank B(xi)=rank A(xi).                              (5)
   ~~~

3. For every \(1\le j\le m\),

   ~~~
   sqrt_geom(I_j(A))=sqrt_geom(I_j(B)) in R_h.         (6)
   ~~~

Equivalently, if \(A\) and \(B\) are presentations with \(m\) rows, the
geometric radicals of all relevant Fitting ideals of
\(\operatorname{coker}A\) and \(\operatorname{coker}B\) agree, since

~~~
Fitt_(m-j)(coker M)=I_j(M).                            (7)
~~~

If the entries are given in the unlocalized Laurent ring, (6) is
equivalently

~~~
sqrt_geom(I_j(A):h^infinity)
  =sqrt_geom(I_j(B):h^infinity)                       (8)
~~~

for every \(j\). If entries have denominators supported on the declared
factors, (6) is interpreted in \(R_h\); clearing them changes the minors only
by units on \(T_h\).

### Proof

Over the residue field at \(\xi\), \(g(\xi)\) belongs to the column span of
\(A(\xi)\) exactly when adjoining it does not increase rank. This proves
1--2.

For any \(j\),

~~~
xi in V(I_j(A))  iff rank A(xi)<j,
xi in V(I_j(B))  iff rank B(xi)<j.                    (9)
~~~

Thus (5) at every \(\xi\) is equivalent to equality of these two geometric
zero sets for every \(j\). The Laurent Nullstellensatz on \(T_h\) identifies
equality of geometric zero sets with equality of the geometric radicals in
(6). Conversely, (6) and (9) give the same rank threshold for every \(j\),
so the two ranks agree pointwise. Localization at \(h\) contracts to
saturation by \(h\), giving (8). No value of \(h\) is inverted outside the
declared chart. \(\square\)

### Corollary 1.1 (response-gated survival criterion)

Retain Theorem 1 and let

~~~
p=(rho_1,...,rho_q) in R_h^q,
Rho=(rho_1,...,rho_q) subset R_h                     (9a)
~~~

be the coordinate vector and coordinate ideal of the desired physical
response on the same contraction chart. Changing the basis of the response
space does not change \(\mathcal R\). The following are equivalent.

1. There is no \(\xi\in T_h\) at which both

   ~~~
   p(xi)!=0              and
   g(xi) notin im A(xi).                              (9b)
   ~~~

2. For every \(1\le j\le m\),

   ~~~
   Rho I_j(B) subset sqrt_geom(I_j(A)) in R_h.        (9c)
   ~~~

In the unlocalized ring, (9c) means the corresponding containment after
saturation by every declared factor in \(h\).

### Proof

Suppose first that (9b) never occurs. Fix \(j\), a response coordinate
\(\rho_\ell\), and a \(j\times j\) minor \(\Delta\) of \(B\). At any
\(\xi\in V(I_j(A))\), either \(\Delta(\xi)=0\), or

~~~
rank A(xi)<j<=rank B(xi).                             (9d)
~~~

Adjoining one column raises rank by at most one, so the second alternative
forces

~~~
rank A(xi)=j-1,       rank B(xi)=j.                   (9e)
~~~

Thus the desired column survives at \(\xi\), and the hypothesis forces
\(p(\xi)=0\), in particular \(\rho_\ell(\xi)=0\). Hence
\(\rho_\ell\Delta\) vanishes on \(V(I_j(A))\). The geometric Laurent
Nullstellensatz puts every such product in
\(\sqrt[\mathrm{geom}]{I_j(A)}\), proving (9c).

Conversely, suppose (9c) holds and let \(a=\operatorname{rank}A(\xi)\). If
the desired column survives at \(\xi\), then
\(\operatorname{rank}B(\xi)=a+1\). Choose a nonzero
\((a+1)\times(a+1)\) minor \(\Delta\) of \(B(\xi)\). Every
\((a+1)\)-minor of \(A(\xi)\) vanishes. Containment (9c) with \(j=a+1\)
therefore gives

~~~
rho_l(xi) Delta(xi)=0
~~~

for every response coordinate \(\rho_\ell\). Since \(\Delta(\xi)\ne0\),
all response coordinates vanish. Thus \(p(\xi)=0\), excluding (9b).
\(\square\)

This corollary concerns the actual response vector \(p\) and desired
coefficient \(g\).  On the complete physical witness equation the GLD7
quotient identity permits a stronger pure-column formulation.

### Corollary 1.2 (physical pure-profile and common-attachment incidence)

Fix one physical graph, one residual pair \(Q\), and one usable fixed-\(Q\)
target \(S\) in the GLD5/GLD7 module.  Assume the **complete** uncontracted
GHZ equation, and let the fully supported residual contraction vary on its
Laurent torus.  In fixed bases, let

~~~
A_S(z)  present the complete nuisance space N_S(z),
D_S=[d_(S,0)|d_(S,1)|d_(S,2)].                         (9f)
~~~

At every geometric torus point \(z\), the following are equivalent.

1. The target has a legal constant GLD5 selector and its physical response
   \(P_S(H;z)\) is nonzero.
2. At least one pure target column survives the nuisance space.
3. One has

   ~~~
   rank[A_S(z)|D_S]>rank A_S(z).                       (9g)
   ~~~

Consequently there is no attached fibre anywhere on the declared chart if
and only if, for every \(1\le j\le\dim L_S^*\),

~~~
sqrt_geom(I_j(A_S))=sqrt_geom(I_j([A_S|D_S])).         (9h)
~~~

This equality includes both response-zero fibres and swallowed-pure fibres;
it makes no generic-rank assumption.  For the four-root module the six pair
targets have \(729\)-row presentations and the four-port target has an
\(81\)-row presentation.

For a finite target family \(\mathcal F\), common attachment at one residual
contraction has a single exact incidence formulation.  Introduce, for every
\(S\in\mathcal F\), a row vector \(y_S\) and three auxiliary scalars
\(u_{S,c}\), and form the ideal

~~~
J_attach=<
  y_S^T A_S(z),
  1-sum_(c=0)^2 u_(S,c) y_S^T d_(S,c)
  : S in F
>                                                       (9i)
~~~

in the polynomial extension
\(R_h[\{y_S\},\{u_{S,c}\}]\) of the localized Laurent residual ring.  The
geometric zero set of \(J_{\mathrm{attach}}\) is nonempty
exactly when one fully supported contraction attaches every target in
\(\mathcal F\).  In particular, the seven-target four-root common-attachment
question is one shared-\(z\) incidence problem, not seven independently
sampled contractions.  For a fixed graph, absence of a common attached fibre
is exactly the unit-ideal condition

~~~
J_attach=<1>.                                          (9j)
~~~

If graph parameters are left symbolic, (9j) is a fibrewise condition; its
parameter locus must be obtained by a proved constructible projection or an
equivalent module identity.  It is not legitimate to replace it by one
generic specialization.

### Proof

At a fully supported residual contraction, GLD7 gives

~~~
sum_(c=0)^2 alpha_c[d_(S,c)] tensor w_(S,c)
  =[g_S] tensor P_S(H;z),                              (9k)
~~~

where every \(\alpha_c\) is a Laurent unit and the three \(w_{S,c}\) are
independent.  The complete target equation also forces the span of the three
classes \([d_{S,c}]\) to have dimension at most one.  Thus (9g) holds exactly
when that span has dimension one.  In this case the left side of (9k) is
nonzero, so both factors on the right are nonzero; GLD5 turns
\([g_S]\ne0\) into the legal constant selector.  Conversely, if both right
factors are nonzero, (9k) is nonzero and at least one pure class survives.
This proves the pointwise equivalences.  The same rank-threshold and Laurent
Nullstellensatz argument as Theorem 1, now with three augmented columns,
proves (9h).

For (9i), a pure class survives exactly when finite-dimensional separation
supplies a row \(y_S\) which kills every nuisance column but is nonzero on at
least one \(d_{S,c}\).  Such a row exists exactly when auxiliary scalars can
normalize the displayed linear combination to one.  Sharing the same
Laurent variables \(z\) across all \(S\) enforces one common contraction;
the selector and normalization variables may differ because the target
output spaces differ.  The geometric Nullstellensatz gives (9j).  No
response coordinate is divided out: its nonvanishing follows from (9k) on
the physical witness locus. \(\square\)

### Two strictness examples

Theorem 1 is a fiberwise statement, not module membership. In
\(K[s^{\pm1}]\), let

~~~
A=[(s-1)^2],       g=s-1.                             (10)
~~~

At \(s=1\) both evaluated columns are zero, while away from \(s=1\) the
column of \(A\) spans the one-dimensional fiber. Thus (4) holds and the
two first-minor ideals have the same radical, but
\(g\notin\operatorname{im}A\) as an \(R\)-module element.

Conversely,

~~~
A=[s-1],           g=1                                (11)
~~~

has \(g\in\operatorname{im}A\) over the function field \(K(s)\), but
absorption fails at \(s=1\). Its two first-minor radicals are respectively
\((s-1)\) and the unit ideal. Generic membership is therefore too weak for
universal pointwise selector failure, just as polynomial module membership
is too strong.

## 2. The exact GLS2 function-field failure locus

The quantifier in GLS2 is different. First work over an arbitrary field
\(F\). Split a companion sensor into nuisance and desired columns:

~~~
Gamma=[Gamma_N Gamma_C]:F^n_N direct-sum F^n_C -> F^m. (12)
~~~

Let \(\pi_C\) be projection of the domain to \(F^{n_C}\), and let

~~~
bar_Gamma_C:F^n_C -> coker Gamma_N                   (13)
~~~

be the map induced by \(\Gamma_C\).

### Theorem 2 (projected-kernel formula)

One has

~~~
pi_C(ker Gamma)=ker bar_Gamma_C,                      (14)

dim_F pi_C(ker Gamma)
  =n_C-(rank Gamma-rank Gamma_N).                     (15)
~~~

Consequently the complete desired coordinate family is observable exactly
when

~~~
rank Gamma-rank Gamma_N=n_C,                          (16)
~~~

and supply failure is exactly

~~~
rank Gamma-rank Gamma_N<=n_C-1.                       (17)
~~~

For GLS2 fixed-\(Q\) observability, \(F=K(X)\), the desired columns are
\(C_Q\), the nuisance columns are \(N_Q\), and \(n_C=|C_Q|=2^r-1\). The
same formula applies to the all-pair block by taking \(C=P_2\), or to any
other explicitly declared desired label family. Failure in (17) means only
that the unrestricted linear sensor does not determine that family; it does
not construct a second regular hafnian deck or a second physical graph.

### Proof

A vector \(c\in F^{n_C}\) lies in the projection of \(\ker\Gamma\) exactly
when there is \(n\in F^{n_N}\) with

~~~
Gamma_N n+Gamma_C c=0.                                (18)
~~~

This is exactly the condition that \(\Gamma_Cc\) vanish in
\(\operatorname{coker}\Gamma_N\), proving (14). The image of
\(\bar\Gamma_C\) is

~~~
(im Gamma_N+im Gamma_C)/im Gamma_N,
~~~

so its rank is \(\operatorname{rank}\Gamma-\operatorname{rank}\Gamma_N\).
Rank--nullity gives (15), and (16)--(17) follow. \(\square\)

### Coefficientwise determinantal strata

Here is the exact finite algebraic encoding when the sensor depends on
outside variables. Let \(P\) be a finitely generated characteristic-zero
\(K\)-algebra of graph parameters on a declared localization chart, put

~~~
S=P[z_1^(+/-1),...,z_d^(+/-1)],                       (19)
~~~

and suppose the entries of \(\Gamma_N,\Gamma_C\) lie in \(S\). Declared
denominators may be cleared only after localizing at their explicitly listed
nonzero factors. For a matrix \(M\), define the **coefficient ideal**

~~~
Coeff_j(M) subset P                                  (20)
~~~

to be generated by every Laurent coefficient in the \(z\)'s of every
\(j\times j\) minor of \(M\). Use
\(\operatorname{Coeff}_0(M)=P\). At a geometric graph-parameter point
\(y\),

~~~
rank_(overline K(z)) M(y;z)<=a
  iff y in V(Coeff_(a+1)(M)).                         (21)
~~~

Therefore the exact locus on which

~~~
rank Gamma_N=a,       rank Gamma=b                    (22)
~~~

is the locally closed stratum

~~~
Sigma_(a,b)=
  V(Coeff_(a+1)(Gamma_N)+Coeff_(b+1)(Gamma))
  intersect D(Coeff_a(Gamma_N))
  intersect D(Coeff_b(Gamma)).                        (23)
~~~

Here \(D(J)=\operatorname{Spec}P\setminus V(J)\), so
\(D(\operatorname{Coeff}_a)\) means that at least one coefficient of at
least one \(a\times a\) minor is nonzero. It is a union of principal opens;
no particular pivot has been silently selected.

Combining (17) and (23), the exact fixed-\(Q\) supply-failure locus is

~~~
F_Q = union Sigma_(a,b),                              (24)
~~~

where the finite union ranges over all feasible \(a,b\) satisfying

~~~
0<=a<=min(m,n_N),
a<=b<=min(m,n_N+n_C),
b-a<=n_C-1.                                          (25)
~~~

Simultaneous failure for every residual pair is the intersection

~~~
intersection_Q F_Q,                                  (26)
~~~

not one chosen rank stratum and not the vanishing locus of a sampled
contraction. To intersect (24) with the complete mixed GHZ equations, add
their coefficient ideal on every locally closed stratum and retain all chart
saturations. An emptiness claim requires an exact unit/radical certificate
on every surviving stratum or a proved identity covering their union.

Equation (18) also gives a kernel-witness encoding, but \(n\) and \(c\) live
in the function field. On the finite cover \(c_i\ne0\), one may normalize
\(c_i=1\); every denominator introduced in expressing the other coordinates
must then be declared and saturated. There is no equivalent constant-vector
ansatz in general. For example,

~~~
Gamma=[1 t],       Gamma_N=[1],       Gamma_C=[t]     (27)
~~~

over \(K(t)\) has projected kernel generated by \((-t,1)\), so the desired
column fails observability, but it has no nonzero kernel vector in \(K^2\).
Likewise, one numerical contraction says nothing about the function-field
rank unless a separate specialization theorem supplies that implication.

## 3. Unrestricted recovery versus a legal decomposable selector

Let \(E,L,W\) be finite-dimensional vector spaces over a field \(K\), and
let

~~~
Gamma:E -> L tensor W,       P:E -> W.                (28)
~~~

Define

~~~
H=Hom(L tensor W,W),
rho_Gamma:H -> Hom(E,W),       T |-> T Gamma,
Ann(Gamma)=ker rho_Gamma,                               (29)

D_W={lambda tensor id_W:lambda in L^*} subset H.      (30)
~~~

The subspace \(D_W\) is intrinsic to the displayed tensor factorization: no
bases of \(L\) or \(W\) enter its definition. Call \(R\in H\) an
**unrestricted recovery** when \(R\Gamma=P\). Call
\(\lambda\otimes\operatorname{id}_W\) a **legal decomposable selector** when

~~~
(lambda tensor id_W)Gamma=P.                          (31)
~~~

### Theorem 3 (decomposable-retraction obstruction class)

The following hold.

1. An unrestricted recovery exists exactly when

   ~~~
   ker Gamma subset ker P.                            (32)
   ~~~

2. Suppose (32) holds and choose any \(R_0\in H\) with
   \(R_0\Gamma=P\). The class

   ~~~
   delta(Gamma,P)=[R_0]
     in H/(D_W+Ann(Gamma))                            (33)
   ~~~

   is independent of the choice of \(R_0\).
3. A legal decomposable selector exists exactly when

   ~~~
   delta(Gamma,P)=0.                                  (34)
   ~~~

Equivalently, precomposition induces the canonical identification

~~~
H/(D_W+Ann(Gamma))
  isomorphic to im(rho_Gamma)/rho_Gamma(D_W),          (35)
~~~

under which \(\delta(\Gamma,P)\) is the class of \(P\).

### Proof

If \(R\Gamma=P\), then \(P\) vanishes on \(\ker\Gamma\). Conversely, under
(32), the rule \(\Gamma x\mapsto Px\) is a well-defined linear map on
\(\operatorname{im}\Gamma\); extend it linearly to \(L\otimes W\). This
proves item 1.

Any two recoveries differ by an element of \(\operatorname{Ann}(\Gamma)\),
so (33) is well-defined. Its vanishing means
\(R_0=d+a\) for some \(d\in D_W\) and
\(a\in\operatorname{Ann}(\Gamma)\). Then
\(P=R_0\Gamma=d\Gamma\), so \(d\) is legal. The converse is immediate by
subtracting a legal \(d\) from \(R_0\). The first isomorphism theorem applied
to \(\rho_\Gamma\) gives (35). \(\square\)

In particular, if \(\Gamma\) is injective, every \(P\) has an unrestricted
recovery, but \(\delta(\Gamma,P)\) can still be nonzero.

### Nuisance-quotient specialization

Suppose \(E=E_{\mathrm{des}}\oplus E_{\mathrm{nuis}}\), \(P\) vanishes on
\(E_{\mathrm{nuis}}\), and

~~~
Gamma=g tensor P+Theta,
Theta|_(E_des)=0.                                    (36)
~~~

Let

~~~
N=span{(id_L tensor eta)Theta(x):
       x in E_nuis, eta in W^*} subset L.             (37)
~~~

Assume \(P|_{E_{\mathrm{des}}}\ne0\). Then (31) holds exactly when

~~~
lambda(g)=1,       lambda|_N=0,                       (38)
~~~

and therefore exactly when

~~~
[g]!=0 in L/N.                                       (39)
~~~

Indeed, on \(E_{\mathrm{des}}\), equation (31) is
\((\lambda(g)-1)P=0\), which is equivalent to
\(\lambda(g)=1\) because \(P|_{E_{\mathrm{des}}}\ne0\). On
\(E_{\mathrm{nuis}}\), it is
\((\lambda\otimes\operatorname{id})\Theta=0\), which is equivalent to
\(\lambda\) killing every slice in (37). A functional with (38) exists
exactly when \(g\notin N\), by finite-dimensional separation and
normalization.

This is the coordinate-free form of the GLD5 quotient criterion. Notice
that injectivity of \(\Gamma\) concerns \(\ker\Gamma\), whereas (39) concerns
the coefficient-slice image (37). Neither condition implies the other.

## 4. Exact abstract countermodels over the rationals

### Theorem 4 (injective maximal-rank swallowed-pure model)

There is an exact module over \(\mathbb Q\) with all of the following
properties:

- \(\Gamma\) is injective, hence every source coordinate has an unrestricted
  recovery;
- the nuisance coefficient-slice map is surjective, so its rank is maximal;
- one source point maps to a nonzero three-term pure diagonal target and has
  nonzero desired response; and
- no legal decomposable selector exists.

### Construction and proof

Let \(L=W=\mathbb Q^3\), with bases \(a_0,a_1,a_2\) and
\(w_0,w_1,w_2\). Let \(E\) have basis

~~~
d_0,d_1,d_2,n_1,n_2,n_3,                             (40)
~~~

and define \(P:E\to W\) and \(\Gamma:E\to L\otimes W\) by

~~~
P(d_i)=w_i,       P(n_j)=0,

Gamma(d_0)=a_0 tensor w_0,
Gamma(d_1)=a_0 tensor w_1,
Gamma(d_2)=a_0 tensor w_2,
Gamma(n_1)=a_1 tensor w_1,
Gamma(n_2)=a_2 tensor w_2,
Gamma(n_3)=a_0 tensor w_0+a_1 tensor w_2.             (41)
~~~

Take \(g=a_0\) and \(\Theta=\Gamma-g\otimes P\). Then \(\Theta\) vanishes
on the desired span of the \(d_i\). The coefficient-slice map

~~~
beta:E_nuis tensor W^* -> L,
beta(n tensor eta)=(id_L tensor eta)Theta(n)           (42)
~~~

has \(a_0,a_1,a_2\) in its image: contract respectively
\(\Theta(n_3)\) by \(w_0^*\), \(\Theta(n_1)\) by \(w_1^*\), and
\(\Theta(n_2)\) by \(w_2^*\). Hence \(\beta\) is surjective and \(N=L\).
In particular \(g\in N\), so (39) fails.

The six tensors in (41) are independent. Indeed the \(a_1\otimes w_2\)
coefficient first kills the coefficient of \(n_3\), the
\(a_1\otimes w_1\) and \(a_2\otimes w_2\) coefficients then kill those of
\(n_1,n_2\), and the \(a_0\otimes w_i\) coefficients kill the remaining
three. Thus \(\Gamma\) is injective.

Now put

~~~
H=d_0+n_1+n_2.                                       (43)
~~~

Then

~~~
Gamma(H)=a_0 tensor w_0+a_1 tensor w_1+a_2 tensor w_2,
P(H)=w_0!=0.                                         (44)
~~~

The first tensor is a three-colour pure diagonal target. If a legal
\(\lambda\) existed, the three desired basis vectors would force
\(\lambda(a_0)=1\). Killing \(n_3\) would then require

~~~
w_0+lambda(a_1)w_2=0,                                (45)
~~~

which is impossible. Thus \(\delta(\Gamma,P)\ne0\).

For completeness, an unrestricted recovery can be displayed explicitly:

~~~
R(a_0 tensor w_i)=w_i       (i=0,1,2),
R(a_1 tensor w_1)=0,
R(a_2 tensor w_2)=0,
R(a_1 tensor w_2)=-w_0,                              (46)
~~~

with \(R\) zero on the remaining tensor-basis vectors. Equations (41) and
(46) give \(R\Gamma=P\), but \(R\) is not
\(\lambda\otimes\operatorname{id}_W\). \(\square\)

This example lies on the **maximal**, not rank-drop, nuisance branch. Thus
selector failure cannot in general be localized to the rank-drop locus of
\(\beta\). Any theorem doing so for physical Krenn--Gu companions must use
additional physical equations.

### Theorem 5 (a legal target selector with separate supply failure)

A legal selector for one desired response can coexist with GLS2-type
projected-kernel failure for a different deck-coordinate family.

### Construction and proof

Let \(L=W=\mathbb Q^2\), with bases \(a_0,a_1\) and \(w_0,w_1\), and let
\(E\) have basis \(d_0,d_1,u,v\). Define

~~~
P(d_i)=w_i,       P(u)=P(v)=0,

Gamma(d_i)=a_0 tensor w_i       (i=0,1),
Gamma(u)=Gamma(v)=a_1 tensor w_0.                     (47)
~~~

Then \(\lambda=a_0^*\) satisfies
\((\lambda\otimes\operatorname{id})\Gamma=P\). In the nuisance quotient,
\(g=a_0\) and \(N=\operatorname{span}(a_1)\), so \([g]\ne0\).

For the supply split

~~~
C=span(u),       E_rest=span(d_0,d_1,v),              (48)
~~~

the induced map \(C\to\operatorname{coker}\Gamma|_{E_{\mathrm{rest}}}\)
is zero because \(\Gamma(u)=\Gamma(v)\). Equivalently,
\(u-v\in\ker\Gamma\) has nonzero \(C\)-projection. Hence that supply
coordinate is not observable. \(\square\)

There is no contradiction with Theorem 3: a legal selector for \(P\) always
implies \(\ker\Gamma\subset\ker P\), so failure for the **same** projection
\(P\) is impossible. GLS2 complete-deck observability and a GLD target
projection are different projections, and (47)--(48) show that their status
is logically independent without extra physical structure.

## 5. Exact proof-DAG consequence and remaining physical identity
