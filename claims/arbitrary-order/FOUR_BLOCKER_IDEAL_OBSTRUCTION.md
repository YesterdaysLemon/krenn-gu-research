# Four-blocker ideal obstruction

## Status

This is an arbitrary-order local theorem in the deeper-blocker branch.
It strengthens the three-colour blocker-union lower bound:

```text
every fully supported zero-coupled root pair has at least
five distinct outside blockers across the three colours.  (1)
```

The proof excludes all three formerly surviving four-vertex incidence
patterns.  It does not yet turn the growing blocker surplus into a
global contradiction.

## Root-pair ideal

Fix root vertices `p,r` and vectors `x,y in C^3` with

```text
B_pr(x,y) = 0,
x[d] y[d] != 0 for d=0,1,2.
```

For every outside vertex `u`, write

```text
a_u(z) = B_pu(x,z),
b_u(z) = B_ru(y,z),
S_u = span(a_u,b_u),
m_u(z) = (a_u(z),b_u(z))^T.
```

Let `D_d` be the vertices for which `e_d^* in S_u`, and suppose for a
contradiction that their union `B` has four vertices.  Put

```text
N = V minus ({p,r} union B),
K_v = ker(a_v) intersect ker(b_v)  for v in N.
```

No vertex of `N` blocks any colour.  Consequently the restriction of
every coordinate functional to every `K_v` is nonzero: otherwise
`K_v` would lie in a coordinate hyperplane and the corresponding
coordinate covector would belong to `S_v`.  Hence the three decomposable
tensors

```text
R_d = tensor_(v in N) (z_v[d] restricted to K_v)       (2)
```

are individually nonzero.

Restrict the vectors at `N` to their spaces `K_v`, while leaving the four
blocker vectors free.  The two roots cannot pair together and cannot
pair into `N`.  They must therefore pair with two distinct blockers.
The root-pair factor on blockers `i,j` is

```text
F_ij(z_i,z_j)
  = a_i(z_i)b_j(z_j) + b_i(z_i)a_j(z_j)
  = m_i(z_i)^T J m_j(z_j),                            (3)

J = [0 1; 1 0].
```

Thus the restricted matching tensor has the form

```text
sum_(i<j in B) F_ij K_ij,                              (4)
```

where each `K_ij` is some multilinear cofactor on the other blockers and
on `N`.

Choose a linear functional `phi` on the tensor space of the `N` modes
which is nonzero on all three tensors in (2).  Such a functional exists
because a finite union of proper hyperplanes cannot cover a complex
vector space.  Applying `phi` to the matching identity turns (4) into

```text
sum_(d=0)^2 lambda_d product_(i in B) z_i[d]
  = sum_(i<j in B) F_ij L_ij,                          (5)
```

with every `lambda_d != 0`.

The right side belongs to the multilinear ideal generated by the six
forms `F_ij`.  In particular, it vanishes at every common zero of those
six forms.  We now give such a common zero at which the left side is
nonzero for each possible four-blocker pattern.

## Excluding the three patterns

The tight blocker-pair span dichotomy leaves, up to a colour
permutation, exactly

```text
A: 0, 0, 12, 12
B: 0, 01, 12, 12
C: 01, 01, 02, 02.                                    (6)
```

A type such as `12` means that `S_u` contains `e_1^*,e_2^*`.
It is then exactly that coordinate plane, so `m_u(e_0)=0`.

### Pattern A

The two type-0 vertices are the exact colour-0 blocker pair.  The tight
span dichotomy forces at least one of them, say `u`, to have

```text
S_u = span(e_0^*).
```

Hence `m_u(e_1)=0`.  For the other type-0 vertex `v`, either its span is
also pure, or it has the form

```text
S_v = span(e_0^*, alpha e_1^* + beta e_2^*),
alpha beta != 0.
```

In either case there is a vector `k in ker(m_v)` with `k[1] != 0`.

The two type-12 vertices form an exact blocker pair for colours 1 and 2.
Their form `F_st`, restricted to the coordinate-12 planes, is a rank-two
diagonal:

```text
F_st(z,w) = delta_1 z[1]w[1] + delta_2 z[2]w[2],
delta_1 delta_2 != 0.                                  (7)
```

Choose `z,w` with all their coordinates 1 and 2 nonzero and with (7)
equal to zero.  At the four blocker vertices put

```text
z_u=e_1, z_v=k, z_s=z, z_t=w.
```

The first two `m`-vectors vanish and `F_st=0`, so all six `F_ij` vanish.
Only the colour-1 term on the left of (5) survives, and it is nonzero.
Contradiction.

### Pattern B

The type-0 vertex is pure by the tight dichotomy for the exact pair
`0,01`.  Put `e_2` at it and at the type-01 vertex; both `m`-vectors
vanish.  The type-12 pair again has the nondegenerate diagonal form (7).
Choose a zero of (7) with both colour-2 coordinates nonzero.  All six
generators vanish, while exactly the nonzero colour-2 term of (5)
survives.  Contradiction.

### Pattern C

The type-01 pair is exact for colour 1, so its root-pair form is

```text
delta_0 z[0]w[0] + delta_1 z[1]w[1],
delta_0 delta_1 != 0.                                  (8)
```

Put `e_1` at both type-02 vertices, making both of their `m`-vectors
zero.  Choose a zero of (8) at which both colour-1 coordinates are
nonzero.  Again every `F_ij` vanishes and only the nonzero colour-1 term
of (5) remains.  Contradiction.

All four-blocker patterns are impossible.  Together with the previous
lower bound of four, this proves (1).

```text
python claims/arbitrary-order/audit_four_blocker_ideal_obstruction.py
```

The primary verifier reconstructs the 12 labelled four-vertex patterns
and their three colour orbits, then checks every common-zero construction
symbolically.  The independent audit repeats the incidence census with
bit masks and exhausts the proof constructions over `F_5` for every
nonzero value of their essential parameters.  The written theorem is
over `C`; the finite-field audit is a separate formula and
case-coverage check.

## Boundary

The result converts the sharp-looking four-blocker boundary into a
strict surplus:

```text
rank-at-least-two root edge
  -> fully supported zero pair
  -> at least five outside blocker vertices.
```

The next task is to propagate this surplus between adjacent root edges
or to combine it with promotion of a nonblocker into a larger
zero-coupled root set.  The theorem alone is not a proof of the global
conjecture.
