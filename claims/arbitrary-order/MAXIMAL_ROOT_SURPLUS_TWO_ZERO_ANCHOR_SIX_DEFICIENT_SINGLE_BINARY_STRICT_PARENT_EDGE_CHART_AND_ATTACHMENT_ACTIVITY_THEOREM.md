## 2. Pure-`P_3` separated-probe edge lemma

### Lemma 2.1 (six charts, including endpoints)

Let three local joint shore spaces have rank two, with

```text
p_i in K[P] tensor V_i^*,
q_i in K[Q] tensor V_i^*,
h_i in V_i^*.                                         (7)
```

Write

```text
g_ij=p_i tensor q_j+q_i tensor p_j.
```

Suppose

```text
sum_({i,j} subset {0,1,2}) g_ij tensor h_({0,1,2}-{i,j})
 =kappa P_a Q_a e_a tensor e_a tensor e_a,
kappa!=0.                                               (8)
```

Then at least one of the following necessary alternatives holds.  The list
is exhaustive, but an endpoint can also satisfy the stronger common-form
description accidentally, so the alternatives are not asserted disjoint.

1. **Nonendpoint.**  There are nonzero forms `C(P),F(Q)` such that

   ```text
   p_i=C(P)u_i,        q_i=F(Q)v_i       for every i, (9)
   C(P) proportional P_a,
   F(Q) proportional Q_a,
   ```

   with fixed local vectors `u_i,v_i`.

2. **P-common endpoint.**  Every `p_i` has one common scalar form
   proportional to `P_a`, and one selected `q_j` has scalar form
   proportional to `Q_a`.  The other `q` columns need not share a form and
   their unselected scalar components may vanish.

3. **Q-common endpoint.**  Every `q_i` has one common scalar form
   proportional to `Q_a`, and one selected `p_j` has scalar form
   proportional to `P_a`.  The other `p` columns need not share a form and
   their unselected scalar components may vanish.

The selected mode and the endpoint type range over all six oriented-edge
charts.  No chart is omitted when one edge coefficient is zero.

### Proof

At each mode, the joint shore rank is two.  Neither `p_i` nor `q_i` can be
zero: a rank-at-least-two pure `P_3` restriction with a zero source column
would have a plane normal supported on one source coordinate, which is
excluded by the decomposable `P_3` classification.  If nonzero `p_i(P)` and
`q_i(Q)` were generically collinear, independence of `P,Q` would put the
coefficient images of both shores on one fixed output line, contradicting
joint rank two.  Thus they are independent over `K(P,Q)`.

The local map `L_i=[p_i,q_i,h_i]` has rank at least two.  The accepted
classification excludes rank three, so it has rank two and

```text
h_i=a_i p_i+b_i q_i.                                  (10)
```

Use `(p_i,q_i)` as the local binary basis, with `0=p` and `1=q`.  The
restricted `P_3` tensor has zero `000,111` coefficients and

```text
T_001=a_0+a_1,       T_010=a_0+a_2,       T_100=a_1+a_2,
T_110=b_0+b_1,       T_101=b_0+b_2,       T_011=b_1+b_2. (11)
```

A nonzero decomposable binary tensor with the two opposite corners zero is
supported on one of the six edges joining a weight-one vertex to a
weight-two vertex.  Mode permutations and the global exchange `p<->q`
act transitively, so take the representative edge `{100,101}`.  Its four
off-edge equations give scalars `R,S`, not both zero, with

```text
2h_0=-R p_0+S q_0,
2h_1= R p_1-S q_1,
2h_2= R p_2+S q_2,                                   (12)
```

and the source tensor is

```text
q_0 tensor p_1 tensor (R p_2+S q_2).                 (13)
```

The pure target makes

```text
q_0=D(Q)e_a,          p_1=A(P)e_a,
R p_2+S q_2=c e_a,                  c in K^times.    (14)
```

The last scalar is probe-independent because the left side is `2h_2`.

Assume first `RS!=0`.  Let `C(P)` be the nonzero transverse component of
`p_0` modulo `K e_a`, and let `F(Q)` be the nonzero transverse component of
`q_1`.  Taking transverse components in the first two equations of (12)
gives

```text
R C(P)=r in K^times,          S F(Q)=s in K^times.   (15)
```

Therefore `R=r/C(P)` belongs to `K(P)` and `S=s/F(Q)` belongs to `K(Q)`.
The third equation of (12), and then the target-line components of the
first two, have the form

```text
u(P)+v(Q)=constant.                                  (16)
```

By (1), each summand is constant.  It follows successively that

```text
p_i=C(P)u_i,             q_i=F(Q)v_i                 (17)
```

for fixed vectors at all three modes.  Substitution into (8) leaves a
nonzero fixed tensor times `C(P)F(Q)`.  Unique factorization and the
independence of the two probe rings force

```text
C(P) proportional P_a,       F(Q) proportional Q_a. (18)
```

If `R=0`, then (12)--(14) show that `S q_0`, `S q_1`, and `S q_2` are fixed.
Thus every `q_i` has one common `Q` form.  Equation (13) and unique
factorization make that form proportional to `Q_a` and make the selected
`p_1` form proportional to `P_a`.  This is the Q-common endpoint.  The case
`S=0` is symmetric and gives the P-common endpoint.  Permuting modes and
exchanging `p,q` gives all six edges and both endpoints. `square`

### Corollary 2.2 (two target colours are incompatible)

No common shores `p_i(P),q_i(Q)` satisfy (8) for two distinct colours.

### Proof

If either equation is nonendpoint, it fixes both common shore forms to its
own independent target coordinates.  The other equation fixes at least one
of those same shores to the other target coordinate, a contradiction.

If both are endpoints of the same type, their common shore would be
proportional to two independent `P` forms or two independent `Q` forms.  If
they are opposite endpoint types, the selected form of each lies on the
whole common shore fixed by the other, again making two independent target
forms proportional. `square`

### Proof

```text

p_1=c(P)e_2,                   q_1=d(Q)e_2,
```

```text

P_1Q_1 e_1e_1 tensor d_1,
P_2Q_1 e_2e_1 tensor d_0,                             (24)
```

which gives (23). `square`

## 5. Three-selector attachment obstruction

Restrict every outside slot to (21).  Put


```text
Z_3B_45+Z_4B_35+Z_5B_34=mu e_2 y_3y_4y_5,

```

### Theorem 5.1 (all three selectors are impossible)

Equations (30)--(31) have no solution over characteristic zero.

### Proof

Tensor the three equations (30) with `Y_3,Y_4,Y_5` on the complementary
ports and add them.  Every `YYZ` term occurs with both orders of its two
`V_1` factors, so alternating those factors kills it.  Equation (31) gives

```text
Alt_(V_1)(A tensor e_1)=0.                            (32)
```

The symmetric `Z` calculation gives

```text
Alt_(V_2)(A tensor e_2)=0.                            (33)
```

For a two-tensor `A`, (32) says that its `V_1` factor lies on `K e_1`,
while (33) says that its `V_2` factor lies on `K e_2`.  Hence

```text
A=alpha e_1 tensor e_2                               (34)
```

for some scalar `alpha`, including `alpha=0`.

Choose functionals taking value one on `e_1,e_2` and project

```text
k_u=(e_1-coordinate of Y_u),
l_u=(e_2-coordinate of Z_u).                         (35)
```

Equations (30)--(31) become

```text
alpha B_vw+k_v l_w+l_v k_w=0,                        (36)
sum_u k_u B_(O-{u})=lambda x_3x_4x_5,
sum_u l_u B_(O-{u})=mu y_3y_4y_5.                    (37)
```

Suppose first `alpha!=0`.  Eliminating the three `B` tensors gives, up to
one common nonzero scalar,

```text
k_3k_4l_5+k_3l_4k_5+l_3k_4k_5 proportional x_3x_4x_5,
l_3l_4k_5+l_3k_4l_5+k_3l_4l_5 proportional y_3y_4y_5. (38)
```

At every outside port, `k_u,l_u` must be independent: otherwise both
nonzero tensors in (38) have the same local factor line, whereas `x_u,y_u`
are independent.  Flatten the first tensor at port `3`.  Its coefficient
`k_4k_5` must be proportional to `x_4x_5`, so

```text
k_4 proportional x_4,        k_5 proportional x_5.   (39)
```

The second tensor similarly gives

```text
l_4 proportional y_4,        l_5 proportional y_5.   (40)
```

But the other coefficient in the first flattening is

```text
k_4l_5+l_4k_5,                                      (41)
```

a nonzero sum of the independent words `x_4y_5,y_4x_5`.  Purity of the
flattening would force this coefficient to be zero, which is impossible.

Now let `alpha=0`.  Equation (36) is

```text
k_vl_w+l_vk_w=0                                     (42)
```

for every pair.  If one port has independent `k,l`, (42) kills both vectors
at every other port, after which the same complementary `B` tensor would
have to supply the independent pure products in (37).  Otherwise every
doubly active port has `k_u=r_u l_u`.  A one-sided active port cannot coexist
with a doubly active port or with a port active on the opposite shore,
because their pair equation in (42) would be a nonzero decomposable tensor.
Two doubly active ports require opposite ratios; three would require a ratio
to equal its own negative.  With exactly two, adding the two attachment
equations after substitution gives

```text
2 l_i B_(O-{i})
 =mu y_3y_4y_5+lambda r_i^(-1)x_3x_4x_5,             (43)
```

whose left flattening has rank at most one and right flattening has rank
two.  One doubly active port again makes one common `B` tensor supply both
independent pure products; only one-sided activity kills one attachment.
These cases are exhaustive and all contradict (37). `square`
