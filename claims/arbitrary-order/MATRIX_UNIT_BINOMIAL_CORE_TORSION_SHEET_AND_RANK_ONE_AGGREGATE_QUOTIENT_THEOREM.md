# Matrix-unit binomial-core torsion-sheet and rank-one aggregate quotient theorem

## Status

This is an exact **arbitrary-order structural reduction** over `C`.  It
continues the complete same-multidegree fibre-lattice theorem at its explicit
aggregate-fibre boundary.

Start with any complete mixed same-multidegree matrix-unit target block and
an active transport cycle whose complete fibres are all binomial.  Choose a
parity-consistent family of binomial fibre equations containing every cycle
fibre.  This theorem proves that:

1. the selected signed binomial core can be untwisted exactly, and its
   quotient is the group algebra of one finitely generated abelian quotient
   lattice;
2. Smith torsion splits that group algebra into a finite product of Laurent
   polynomial rings, one for each torsion character;
3. every residual aggregate fibre becomes one exact Laurent polynomial on
   each character sheet;
4. the complete residual unit/properness question is decided by finitely many
   scalar evaluations when the quotient has free rank zero, and by finitely
   many univariate Laurent gcds when it has free rank one; and
5. because the active cycle is in the binomial core, aggregate equations can
   only change its holonomy elimination from the known sign ideal to the unit
   ideal.  A proper aggregate quotient cannot impose a different or stronger
   nonunit polynomial in `H`.

The theorem does **not** apply when one of the selected cycle fibres is
aggregate rather than binomial.  It does not prove that the quotient free
rank is at most one, or force the finite/rank-one criteria to give the unit
ideal.  Free rank at least two remains a multivariate Laurent ideal problem.
Aggregate cycle fibres, cross-multiplicity coupling, pure-cofactor exits,
deeper blockers, exclusion of the complete nonzero `r=1` matrix-unit branch,
and the global Krenn--Gu conjecture remain **UNKNOWN/UNRESOLVED**.

## 1. Input from the complete-block lattice reduction

Use the notation and exact reduction of

Thus a complete mixed word block has normalized fibre polynomials

```text
f_chi = sum_(M in F_chi) X^(1_M-1_(M_chi))
```

in

```text
A=C[L],                                               (1)
```

where `L` is the integer lattice generated by all within-fibre matching
differences.  Unit-ideal status and in-lattice holonomy elimination in `A`
are exactly the corresponding questions in the full physical Laurent ring.

Choose a set `B` of binomial fibres.  Orient their normalized equations as

```text
1+X^r_i=0,                 i in B,                    (2)
```

and set

```text
L_B=<r_i : i in B>_Z <= L.                            (3)
```

Assume one selected active cycle of length `m` has complete binomial fibres
and that `B` contains all `m` of their equations.  If (2) has an odd
integer-kernel dependency, the preceding theorem already makes the complete
block the unit ideal.  The new content therefore concerns the
**parity-consistent** branch, where

```text
rho_B(sum_i c_i r_i)=(-1)^(sum_i c_i)                 (4)
```

is a well-defined character `L_B -> {+1,-1}`.

Let `J_B` be the ideal generated by (2), and let `J_res` denote the images of
all remaining complete-block fibre polynomials, including aggregate fibres
and any binomials not selected into `B`.

## 2. Exact untwisting of the signed binomial core

### Theorem 1 (binomial-core quotient)

The sign character `rho_B` extends to a character

```text
theta:L -> C*.                                        (5)
```

For any such extension, the diagonal group-algebra automorphism

```text
alpha_theta(X^l)=theta(l)X^l                           (6)
```

sends every selected equation `1+X^r_i` to `1-X^r_i`.
Consequently

```text
A/J_B  isomorphic to  C[G],
G=L/L_B.                                              (7)
```

Under this isomorphism a residual fibre becomes

```text
p_chi=sum_(M in F_chi)
        theta(1_M-1_(M_chi)) X^[1_M-1_(M_chi)]
        in C[G],                                      (8)
```

where brackets denote the class in `G`.

Changing the extension `theta` composes (7) with a character automorphism of
`C[G]`.  Therefore the residual ideal's unit/properness, its quotient rank,
and all elimination conclusions below are independent of that choice.

### Proof

The multiplicative group `C*` is divisible.  Equivalently, put the inclusion
`L_B <= L` in Smith form and choose the required roots on each finite-index
coordinate, with arbitrary nonzero values on a free complement.  This extends
(4) and proves (5).

Because `theta(r_i)=-1`, equation (6) gives

```text
alpha_theta(1+X^r_i)=1-X^r_i.                        (9)
```

The kernel of the natural group-algebra map

```text
C[L] -> C[L/L_B]
```

is generated by `1-X^l` for `l in L_B`.  The generators `r_i` suffice because

```text
1-X^(a+b)=(1-X^a)+X^a(1-X^b)
```

and Laurent monomials are units, so inverses and integer multiples introduce
no new generators.  Quotienting (9) proves (7), and applying the same map to
each term of a residual fibre proves (8).

If `theta'` is another extension, `theta'/theta` is trivial on `L_B` and hence
descends to a character of `G`.  Multiplying every group monomial by that
character is an automorphism taking one residual presentation to the other.
QED.

This is an exact quotient, not a numerical phase specialization.  Torsion in
`G` is retained.

## 3. Smith torsion-character sheets

Write the finitely generated abelian quotient in Smith form as

```text
G isomorphic to Z^q direct_sum T,
T=Z/d_1 direct_sum ... direct_sum Z/d_s,
1<d_1 | d_2 | ... | d_s.                            (10)
```

Let `T^` be the finite character group `Hom(T,C*)`.  A choice of Smith
coordinates sends each residual `p_chi` to a Laurent polynomial

```text
p_(chi,tau) in C[y_1^(+/-1),...,y_q^(+/-1)]          (11)
```

by evaluating the torsion coordinates at `tau in T^`.

### Theorem 2 (finite sheet decomposition)

There is an exact algebra isomorphism

```text
C[G]
 isomorphic to
 product_(tau in T^) C[y_1^(+/-1),...,y_q^(+/-1)].  (12)
```

Under (12), the residual ideal is the product of the sheet ideals

```text
P_tau=(p_(chi,tau) : chi residual).                 (13)
```

Hence

```text
J_B+J_res is the unit ideal
 iff P_tau is the unit ideal for every tau in T^,     (14)

J_B+J_res is proper
 iff P_tau is proper for at least one tau in T^.      (15)
```

### Proof

After choosing the splitting (10),

```text
C[G]=C[Z^q] tensor_C C[T].                           (16)
```

Over `C`, the finite Fourier transform gives

```text
C[T] isomorphic to product_(tau in T^) C,
X^t maps to (tau(t))_tau.                            (17)
```

Tensoring (17) with the free Laurent ring gives (12).  Ideals in a finite
direct product are exactly products of ideals in the factors.  The unit of a
product belongs to the residual ideal exactly when it belongs in every
factor, proving (14) and (15).  QED.

The coefficients in (11) are exact roots of unity and exact values of the
chosen extension character.  Smith form and cyclotomic arithmetic make the
decomposition reproducible without floating-point phases.

## 4. Complete low-free-rank aggregate criteria

### Theorem 3 (free rank zero)

If `q=0`, every sheet ring is `C`.  The complete residual ideal is proper
exactly when there exists a torsion character `tau` such that

```text
p_(chi,tau)=0  for every residual fibre chi.         (18)
```

Equivalently, it is the unit ideal exactly when every torsion character is
detected by at least one nonzero residual evaluation.

This is a finite exhaustive criterion with exactly

```text
|T|=d_1...d_s
```

character sheets.

### Proof

An ideal in one copy of `C` is either zero or the whole field.  The sheet
ideal `P_tau` is zero precisely when all of its scalar generators vanish.
Apply Theorem 2.  QED.

### Theorem 4 (free rank one)

If `q=1`, write the free coordinate as `t`.  For each torsion character,
let

```text
g_tau(t)=gcd_chi p_(chi,tau)(t)                     (19)
```

in `C[t^(+/-1)]`, with the conventions:

- common Laurent monomial factors are units and are removed;
- a nonzero constant gcd is a unit;
- if every residual polynomial on the sheet is zero, `g_tau=0`.

Then

```text
P_tau=(g_tau),                                      (20)

J_B+J_res is the unit ideal
 iff every g_tau is a nonzero constant,             (21)

J_B+J_res is proper
 iff some g_tau is zero or nonconstant.             (22)
```

In the proper case there is an actual common point on the corresponding
complex torus sheet: any nonzero root of a nonconstant normalized gcd, or an
arbitrary nonzero `t` when the gcd is zero.  Composing with the inverse
untwisting and extending the resulting character from `L` to the physical
edge lattice gives a complex physical-torus solution of this complete block.

### Proof

The Laurent polynomial ring `C[t^(+/-1)]` is the localization of the PID
`C[t]` at the powers of `t`; it is again a PID.  Its finitely generated sheet
ideal is therefore generated by the Laurent gcd (19), proving (20).  A
Laurent polynomial is a unit exactly when it is a nonzero scalar times a
power of `t`; after the stated normalization this is exactly a nonzero
constant.  Theorem 2 now gives (21) and (22).

A normalized nonconstant polynomial has a nonzero complex root: any factor
`t` was removed as a Laurent unit.  Such a root annihilates every generator
because the gcd generates their ideal.  The zero-gcd case is tautological.
The character-extension argument is the same Smith/root argument as in
Theorem 1.  QED.

### Boundary at free rank at least two

For `q>=2`, Theorem 2 remains exact but a list of multivariate Laurent
polynomials is not classified by a single gcd.  A fixed instance can be
tested by exact saturation or Groebner methods over the relevant cyclotomic
field, but this theorem supplies no uniform unit certificate and no bound on
that computation.  A finite search window, random specialization, modular
image, or timeout is not a proof of either branch.

## 5. Holonomy consequence

Let the selected binomial core contain the `m` cycle fibres and orient them
so that

```text
z=r_1+...+r_m,
H=X^z.                                              (23)
```

The core alone forces

```text
H=(-1)^m.                                          (24)
```

### Theorem 5 (aggregate unit-or-known-sign dichotomy)

After adjoining the complete residual fibre equations and the exact
definition of `H`, the elimination ideal in `C[H]` is

```text
(1)                 if every torsion sheet is killed;
(H-(-1)^m)          if at least one torsion sheet survives.  (25)
```

There is no third nonunit possibility.

### Proof

If every sheet ideal is a unit, Theorem 2 makes the complete block ideal a
unit.  Otherwise the quotient is nonzero.  Equation (24) holds before any
aggregate equation is added, so `H-(-1)^m` lies in the elimination ideal.
In a nonzero `C`-algebra the scalar field embeds and `H` acts as exactly the
scalar `(-1)^m`; hence the kernel of `C[H]` is precisely the maximal ideal
`(H-(-1)^m)`.  QED.

Thus aggregate same-degree equations are load-bearing only through a unit
certificate.  If they leave any quotient sheet alive, they do not strengthen
the selected cycle's holonomy beyond its existing sign equation.
