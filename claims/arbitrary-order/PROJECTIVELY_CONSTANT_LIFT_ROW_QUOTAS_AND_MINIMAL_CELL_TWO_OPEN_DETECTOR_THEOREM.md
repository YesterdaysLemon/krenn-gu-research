# Projectively constant lift row quotas and the minimal-cell two-open detector

## Status

**Exact conditional characteristic-zero Hall transfer and minimal-cell
detector.**  Work on the aligned common-two-row, projectively constant branch
of the single-open consecutive permanent lift.  Its complete open-root
identity is an honest weighted restriction

```text
P_M -> Delta_3,       M=r+2q+1,       r>=2, q>=0.      (1)
```

The repeated lifted rows obey separate physical-mode quotas.  If `p_a` and
`p_b` count the nonzero outside covectors in the two common rows, then

```text
p_a>=3q+2,             p_b>=3q+3.                     (2)
```

The second inequality recovers `r>=q+3`.  At equality, the `b` row is
coordinate-proportional at every outside mode, with exactly `q+1` modes of
each target colour.  The first inequality is new bookkeeping from the lifted
`a` row and has an important tight-surplus consequence: when `q=0`, the
one-mode `a` star from the two-open sharpness theorem cannot satisfy the
complete single-open diagonal identity.

In the minimum allowed cell

```text
q=0,                  r=3,                            (3)
```

the conclusion is stronger.  For either root `i` other than the aligned root
`j`, the two-open row-replacement tensor is nonzero.  Consequently every
nonzero affine absorption direction at `i` is detected by the complete

permanent lift to the next row-replacement depth.

The individual coefficient-to-cofactor map is not claimed injective: in (3)
its effective domain has dimension two but only one companion coefficient
remains after `i` is opened.  Nonzero detection, not injectivity, is the exact
conclusion.  The argument does not force the aligned factorization or

## 2. Separate repeated-row Hall quotas

### Theorem 1 (lifted physical-row quotas)

For each target colour `c`, at least `q+1` distinct modes of `B^+` have
`hat a` a nonzero multiple of `e_c^*`, and at least `q+1` distinct modes have
`hat b` a nonzero multiple of `e_c^*`.  Hence (2) holds.

At `r=q+3`, every `b_u`, `u in B`, is coordinate-proportional and the three
coordinate directions occur exactly `q+1` times each.

### Proof

For `q>=1`, apply the all-subset kernel Hall theorem to the source subset
formed by the `q+1` identical copies of `hat a`.  At any target mode their
span is the single line generated by `hat a`, so the Hall conclusion

```text
e_c^* in span{the selected source rows}                (9)
```

at `q+1` modes says exactly that `hat a` is a nonzero multiple of `e_c^*`
there.  Repeat with the `q+1` copies of `hat b`.  When `q=0`, the singleton
tricolour-cover theorem gives the same statement for the one `hat a` and one
`hat b` row.

A one-dimensional covector line cannot contain two distinct coordinate axes.
The three colours therefore require `3(q+1)` distinct modes for each row
family.  Since `hat b|_j=0`, all its modes lie in `B`, so

```text
p_b>=3(q+1)=3q+3.                                     (10)
```

At most one of the required `hat a` modes is the new mode `j`, giving

```text
p_a>=3(q+1)-1=3q+2.                                   (11)
```

Now `p_b<=|B|=r+2q`, and (10) gives `r>=q+3`.  At equality
`|B|=3(q+1)`, so every outside mode is used by exactly one `b` coordinate
direction and each direction occurs exactly `q+1` times.  This proves all
claims.

### Tight-surplus star consequence

At `q=0`, (11) says `p_a>=2`.  The exact star gauge in
[`BALANCED_TWO_OPEN_ROOT_GAUGE_DETECTOR_AND_STAR_INVISIBILITY_BOUNDARY.md`](BALANCED_TWO_OPEN_ROOT_GAUGE_DETECTOR_AND_STAR_INVISIBILITY_BOUNDARY.md)
has `p_a=1`.  It therefore remains a valid full-tensor gauge construction and
a valid sharpness result for the truncated data, but it cannot simultaneously
satisfy the lifted diagonal restriction (7).  This does not exclude other
tight-surplus representations or other mechanisms of row-replacement
vanishing.

## 3. The minimum Hall cell

Put `q=0,r=3`, fix

```text
R={i,j,s},             B={u_0,u_1,u_2}.               (12)
```

Theorem 1 and `|B|=3` permit a relabelling of the outside modes such that

```text
b_(u_c)=beta_c e_c^*,       beta_c!=0,
c=0,1,2.                                                (13)
```

The two row-replacement tensors in (8) coincide:

```text
A_(i,j;s)=A_(s,j;i)=A,
A=P_3(a,a,b).                                          (14)
```

There is no factorial divisor because `(q+1)!=1`.

### Lemma 2 (zero collision forces local proportionality)

If `A=0`, then every `a_(u_c)` is nonzero and

```text
a_(u_c) is proportional to b_(u_c)                    (15)
```

for all three modes.

### Proof

The labelled permanent expansion is

```text
A/2
 =b_(u_0) tensor a_(u_1) tensor a_(u_2)
  +a_(u_0) tensor b_(u_1) tensor a_(u_2)
  +a_(u_0) tensor a_(u_1) tensor b_(u_2).              (16)
```

The factor `2` labels the two identical `a` rows and is nonzero in
characteristic zero.

If, for example, `a_(u_0)=0`, equation (16) and `b_(u_0)!=0` force
`a_(u_1)=0` or `a_(u_2)=0`.  Thus at most one outside `a` covector would be
nonzero, contradicting `p_a>=2` from (11).  Hence all three `a` covectors are
nonzero.

Flatten (16) at mode `u_0`.  If `a_(u_0)` and `b_(u_0)` were independent,
a covector on their two-dimensional span could annihilate `a_(u_0)` but not
`b_(u_0)`.  Contracting (16) with it would leave the nonzero decomposable
tensor

```text
a_(u_1) tensor a_(u_2),                               (17)
```

a contradiction.  Thus the two covectors are proportional.  The same
argument at `u_1,u_2` proves (15).

By (13), (15) makes `a_(u_c)` a nonzero multiple of `e_c^*` at every outside
mode.

## 4. A one-coordinate recolouring contradiction

### Theorem 3 (minimal-cell two-open detection)

In (12), the tensor `A` in (14) is nonzero.  Consequently, for each
`i in R-{j}`, every nonzero affine absorption direction at `i` is detected by
the complete two-open tensor.

### Proof

Assume `A=0`.  Lemma 2 and (13) say that both `a` and `b` evaluate to zero on
`e_c` at every outside mode except `u_c`.

Evaluate the lifted restriction (7) with every outside mode set to `e_c` and
the new mode `j` set first to `e_c`.  The `b` row must occupy column `u_c`.
The `a` row cannot use another outside column, so it must occupy column `j`.
The two persistent rows `hat h_i,hat h_s` occupy the remaining outside
columns.  Thus the coefficient is exactly

```text
eta_j(e_c) beta_c K_c=bar X_c!=0,                    (18)

K_c=P_2(
  h_i|_(B-{u_c}),h_s|_(B-{u_c});
  every retained mode evaluated at e_c).              (19)
```

In particular, all three factors on the left of (18) are nonzero.

Now keep every outside mode at `e_c` but set the open mode `j` to `e_d` with
`d!=c`.  The same unique row-type assignment gives

```text
eta_j(e_d) beta_c K_c.                                (20)
```

The target word is mixed, so (7) says (20) is zero.  Since `beta_c K_c!=0`,
this forces `eta_j(e_d)=0`.  Taking `c=0,d=1` contradicts the nonvanishing of
`eta_j(e_1)` forced by (18) with `c=1`.  Hence `A!=0`.

The companion-frame theorem gives

```text
span{ell_(j,i),ell_(j,s)}=Ann(x_j),
dim Ann(x_j)=2.                                       (21)
```

There are exactly two covectors in (21), so both are nonzero.  On the
projectively constant branch, the two-open variation at root `i` is

```text
delta T_ij(y,z)
 =tau kappa_i(y) ell_(j,s)(z) A,                      (22)
```

and the analogous formula with `i,s` exchanged holds at root `s`.  A tensor
product of three nonzero factors over a field is nonzero, so every nonzero
`kappa_i` is detected.  This proves the theorem.

### Why this is detection, not injectivity

For fixed `i`, deleting its coordinate leaves only the scalar coefficient
`ell_(j,s)(z)`.  The resulting map from the two-dimensional effective
companion plane has a one-dimensional kernel.  Equation (22) is nevertheless
nonzero, which is the necessary and sufficient condition for the particular
affine gauge direction to change the two-open tensor.  The stronger
injectivity condition in the general two-open theorem is sufficient but not
necessary here.
