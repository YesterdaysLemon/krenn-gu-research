# Matrix-unit all-bridge beta-three route-port fixed-completion mixed-fibre rank-three block theorem

## Status and exact scope

This is an exact conditional characteristic-zero composition theorem in the
simultaneous balanced all-bridge branch.  Start with the globally least
bipartite pure core of A5 at beta three and an extremal sparse quartic site.
Thus its first-cofactor core A has one of the A4 suppressed route kernels
Q/Q or Q/C^2 and has exactly four perfect matchings.

The new hypothesis is deliberately explicit:

> There is one fixed nonzero U7K-compatible completion K that extends all
> four core perfect matchings into one complete mixed word fibre F_chi.

Under that hypothesis, the four extensions form an internally cancelling
four-term block B_K.  Its exponent-difference lattice has rank three.  The
complement of B_K in the complete fibre has zero total and therefore is
either empty or contains at least two terms.  In particular,

~~~text
|F_chi|=4 or |F_chi|>=6; in particular |F_chi|!=5.
~~~

The A5 route-port types survive inside the injected block: Q/Q gives four
paired singleton blocks, while Q/C^2 gives four paired singleton blocks and
two nonzero exact-negative doubleton blocks.  These are B_K-restricted port
statements, not claims about all terms in F_chi.

The fixed completion is an assumption, not a conclusion.  This theorem does
not force such a K, does not force a nonempty complementary fibre, does not
produce a Laurent unit, does not exclude either route kernel, and does not
close the simultaneous all-bridge branch.  It makes no mixed-cut, Larry,
S2N, or global claim.  The global Krenn--Gu conjecture remains
**UNRESOLVED**.

## 1. Pure core and four matching terms

Work over C.  Let (e,S) be globally least over all colours and all nonempty
proper even shores supporting a pure cancellation, and let A be its
connected bipartite matching-covered first-cofactor core.  Assume

~~~text
beta(A)=3
~~~

and that A has an extremal sparse quartic site.  By A5, A is in the Q/Q or
Q/C^2 route-kernel case and

~~~text
PM(A)={M_0,M_1,M_2,M_3}.                             (1)
~~~

For a core perfect matching M, write

~~~text
lambda(M)=product_(f in M) z_f !=0.                  (2)
~~~

For a core edge f=ab, the inherited A5 port contribution is

~~~text
C_f=z_f haf(Z^e[S-{a,b}])
   =sum_(M in PM(A): f in M) lambda(M)!=0.            (2a)
~~~

Thus C_f includes the endpoint edge weight z_f; it is not the bare deletion
hafnian.

The pure zero is the full four-term relation

~~~text
lambda(M_0)+lambda(M_1)+lambda(M_2)+lambda(M_3)=0.  (3)
~~~

Let

~~~text
a_i=1_(M_i) in Z^(E(A))                              (4)
~~~

be the edge-incidence vector of M_i.

### Lemma 1 (the four core exponents are affinely independent)

The four vectors a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3 are affinely independent.  Consequently,

~~~text
u_i=a_i-a_0,  i=1,2,3,                               (5)
~~~

are linearly independent over R and over Q, and

~~~text
L_B=Z u_1 + Z u_2 + Z u_3                            (6)
~~~

is a free rank-three lattice with the displayed u_i as a basis.

#### Proof

A3 identifies the matching polytope of A as the convex hull of the
incidence vectors of PM(A) and proves that its affine dimension is beta(A).
Here that dimension is three.  By (1), its affine hull is generated by only
the four displayed points.  Four points can have affine hull of dimension
three only when they are affinely independent.  Thus u_1,u_2,u_3 are
linearly independent over R.  Integer independence follows immediately,
and (6), by its definition as their integer span, is free of rank three.
No saturation in a larger ambient edge lattice is asserted.  QED.

In particular, there is no nontrivial integer affine relation among the four
exponents:

~~~text
sum_(i=0)^3 c_i a_i=0 and sum_(i=0)^3 c_i=0
        imply c_0=c_1=c_2=c_3=0.                     (7)
~~~

Equivalently, there is no nonzero integer dependency among u_1,u_2,u_3,
odd or otherwise.  This statement concerns exponent vectors.  It must not
be confused with the scalar amplitude cancellation (3).

## 2. The fixed-completion hypothesis

Now work inside a hypothetical complete nonzero r=1 matrix-unit witness in
the simultaneous balanced all-bridge branch.  Assume there is a perfect
matching K on the complementary vertex set V-S such that:

1. every edge used by K has nonzero weight;
2. K is vertex-disjoint from each M_i;
3. each union

   ~~~text
   Mhat_i=K union M_i                                (8)
   ~~~

   is a full physical perfect matching;
4. all four Mhat_i induce the same word chi; and
5. chi is mixed, so its complete target coefficient is zero.

These are exactly the fixed-completion conditions used by U7K, specialized
to residual shore R=S and empty fixed internal matching C.  Put

~~~text
kappa=lambda(K)!=0,
B_K={Mhat_0,Mhat_1,Mhat_2,Mhat_3} subset F_chi,      (9)
~~~

where F_chi denotes the complete set of compatible full perfect matchings
inducing chi.  Every term of F_chi has a nonzero monomial weight because the
matrix-unit table is complete nonzero.

The word complete in F_chi is load-bearing: it includes every compatible
physical perfect matching at chi, not only the four fixed completions.

### Theorem 2 (zero block and preserved rank)

The fixed-completion block B_K has exactly four distinct terms and

~~~text
sum_(Mhat in B_K) lambda(Mhat)
  =kappa sum_(M in PM(A)) lambda(M)
  =0.                                                 (10)
~~~

If ahat_i=1_(Mhat_i), then

~~~text
ahat_i=1_K+a_i,
ahat_i-ahat_0=a_i-a_0=u_i.                           (11)
~~~

Hence the exponent-difference lattice of B_K has rank exactly three.

#### Proof

The map M -> K union M is injective because restriction to S recovers M.
Disjointness gives multiplicativity

~~~text
lambda(K union M)=kappa lambda(M).
~~~

Equation (10) is therefore (3) multiplied by the common nonzero factor
kappa.  Incidence vectors add on disjoint edge sets, so the common vector
1_K cancels from every difference in (11).  Lemma 1 then gives rank three.
QED.

The two conclusions in Theorem 2 have different sources.  Affine
independence is combinatorial and comes from the A3 polytope dimension plus
the exact A5 count.  Cancellation is a pointwise equality among nonzero
complex amplitudes and comes from the pure hafnian zero.  Neither conclusion
implies the other.

### Corollary 2.1 (complete-fibre rank floor)

The exponent-difference lattice of the complete fibre F_chi has rank at
least three.  Indeed, it contains all differences between terms of B_K and
therefore contains the rank-three lattice L_B from (6).  Thus this fibre is
outside any quotient case whose hypothesis is complete-fibre difference
rank zero or one.  This is only a rank placement: it does not make the fibre
all-binomial and supplies no exclusion of an aggregate fibre.

## 3. Complete-fibre complement

### Theorem 3 (the fibre cannot have exactly five terms)

Let

~~~text
R_chi=F_chi-B_K.                                     (12)
~~~

Then

~~~text
sum_(X in R_chi) lambda(X)=0,                        (13)
~~~

and

~~~text
|R_chi|=0 or |R_chi|>=2.                             (14)
~~~

Consequently,

~~~text
|F_chi|=4 or |F_chi|>=6,                             (15)
~~~

so the complete mixed fibre cannot have cardinality five.

#### Proof

Because chi is mixed, the complete target equation is

~~~text
sum_(X in F_chi) lambda(X)=0.                        (16)
~~~

Subtract the block equality (10) from (16) to obtain (13).  If R_chi had
exactly one element X, (13) would say lambda(X)=0, contrary to complete
nonzero support.  The empty complement is allowed, and every other
possibility has at least two elements.  Adding |B_K|=4 proves (15).  QED.

This is only a one-term-complement obstruction.  In particular, (13) does
not force R_chi to be nonempty and gives no classification of a complement
with two or more terms.

## 4. Route-port types inside the injected block

For a core vertex p and incident core edge f, A5 uses

~~~text
P_A(p,f)={M in PM(A): f in M}.                       (17)
~~~

Define the corresponding B_K-restricted full-matching port by

~~~text
P_B(p,f)={K union M: M in P_A(p,f)} subset B_K.      (18)
~~~

Its edge-inclusive block contribution is

~~~text
C_B(p,f)=sum_(Mhat in P_B(p,f)) lambda(Mhat)
        =kappa sum_(M in P_A(p,f)) lambda(M)
        =kappa C_f.                                   (19)
~~~

The notation is intentionally restricted to B_K.  An unrelated term of
F_chi-B_K may also use f, so no assertion about the cardinality of the full
F_chi-port follows.

### 4.1 Q/Q

Let v,w be the quartic sites, let R_1,...,R_4 be the internally disjoint odd
v--w routes, and let

~~~text
a_i=f_v(R_i),  b_i=f_w(R_i),  i=1,...,4.             (20)
~~~

Using the A5 indexing PM(A)={M_1,...,M_4}, the injected block has

~~~text
P_B(v,a_i)=P_B(w,b_i)={K union M_i},                 (21)

C_B(v,a_i)=C_B(w,b_i)
            =kappa lambda(M_i)!=0.                   (22)
~~~

Thus each route gives the same singleton at its two endpoints and the same
nonzero full matching contribution.  The four singleton contributions sum
to zero by (10).  Equation (22) does not identify endpoint edge weights or
bare deletion hafnians.

### 4.2 Q/C^2

Use the A5 notation: v is the sparse quartic site; x,y are the cubic sites;
R_x1,R_x2 are the odd v--x routes; R_y1,R_y2 are the odd v--y routes; and E
is the unique even x--y route.  Let a_xi,b_xi,a_yj,b_yj be the odd-route
endpoint edges and let g_x,g_y be the endpoint edges of E.

With

~~~text
PM(A)={M_x1,M_x2,M_y1,M_y2},                         (23)
~~~

the four odd routes give the B_K-restricted paired singletons

~~~text
P_B(v,a_xi)=P_B(x,b_xi)={K union M_xi},  i=1,2,
P_B(v,a_yj)=P_B(y,b_yj)={K union M_yj},  j=1,2.      (24)
~~~

The even route gives disjoint complementary doubletons inside B_K:

~~~text
P_B(x,g_x)={K union M_y1,K union M_y2},
P_B(y,g_y)={K union M_x1,K union M_x2}.              (25)
~~~

Their edge-inclusive block contributions are

~~~text
C_B(x,g_x)=kappa[lambda(M_y1)+lambda(M_y2)]!=0,
C_B(y,g_y)=kappa[lambda(M_x1)+lambda(M_x2)]!=0,      (26)

C_B(x,g_x)=-C_B(y,g_y).                              (27)
~~~

The nonvanishing in (26) is imported from A5 and survives because
kappa!=0.  Equation (27) is the same exact-negative relation multiplied by
kappa.  It is not algebraic independence, a relation between bare deletion
hafnians, or a statement that the corresponding ports in all of F_chi are
doubletons.

## 5. Normalized rank-three block polynomial

Attach a formal monomial t^ahat to a full matching incidence vector ahat.
After division by the nonzero reference monomial t^ahat_0, the formal block
polynomial is

~~~text
p_B=1+t^u_1+t^u_2+t^u_3 in C[L_B].                  (28)
~~~

By Lemma 1, the basis u_1,u_2,u_3 identifies the group algebra C[L_B] with

~~~text
C[X,X^(-1),Y,Y^(-1),Z,Z^(-1)]
~~~

and sends (28) to

~~~text
p_B=1+X+Y+Z.                                         (29)
~~~

Here X,Y,Z are formal characters of the free difference lattice.  Calling
them coordinates does not claim that their numerical values at a witness
are algebraically independent.  In particular, p_B is not the zero
polynomial.  Equation (10) says that the physical edge-weight evaluation of
this nonzero formal polynomial is zero; it is not a polynomial identity in
independent variables.

### Theorem 4 (proper nonunit, with no exponent dependency)

The normalized four-term polynomial p_B is a proper nonunit of C[L_B].
It has a torus zero, for example

~~~text
(X,Y,Z)=(-1/3,-1/3,-1/3).                            (30)
~~~

At the hypothetical witness, its coordinate values are

~~~text
X=lambda(M_1)/lambda(M_0),
Y=lambda(M_2)/lambda(M_0),
Z=lambda(M_3)/lambda(M_0),                           (31)
~~~

up to relabelling, and (3) gives another torus zero of (29).  Nevertheless,
the four exponents have no nontrivial integer affine dependency, and the
three exponent differences have no nonzero integer dependency, including
none with odd coefficient sum.

#### Proof

All three coordinates in (30) are nonzero and substitute into (29) to give
zero.  Evaluation at (30) is a unital homomorphism C[L_B] -> C that kills
p_B but not 1, so the principal ideal (p_B) is proper and p_B is not a unit.
Equivalently, Laurent polynomial units are nonzero scalar monomials, whereas
p_B has four distinct exponents.

Equation (31) consists of nonzero ratios.  Dividing (3) by
lambda(M_0)!=0 proves that these ratios also satisfy (29).  The absence of
integer exponent dependencies is exactly Lemma 1 and (7); it is unaffected
by either numerical zero.  QED.

This proper-nonunit statement concerns the single block polynomial in its
rank-three group algebra.  It neither says that p_B generates a unit nor
says that some larger collection of complete target equations cannot
generate a unit.  No odd-dependency exclusion follows from this block.

## 6. Conditional U7J split-extra reading

Suppose the mixed word chi is a word chi_i on a selected U7J active
transport cycle, whose selected incoming and outgoing matching terms are
G_(i-1) and F_i.  Impose the additional condition

~~~text
B_K intersect {G_(i-1),F_i}=empty.                  (32)
~~~

Only under (32), every term of B_K belongs to U7J's extra-matching list at
chi_i.  Its normalized extra subtotal is

~~~text
A_i(B_K)
  =sum_(X in B_K) lambda(X)/lambda(F_i)
  =0.                                                 (33)
~~~

Thus B_K is one exact split-extra zero subblock.  It need not exhaust the
extra list, so (33) does not imply that U7J's full aggregate defect A_i is
zero.

In the Q/C^2 case, the two doubletons in (25) give normalized partial sums

~~~text
A_i^x=C_B(x,g_x)/lambda(F_i)!=0,
A_i^y=C_B(y,g_y)/lambda(F_i)!=0,
A_i^x=-A_i^y.                                        (34)
~~~

Their union is the zero block (33).  If (32) fails, this section makes no
extra-subfibre claim; Theorems 2--4 and the complete-fibre complement
statement remain the only conclusions.

## 9. Exact boundary

This theorem proves exactly a conditional rank-three zero block and its
one-term-complement obstruction.  It does **not** prove any of the following:

- existence of a compatible fixed completion K;
- existence of any term outside B_K;
- equality of a B_K-restricted route port with a full F_chi-port;
- equality of bare deletion hafnians;
- algebraic independence of edge weights, port sums, or numerical ratios;
- a distinct mixed fibre for any route or port;
- target attachment without the fixed-completion hypothesis;
- a Laurent-unit certificate or an all-bridge exclusion;
- a mixed-cut or complementary-shore obstruction;
- a Larry- or S2N-lane conclusion;
- a construction of a simultaneous all-bridge witness; or
- a proof or refutation of the Krenn--Gu conjecture.

The complete-fibre complement may be empty.  A proper nonunit polynomial
having torus zeros is compatible with a hypothetical witness and is not an
exclusion.  The global status remains **UNRESOLVED**.
