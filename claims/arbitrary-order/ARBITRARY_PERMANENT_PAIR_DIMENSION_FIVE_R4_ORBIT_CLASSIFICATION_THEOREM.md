# Arbitrary permanent pair-dimension-five `r=4` orbit classification

## Status

This note gives an exact characteristic-zero classification of pairs of
three-planes

```text
U,V subset (Z_4)_1,
dim U=dim V=3,
dim(UV)=5.                                                   (1)
```

The equivalence is generated by coordinate permutations, nonzero coordinate
scalings, and changes of bases inside `U,V`.  There are five unbased orbits.
Three, and only three, admit colour bases for which the six mixed products
span a two-space and the three same-colour products are independent modulo
that two-space.  This last property is the exact pair-level equality
condition forced by a `Delta_3` restriction.

The fixed pair used in
`ARBITRARY_PERMANENT_FIXED_PAIR_DIMENSION_FIVE_FULL_PROJECTION_BOUNDARY.md`
is only one of the three admissible orbits.  In particular, it is not a
universal normal form for equality five.  An explicit inequivalent
`1+3`-split frame is given below.

The classification is for `r=4`.  It gives exact inequivalent examples in
`r=6` by zero extension, but it does not classify equality-five pairs whose
active coordinate support has size five or six.  It therefore does not
transport either fixed-pair exclusion to every `P_6 -> Delta_3` equality
pair.  Unrestricted permanent nonrestriction and the global Krenn--Gu
conjecture remain **UNRESOLVED**.

## 1. Product spaces and the pair-level diagonal condition

Let `K` be a field of characteristic zero and put

```text
Z_4=K[x_0,x_1,x_2,x_3]/(x_0^2,x_1^2,x_2^2,x_3^2).          (2)
```

For three-planes `U,V subset (Z_4)_1`, write

```text
B=UV=span{uv:u in U, v in V} subset (Z_4)_2.                (3)
```

### Definition 1 (Delta-admissible equality frame)

An equality-five pair `(U,V)` is **Delta-admissible at the pair level** if
there are ordered bases `(u_0,u_1,u_2)` of `U` and `(v_0,v_1,v_2)` of `V`
such that, with

```text
M=span{u_i v_j:i!=j},                  d_e=u_e v_e,          (4)
```

one has

```text
dim M=2,
B=M direct-sum span{d_0,d_1,d_2}.                            (5)
```

This is necessary for an actual `Delta_3` extension: the mixed products
lie in the left radical, while the three diagonal products induce the three
independent target directions.  Definition 1 does not assert that the pair
extends to the other modes.

There is a useful invariant reformulation.  Let

```text
mu:U tensor V -> B,
L=mu^*(B^*) subset U^* tensor V^*.                           (6)
```

### Lemma 2 (rank-one criterion)

Definition 1 holds if and only if `L` contains three rank-one forms

```text
lambda_e tensor rho_e,                  e=0,1,2,              (7)
```

such that the three `lambda_e` form a basis of `U^*` and the three `rho_e`
form a basis of `V^*`.

Indeed, from (5), pull back the basis of `(B/M)^*` dual to the three
diagonals.  Conversely, take bases of `U,V` dual to the two factor bases in
(7), and put `M` equal to the common kernel of the three functionals in
`B`.  The mixed products lie in `M`; the three diagonals map to a basis of
`B/M`; and `dim M=5-3=2`.

The criterion concerns arbitrary choices of the pair-level radical.  It is
therefore stronger than checking one preselected colour basis, but weaker
than producing a full six-mode restriction.

## 2. The annihilator of a hyperplane product

Every three-plane in the four-space `(Z_4)_1` is a hyperplane.  Write

```text
U=ker(alpha),                 V=ker(beta),                    (8)
```

for projective covectors `alpha,beta in K^4`.

Identify the dual of `(Z_4)_2` with symmetric zero-diagonal `4 x 4`
matrices: an edge functional `q=(q_ij)` acts on `uv` as `u^T Qv`, where
`Q_ij=Q_ji=q_ij` and `Q_ii=0`.

### Lemma 3 (distinct hyperplanes)

Suppose `alpha,beta` are independent.  Every symmetric bilinear form
vanishing on `U x V` is uniquely

```text
Q=c alpha alpha^T+e beta beta^T.                             (9)
```

Consequently the ordinary annihilator of `B` consists of the pairs `(c,e)`
satisfying

```text
c alpha_i^2+e beta_i^2=0,                 i=0,1,2,3.         (10)
```

To prove (9), put `H=U intersect V`.  Symmetry and vanishing on `U x V`
make `Q` kill `H`, so it descends to the two-space `K^4/H`.  The two lines
`U/H` and `V/H` are orthogonal.  In the dual coordinates supplied by
`alpha,beta`, the descended form is diagonal, which is exactly (9).

### Corollary 4 (the four sign-split normal forms)

For distinct hyperplanes, `dim B=5` if and only if

```text
supp(alpha)=supp(beta)=S,
beta_i/alpha_i in {tau,-tau} on S,                           (11)
```

for one nonzero `tau`, with both signs occurring.

Indeed, `dim B=5` says that (10) has a one-dimensional nonzero solution.
Both `c,e` are nonzero.  Thus `alpha_i=0` exactly when `beta_i=0`, and on
their common support all squared ratios are the same.  Dividing `beta` by
one ratio makes every ratio `+1` or `-1`; both signs occur because the
hyperplanes are distinct.  Conversely such a sign pattern makes (10) the
single equation `c+e=0`.

Coordinate scaling now makes `alpha_i=1` on `S`, while a coordinate
permutation groups the two signs.  Multiplying `beta` by `-1` exchanges the
two groups.  If

```text
s=|S|,                    k=min(number of + signs, number of - signs),
```

the four possibilities are

```text
(s,k)=(2,1), (3,1), (4,1), (4,2).                          (12)
```

Their normal forms are

```text
alpha=(1,...,1,0,...,0),
beta =(1,...,1,-1,...,-1,0,...,0),                          (13)
```

with `s` active entries and sign-block sizes `k,s-k`.

The unique annihilator has support graph

```text
K_(k,s-k) plus (4-s) isolated vertices.                      (14)
```

Indeed, after normalization it is `alpha alpha^T-beta beta^T`, whose
off-diagonal entries are nonzero exactly across the two sign blocks.  The
four degree multisets are

```text
(1,1,0,0), (2,1,1,0), (3,1,1,1), (2,2,2,2),                (15)
```

so these four orbits are mutually inequivalent under the monomial ambient
group.

### Lemma 5 (coincident hyperplanes)

If `U=V=ker(alpha)` and `s=|supp(alpha)|`, then

```text
dim(U^2)=2+s.                                                (16)
```

Every symmetric form vanishing on `U x U` is

```text
alpha z^T+z alpha^T,
```

and its zero-diagonal conditions are `2 alpha_i z_i=0`.
Hence the annihilator has dimension `4-s`, proving (16).  Equality five
occurs exactly at `s=3`.  Coordinate scaling and permutation give the one
normal form

```text
U=V=ker(x_0+x_1+x_2).                                      (17)
```

This orbit is not equivalent to `(4,1)`, despite the fact that both unique
annihilator graphs are `K_(1,3)`: in (17), `dim(U intersect V)=3`, while
the four sign-split cases have intersection dimension two.

Corollary 4 and Lemma 5 prove the complete unbased classification:

```text
coincident support-three hyperplane,                         (P3)
distinct sign splits (2,1), (3,1), (4,1), (4,2).            (18)
```

## 3. The two nonadmissible orbits

We now apply Lemma 2.

### Proposition 6 (the coincident orbit is not Delta-admissible)

For (17), use bases of the common hyperplane beginning with
`-x_0+x_1,-x_0+x_2,x_3`.  The multiplication-dual space `L` is exactly

```text
{ [ a b c ]
  [ b d e ] : a,b,c,d,e in K }.                             (19)
  [ c e 0 ]
```

A nonzero symmetric rank-one matrix is a scalar multiple of `ww^T`.
Membership in (19) forces the third coordinate of `w` to vanish.  Thus all
left and right factors of rank-one members of `L` lie in a fixed two-space;
three of them cannot form bases.  Lemma 2 excludes the orbit.

### Proposition 7 (the `(2,1)` orbit is not Delta-admissible)

Use the canonical bases

```text
U=span{-x_0+x_1,x_2,x_3},
V=span{ x_0+x_1,x_2,x_3}.                                  (20)
```

The five nonzero product directions give

```text
L={ [ 0 a b ]
    [ c 0 e ] : a,b,c,d,e in K }.                           (21)
    [ d e 0 ]
```

The nonzero rank-one locus of (21) is exactly

```text
{ e_0 tensor eta : eta in span(e_1,e_2) }
union
{ xi tensor e_0 : xi in span(e_1,e_2) }.                    (22)
```

Indeed, rank at most one first forces `e=0`, because the lower-right
`2 x 2` minor is `-e^2`.  The four minors using row zero and column zero
then give

```text
ac=ad=bc=bd=0.
```

Over a field, either `(a,b)=(0,0)` or `(c,d)=(0,0)`.  These are precisely
the second and first families in (22), respectively.

To span the left three-space, a triple from (22) needs one member of the
first family and two members of the second.  Its right factors then span at
most the plane generated by `e_0` and the one remaining `eta`.  The
opposite choice has the symmetric failure.  Lemma 2 again excludes a
Delta-admissible equality frame.

## 4. Exact admissible frames in the other three orbits

Use edge order

```text
(01,02,03,12,13,23).                                        (23)
```

The following displayed frames all satisfy

```text
dim span{p_ij:i!=j}=2,
dim span{p_ij:all i,j}=5,
B=M direct-sum span{p_00,p_11,p_22}.                         (24)
```

Thus they prove existence in each remaining orbit without an algebraic
closure or generic choice.

### Type `(3,1)`

Take

```text
u_0=x_1-x_2,       u_1=x_3,          u_2=-x_0+x_2,
v_0=-x_1+x_2,      v_1=x_0+x_1,      v_2=x_3.                (25)
```

These span the hyperplanes with normals `(1,1,1,0)` and
`(1,-1,-1,0)`.  Put

```text
m_1=(1,-1,0,-1,0,0),       m_2=(0,0,0,0,1,-1).              (26)
```

The mixed products are

```text
p_01=m_1,   p_02=m_2,   p_10=-m_2,
p_12=0,     p_20=m_1,   p_21=-m_1,                           (27)
```

and the diagonals are

```text
p_00=(0,0,0,2,0,0),
p_11=(0,0,1,0,1,0),
p_22=(0,0,-1,0,0,1).                                       (28)
```

The unique ordinary annihilator is `(1,1,0,0,0,0)`, the graph
`K_(1,2)` plus one isolated vertex.

### Type `(4,1)`: an exact separator from the fixed pair

Take

```text
u_0=-x_0+x_2,      u_1=x_0-x_3,       u_2=x_1-x_2,
v_0=x_0+x_1-x_2+x_3,
v_1=x_0+x_1,       v_2=-x_1+x_2.                            (29)
```

The two hyperplane normals are `(1,1,1,1)` and `(1,-1,-1,-1)`.
The full product table is

```text
p_00=(-1, 2,-1, 1, 0, 1),
p_01=(-1, 1, 0, 1, 0, 0),
p_02=( 1,-1, 0,-1, 0, 0),

p_10=( 1,-1, 0, 0,-1, 1),
p_11=( 1, 0,-1, 0,-1, 0),
p_12=(-1, 1, 0, 0, 1,-1),

p_20=( 1,-1, 0,-2, 1,-1),
p_21=( 1,-1, 0,-1, 0, 0),
p_22=( 0, 0, 0, 2, 0, 0).                                 (30)
```

With

```text
m_1=p_01=(-1,1,0,1,0,0),
m_2=p_10=( 1,-1,0,0,-1,1),                                 (31)
```

the other mixed products are

```text
p_02=-m_1, p_12=-m_2, p_20=-2m_1-m_2, p_21=-m_1.           (32)
```

The unique ordinary annihilator is

```text
(1,1,1,0,0,0),                                             (33)
```

whose support graph is the star `K_(1,3)`.  The fixed pair's annihilator
graph is `K_(2,2)`.  Coordinate scaling preserves the zero pattern and a
coordinate permutation preserves graph isomorphism, so (29) is exactly
inequivalent to the fixed model even before colour bases are considered.

### Type `(4,2)`: the fixed model

Take

```text
u_0=x_0-x_3,       u_1=x_1-x_3,       u_2=x_2-x_3,
v_0=x_1+x_2,       v_1=x_0+x_2,       v_2=x_2-x_3.          (34)
```

Its normals are `(1,1,1,1)` and `(1,1,-1,-1)`.  Its mixed plane is

```text
m_1=(0,1,-1,0,0,-1),       m_2=(0,0,0,1,-1,-1),            (35)
```

with

```text
p_01=p_02=p_21=m_1,
p_10=p_12=p_20=m_2.                                         (36)
```

The diagonals are

```text
p_00=(1,1,0,0,-1,-1),
p_11=(1,0,-1,1,0,-1),
p_22=(0,0,0,0,0,-2),                                       (37)
```

and the annihilator is `(0,1,1,1,1,0)`, supported on `K_(2,2)`.

Propositions 6--7 and the three frames above prove the admissibility
classification:

```text
coincident support-three orbit:   NOT Delta-admissible;
distinct (2,1) orbit:             NOT Delta-admissible;
distinct (3,1) orbit:             Delta-admissible;
distinct (4,1) orbit:             Delta-admissible;
distinct (4,2) orbit:             Delta-admissible.          (38)
```

This classifies which unbased equality-five orbits admit at least one
Delta-admissible colour frame.  It does not classify the possibly finer
orbits of all admissible colour frames under the stabilizer of `Delta_3`.

## 5. Transport boundary in `r=6`

Zero-extend any frame above by setting its `x_4,x_5` coefficients to zero.
It remains an equality-five frame in `Z_6`.  Its active coordinate support
has size four, an invariant of coordinate permutations and scalings.  On
that active support, the annihilator graph from (14) is also invariant.
Therefore the `(3,1)`, `(4,1)`, and `(4,2)` examples remain mutually
inequivalent in `r=6`.

The orbit separation is exact and already occurs at the pair level.  It is
