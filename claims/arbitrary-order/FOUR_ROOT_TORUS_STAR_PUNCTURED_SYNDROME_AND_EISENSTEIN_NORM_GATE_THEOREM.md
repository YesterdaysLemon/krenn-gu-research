## 1. Pair erasure and the punctured code

Let

```text
E_0=E_1=E_2=E_3=C^3,             V=E_0 tensor E_1 tensor E_2 tensor E_3,
```

and use the local bases in the `h=1` representative of the `GLD70` torus
star.  Write `N_star subset V` for its complete nuisance space.  It has the
exact `1+24+54=79` raw presentation and dimension `44`.

The `54` pair-layer columns span the coordinate subspace

```text
P = E_0 tensor <e_i tensor e_j tensor e_k:
                  at least two of i,j,k equal 2>.                 (1)
```

There are seven displayed leaf words, so

```text
dim P=3*7=21.                                                (2)
```

Let `pi:V->V_bar` erase those `21` coordinates.  Thus `V_bar` retains the
four-tuples whose three leaf indices contain at most one `2`, and

```text
dim V_bar=3*(2^3+3*2^2)=60.                                 (3)
```

### Theorem 1.1 (exact punctured dimensions)

The kernel of `pi|N_star` is exactly `P`, and

```text
dim N_bar=dim pi(N_star)=23,
dim H=dim N_bar^perp=37.                                    (4)
```

#### Proof

Every pair-layer column is supported on (1), and the pair columns have rank
`21` on those `21` coordinates.  The `Q` and residual layers have rank `23`
after puncturing.  The complete raw map has rank `44`.  Therefore the
punctured kernel has dimension `44-23=21` and is exactly `P`.  The final
dimension in (4) follows from (3).  All ranks are replayed over `Q` by both
the primary verifier and the independent audit.  `square`

This quotient is not merely a parameter reduction.  It turns membership in
the fixed nuisance space into parity checks on only `60` coordinates.

## 2. The determinant-safe syndrome problem

Fix a basis `h_1,...,h_37` of `H`.  For a decomposable leaf word

```text
f=b tensor c tensor d in E_1 tensor E_2 tensor E_3,
```

define its syndrome map

```text
Sigma_f:E_0 -> C^37,
Sigma_f(a)=(h_r(pi(a tensor f)))_(r=1)^37.                 (5)
```

For three leaf words `f_0,f_1,f_2`, concatenate these blocks:

```text
M(B,C,D)=[Sigma_(f_0) Sigma_(f_1) Sigma_(f_2)] in Mat_(37 x 9), (6)
```

where the columns of `B,C,D` are the three local vectors occurring in the
corresponding words.  If `A` is the centre frame, in root-major order, then

```text
sum_(s=0)^2 a_s tensor b_s tensor c_s tensor d_s in N_star
       iff M(B,C,D) vec(A)=0.                              (7)
```

At the time of `GLD71`, the unresolved fixed-star exclusion was represented by
the following exact determinant-safety statement:

```text
det B det C det D !=0 and M(B,C,D) vec(A)=0
       imply det A=0.                                      (8)
```

In ideal language, if `I_syn` is generated by the `37` entries in (7), the
desired calculation is

```text
1 in I_syn : (det A det B det C det D)^infinity.           (9)
```

`GLD72` subsequently provides an exact point on the localized variety, so
(8)--(9) are now refuted rather than open.

Equations (8)--(9) are reformulations, not conclusions of this theorem.

## 3. Complete one-word syndrome theorem

### Lemma 3.1 (pair-erasure locus)

For nonzero `b,c,d`, the punctured leaf word `pi(b tensor c tensor d)` is zero
if and only if at least two of `b,c,d` are multiples of `e_2`.

#### Proof

The reverse implication is immediate from the definition of the erased
coordinates.  Conversely, if at most one factor is a multiple of `e_2`, at
least two factors have a nonzero coordinate indexed by `0` or `1`.  Choose
such coordinates in those factors and any nonzero coordinate in the third.
The resulting nonzero product has at most one leaf index equal to `2`, so it
survives puncturing.  `square`

### Theorem 3.2 (one-word dichotomy)

Over every characteristic-zero field extension of `Q`,

```text
rank Sigma_(b tensor c tensor d) =
  0,  if at least two of b,c,d are multiples of e_2;
  3,  otherwise.                                           (10)
```

In particular, outside the known pair layer, `N_star` contains no nonzero
decomposable four-factor tensor.

#### Proof

The rank-zero direction follows from Lemma 3.1.  For the other direction,
suppose a nonzero `a` lies in the kernel of (5).  A non-erased leaf word has
at least two leaf factors with a nonzero binary projection to
`<e_0,e_1>`.  The three leaf factors are symmetric in `N_star`, so call two
such factors `b,c`.  Choose projective pivots

```text
b_i=1, c_j=1 with i,j in {0,1};
d_k=1, a_l=1 with k,l in {0,1,2}.                         (11)
```

This gives exactly `2*2*3*3=36` affine charts.  On each chart, substitute
(11) into `36` pinned sparse generators of the syndrome ideal.  Exact
Groebner reduction over `Q` returns the unit ideal on every chart.  Hence no
projective quadruple outside the erasure locus has zero syndrome, and every
non-erased map (5) is injective.  Its domain has dimension three, proving
(10).  `square`

The primary verifier reconstructs the `37`-dimensional annihilator, verifies
that the pinned sparse rows span and annihilate the independently rebuilt
fixed nuisance space, verifies leaf-permutation invariance, and executes the
`36` exact SymPy charts.  A separate exact Singular 4.3.2 replay uses all
three choices of the two binary-visible leaves, hence `108` charts, and
reports `108/108` unit ideals.  The no-import audit independently reconstructs
the code and exhausts all `31^3=29791` projective leaf triples over `F_5`; it
finds exactly `91` pair-erased rank-zero words and `29700` rank-three words.
The finite census corroborates the formulas but is not substituted for the
characteristic-zero calculation.

Theorem 3.2 closes the first generalized syndrome weight.  It says nothing
by itself about dependencies among two or three non-erased words.

## 4. Root-slice gates

Restrict the punctured code to each fixed centre coordinate.  Its parity
dimensions for centre indices `0,1,2` are respectively

```text
4, 4, 6.                                                    (12)
```

The third root slice admits the following particularly sparse six checks.
On the dense leaf chart

```text
B=[1; x; p],       C=[1; y; q],       D=[1; z; r],          (13)
```

where each displayed row has three component entries, the six syndrome rows
are, component by component,

```text
r(y-x),       r(1-xy),
q(z-x),       q(1-xz),
p(z-y),       p(1-yz).                                     (14)
```

Each pair in (14) is the alternating part of a binary `2 x 2` cross-moment
matrix, followed by its anti-diagonal companion.  These rows provide a small
necessary gate for (8), but the remaining parity checks couple centre slices.
They may not be discarded.  In particular, a later ten-row experimental
compression that retained too few cross-slice checks produced an apparent
invertible-centre point; direct replay against all `37` rows rejected it.

## 5. Binary Eisenstein-norm gate

On the binary coordinate cube `{0,1}^4`, the pair layer vanishes.  Write the
free residual coefficients at mode `u` as `(x_(u0),x_(u1))` and
`(y_(u0),y_(u1))`, and put

```text
alpha_u=x_(u0)-y_(u0),
beta_u =x_(u1)-y_(u1),
s_u=alpha_u^2-alpha_u beta_u+beta_u^2.                    (15)
```

### Theorem 5.1 (three balanced binary determinants)

The three `4 x 4` binary balanced-flattening determinants of an arbitrary
`Q+residual` tensor in `N_star` are

```text
16(s_0-s_1)(s_2-s_3),
16(s_0-s_2)(s_1-s_3),
16(s_0-s_3)(s_1-s_2).                                     (16)
```

#### Proof

Insert the fixed-star port vectors into the permanent definitions of the `Q`
and residual columns, restrict to the binary cube, and expand each balanced
determinant.  The `Q` coefficient and the residual sums cancel.  Collecting
the remaining differences gives (15)--(16).  The primary verifier performs
this expansion symbolically over `Q`; the independent audit reconstructs the
permanents by subset dynamic programming and checks the resulting identities
at independent exact rational assignments.  `square`

Every third-secant tensor has all three determinants zero.  Since the ground
ring is a domain, (16) then forces at least three of

```text
s_0,s_1,s_2,s_3                                               (17)
```

to be equal.  Over a field containing roots of `t^2-t+1`,

```text
s(alpha,beta)=(alpha-zeta beta)(alpha-zeta^(-1) beta),
zeta+zeta^(-1)=1.                                          (18)
```

Thus (17) is an `A_2` or Eisenstein-norm synchronization gate with a natural
torus/isotropic boundary.  It is only a necessary binary-subcube condition.
The variables in (15) are nuisance coefficients, not the leaf-frame
coordinates in (5); no unproved identification between those two parameter
spaces is made.

## 6. Exact nonhidden two-word boundary control

The tempting strengthening

```text
two non-erased leaf words always give six independent syndrome columns
```

is false in characteristic zero.  Let

```text
G=B=C=D = [ 1  1  1 ]
            [-1  0  0 ]
            [ 0 -1  0 ],                                  (19)

A = [0 1 -1]
    [0 0  0]
    [0 0  1].                                               (20)
```

Then `det G=1`, while `rank A=2` and `det A=0`.  The full syndrome matrix in
(6) has rank `8` and kernel generated, in root-major order, by

```text
(0,1,-1, 0,0,0, 0,0,1).                                   (21)
```

Only the last two decomposition terms are used, and neither leaf word is
pair-erased.  Their six-column syndrome block has rank `5`.  The resulting
tensor lies in `N_star` exactly; it has

```text
epsilon=0,                 balanced ranks=(2,2,2).          (22)
```

Thus the fixed nuisance space genuinely meets the nonhidden second secant.
This does not threaten (8), because the centre frame in (20) is singular.
At publication this motivated determinant-safe reasoning rather than absence.
The exact three-word survivor in `GLD72` shows that the stronger inference is
also false; the two-word counterexample and every assertion proved above are
unchanged.
