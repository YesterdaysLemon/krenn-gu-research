## Universal mixed-star normal form

Let the center pure basis be `(u,v)`, with `u` the kernel row and `v` the
active row.  In the mixed orientation the rank-one spokes are

```text
u*x_2=0,              v*y_3=0.                     (1)
```

Every nonzero degree-one zero divisor in the squarefree algebra has support
one or two.  Its annihilator line has the same support: a singleton is its
own annihilator, while `(aX_i+bX_j)` is annihilated by
`(aX_i-bX_j)`.  Write these polar partners as `u^perp,v^perp`.

The rank-two synchronizer relation is

```text
u*x_1-v*y_1=0.                                     (2)
```

The complete degree-one syzygy space of (2) is generated by

```text
(x_1,y_1)=(v,u), (u^perp,0), (0,v^perp).           (3)
```

If the coefficient of `(v,u)` vanishes, the pair `U_0U_1` has at least two
relations and rank at most two.  Otherwise scale it to one and write

```text
U_0=<u,v>,
U_1=<u+k v^perp,v+s u^perp>,
U_2=<y_2,u^perp>,
U_3=<v^perp,x_3>.                                  (4)
```

Thus the support problem is finite before any purity calculation.

## Exhaustive support ledger

Because `u,v` are independent, their ordered support sizes and intersection
give exactly eight signatures:

```text
(1,1,0),
(1,2,0), (1,2,1),
(2,1,0), (2,1,1),
(2,2,0), (2,2,1), (2,2,2).                        (5)
```

The missing `(1,1,1)` signature has proportional center rows and is not a
plane.

### Equal and contained supports

For `(2,2,2)`, `(1,2,1)`, `(2,1,1)`, and `(1,1,0)`, the center and
synchronizer planes in (4) lie in the same coordinate two-plane.  Their
degree-two product has dimension at most two (at most one when both planes
fill that coordinate two-plane).  These signatures are lower-pair.

### Overlapping binary supports

Normalize

```text
u=(1,1,0,0),       u^perp=(1,-1,0,0),
v=(0,1,1,0),       v^perp=(0,1,-1,0).              (6)
```

Write `y_2=(a_0,a_1,a_2,a_3)` and
`x_3=(g_0,g_1,g_2,g_3)`.  The only potentially nonzero coefficients are

```text
T_0000=-2a_3(k+1),
T_0001=-a_0g_3k-a_1g_3k+a_2g_3k+2a_2g_3
        -a_3g_0k-a_3g_1k+a_3g_2k+2a_3g_2,
T_0101=T_1001=g_3(a_0+a_1+a_2)+a_3(g_0+g_1+g_2),
T_1101=(2-s)(a_0g_3+a_3g_0)
        +s(a_1g_3+a_2g_3+a_3g_1+a_3g_2),
T_1111=-2g_3(s-1).                                 (7)
```

Nonzero purity gives `g_3(s-1)!=0`.  If `k!=-1`, then `a_3=0`, and the
remaining three homogeneous equations in `(a_0,a_1,a_2)` have determinant

```text
4(k+1)(s-1).                                       (8)
```

Hence `y_2=0`, impossible.  If `k=-1`, the `U_0U_1` product matrix has rank
two, with independent relations `(-1,1,0,0)` and `(-1,0,1,0)`.  The whole
overlapping signature is therefore empty on the all-pair locus.

### Disjoint binary and singleton supports

First let the center kernel be binary and the center active row singleton:

```text
u=(1,1,0,0),       v=(0,0,1,0).                    (9)
```

Nonzero purity forces `g_3s!=0`.  Its equations successively give

```text
a_3=0,       a_0+a_1=0,       a_0-a_1=0,       a_2=0,
```

so `y_2=0`, impossible.

For the reverse role order `u=(0,0,1,0)`, `v=(1,1,0,0)`, nonzero purity
forces `g_3!=0`.  If `k!=0`, the same four equations again give `y_2=0`.
If `k=0`, then `U_1=U_0` and `dim(U_0U_1)<=2`.  Both ordered signatures are
therefore empty on the all-pair locus.

### Disjoint binary supports

The final signature `(2,2,0)` is exactly the split-center chart

```text
u=A,       v=B,       u^perp=C,       v^perp=D.    (10)
```

Its complete purity split and projective closure are the theorem in
[`P4_SPLIT_CENTER_MIXED_STAR_211_COMPONENT.md`](P4_SPLIT_CENTER_MIXED_STAR_211_COMPONENT.md):

mixed-orientation classification.
