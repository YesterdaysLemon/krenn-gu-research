## Family

Work in the squarefree algebra

```text
R=C[X0,X1,X2,X3]/(X0^2,X1^2,X2^2,X3^2)
```

and put `e=X0`, `V=span(X1,X2,X3)`.  For
`ell,v1,v2,v3 in V`, define

```text
U0=span(e,ell),
Ui=span(e,vi),  i=1,2,3.
```

Let `P3` be the squarefree ternary permanent and let

```text
B_ell(u,v)=P3(ell,u,v).
```

If the three `vi` are pairwise `B_ell`-orthogonal, then every mixed
coefficient of the four-plane restriction vanishes: a mixed word either
contains `e^2=0`, or has the form `P3(ell,vi,vj)` with `i!=j`.  Its only
possibly nonzero coefficient is

```text
P3(v1,v2,v3)
```

at the word `(0,1,1,1)` in the row convention used by the verifier.

A rational chart has parameters `(L,M,a,b,c)`:

```text
ell=(1,L,M),       v1=(1,a,b),       v2=(1,c,d),

B=[[0,M,L],[M,0,1],[L,1,0]],

d=-(L*b+M*a+M*c+b*c)/(L+a),
v3=((B*v1) cross (B*v2))/(((B*v1) cross (B*v2))_0).
```

The displayed denominators and `P3(v1,v2,v3)` are nonzero on a dense open
set.  The image closure is irreducible.  At

```text
(L,M,a,b,c)=(-3,-2,-1,-1,-1)
```

one has

```text
d=2,   v3=(1,3,-1),   P3(v1,v2,v3)=4.
```

In the `pivot01` Grassmann chart this is the integral point

```text
(0,0,-3,-2, 0,0,-1,-1, 0,0,-1,2, 0,0,3,-1).
```

The differential of the five-parameter family has rank five there; the
verifier exhibits a `5 x 5` minor equal to `1`.  Hence the family closure has
dimension five.

## Why one prime proves the characteristic-zero local statement

Let `p=32003`, and let

```text
S=Z_(p)[y1,...,y15]_(p,y1,...,y15),
n=(p,y1,...,y15),
q=(y1,...,y15).
```

Let `J` be the ideal generated by the fifteen integer slice equations.
The equations vanish at the origin, so `J` is contained in `q`.  The local
standard-basis result says that `S/(J,p)` has dimension zero, hence

```text
rad(J+(p))=n,    height(J+(p))=16.
```

Choose a minimal prime `P` of `J` contained in `q`.  Since `J` has fifteen
generators, the generalized principal ideal theorem gives `height(P)<=15`.
In particular `P` cannot contain `p`, because the only prime over `J+(p)` is
the height-sixteen maximal ideal `n`.  The unique minimal prime over
`P+(p)` is therefore `n`; the principal ideal theorem now gives

```text
16=height(n) <= height(P)+1.
```

Thus `height(P)=15`.  But `P` is contained in `q`, and `q` itself has height
fifteen, so `P=q`.  Consequently `q/J` is a minimal prime of `S/J`.
Localizing at `q` inverts `p` and gives

```text
Q[y1,...,y15]_(y1,...,y15)/J_Q,
```

whose unique localized prime is minimal.  The characteristic-zero graph
slice therefore has local dimension zero at the rational origin.  This is a
local height argument through the integral model; it assumes neither
flatness nor that `32003` is a generically good prime.

Adding five equations can lower local dimension by at most five.  Thus the
zero-dimensional characteristic-zero slice implies that the original
twenty-variable incidence has local dimension at most five.  Its fifteen
defining equations give the reverse bound, and the explicit family also has
dimension five.  Hence the incidence has local dimension exactly five at the
sample.  The irreducible five-dimensional family closure is consequently an
irreducible component.

## Separation from the seventeen known components

For every pair `i<j` in this family,

```text
Im(Ui*Uj) = span(e*vi,e*vj,vi*vj),
```

with the evident replacement of one `v` by `ell` when `i=0`.  Thus all six
pair-product ranks are at most three on the whole family closure.  At the
sample all are exactly three and every unique pair relation is the rank-one
matrix represented by `e tensor e`.

The condition that all six pair-product ranks are at most three is closed.
Each of the seventeen old irreducible component orbits has a certified point
